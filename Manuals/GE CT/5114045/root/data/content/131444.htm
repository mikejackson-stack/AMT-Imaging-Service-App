4  Procedure

4.1  Purpose (Why/When to Clean)

  1. Communication errors, including Taxi Link Errors.

  1. PM.

4.2  General Information about Slip Ring Vacuuming

  1. When done properly and on a regular PM schedule, slip ring vacuuming is sufficient to avoid premature power ring and brush failures, and / or taxi-link failures due to signal brush, and ring problems. It will also prevent any need for ring repair, or craytex sanding.

  1. Not removing ALL of the brush dust will necessitate an alcohol cleaning or worse, will cause arcing on the power rings. If the contaminated alcohol gets on the brushes, it will significantly increase brush wear. Alcohol cleaning is a service action NOT a PM action.

  1. Arcing on the power rings will most times cause pitting and will necessitate sanding with the Craytex sanding crayon. Sanding the rings will remove ring material and significantly reduce ring life and if the residue from the sanding is not completely removed, will also significantly increase brush wear. Sanding with a Craytex sanding crayon is a service repair action NOT a PM action.

4.3  Initial Procedure

ELECTROCUTION HAZARD.
HIGH VOLTAGE PRESENT.
use proper lockout/tagout procedures before servicing equipment.

  1. Tag and Lock System power at the A1 Panel.

Read instructions in PM procedure CTPM-1319 (Removing Slip Ring Brush Debris) before proceeding.
 
 

  1. Visually inspect all the 14 slip ring tracks. Tracks should be smooth. Look for pits or deposits, which are sometimes hard to see. Deposits can be silver or black from arching. If there is a suspect area on the ring, take the end of a CLEAN tie wrap and gently drag it across the suspect area. You should be able to feel a vibration in the tie wrap if a pit or deposit is present. If pits or deposits are found, refer to PM procedure CTPM-1306 (Clean Slip Ring Tracks).

4.4  VACUUMING

  1. Vacuuming the Signal brushes.

    1. Remove both the brush blocks.

    1. Tap lightly with a small hammer to dislodge the dust particles.

    1. Vacuum thoroughly.

    1. Do not replace the removed components until the rest of the ring is vacuumed.

  1. Vacuuming the Power brushes (Same as Signal brush vacuuming).

    1. Remove both the brush blocks.

    1. Tap lightly with a small hammer to dislodge the dust particles.

    1. Vacuum thoroughly.

    1. Do not replace the removed components until the rest of the ring is vacuumed.

  1. Check both top and bottom tips for significant wear.

    1. The brush tips will wear at slightly different rates and may appear out of alignment, this is expected. DO NOT touch the tips with your finger or any tool.

    1. If any tip appears to be less than 4mm (0.20 in.), refer to PM procedure CTPM-1303 (Remove, Measure Power Brush).

  1. Vacuum the Stationary Terminator and Buffer boards. The rotating Terminator and Buffer boards do not need vacuuming.

    1. Vacuum both sides of the boards, using a soft artists brush to dislodge the dust particles.

    1. Do not replace the removed components until the rest of the ring is vacuumed or alcohol cleaned.

  1. Vacuum the inside of the Slip Ring Cover.

  1. Vacuum the Gantry Rotating base.

  1. Vacuum the Slip Ring itself. DO NOT TOUCH THE RING.

  1. Replace all of the removed components.

    NOTE:Reassembling the component is critical to brush and ring performance. Please refer to the proper replacement procedures in the General Service Manual 2152918-100.

  1. Install the slip ring covers.
