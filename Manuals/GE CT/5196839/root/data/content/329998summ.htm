3  Procedure

3.1   Fill the compensation tool with tube oil.

  1. Push the tool's piston until it stops to force out the air inside.

  1. Attach the compensation tool's hose onto the valve spout.

  1. Insert the open end of the hose into the oil can.

  1. Open the valve fully

  1. Slowly pull the piston back as far as you can to fill the cylinder with oil

    (Step Details)

NOTE:Small amounts of oil may leak from the small hole at the end of the tool, near the handle.

  1. Tip the compensation tool so that the piston handle is up, forcing any air to rise to the top of the cylinder

    (Step Details)

  1. If there is air present in the cylinder, you will need to bleed the air from the cylinder.

    1. Lay the tool flat. The tool is designed such that the valve area by the sightglass is the highest point when flat, thus the air will rise to the valve area.

    1. Next, push the piston all the way in again, forcing out all air and liquid, then slowly pull back. Usually this will remove all of the air, however, a few iterations may be necessary.

    (Step Details)

  1. With the cylinder full and air free, close the valve tightly.

  1. Prepare for the next step by gathering a dry absorbent wipe.

  1. Hold the tool above the oil can and slowly pull the feeder tube off of the tool, draining the oil into the can.

    (Step Details)

3.2   Connect the Compensation Tool and Perform Oil Compensation

  1. Remove the gantry side and front covers.

  1. Rotate the gantry so that the tube is between the 3 o'clock and 4 o'clock positions

  1. Safely remove power to the gantry

  1. Engage Gantry Rotational Lock. This forces the gantry to be stationary while moving the compensation tool's piston.

Tool can be used on oil-based systems only. Use of this tool on water-based cooled systems (blue hoses) will cause unknown system damage.
 
 

  1. Connect the compensation tool between the pump and the X-Ray tube as shown. The handle should angled upward.

    (Step Details)

Unnecessary Exposure to Patient Possible
Large amounts of air in the heat exchanger can cause IQ artifacts or scan aborts
NEVER open the compensation tool's valve while it is connected to the gantry.

  1. Keeping the handle higher than the rest of the tool, push the handle / piston until it stops. This fills the heat exchanger and bellows with oil, while keeping any small traces of air at the top of the cylinder.

NOTE:It may be possible to push the piston such that it bottoms out, indicating that the bellows may not be completely full with oil. In this rare case, you'll need to disconnect the tool, fill it with oil per instructions, and push more oil in until the bellows is full.

  1. With the bellows now full of oil, slowly pull back on the handle of the tool so that 30 units of oil are removed. Use the bottom of the piston (where oil and the piston meet) as your line to measure with. The bellows may slowly push a small amount oil back into the cylinder; this is normal.

NOTE:In the rare case that you do not have room to pull 30 units of oil from the system, you can perform the oil removal in two steps.

  1. Disconnect the tool and reconnect the pump and heat exchanger hoses to the tube.

  1. Secure the quick disconnect safety mechanisms. The torque spec. for the quick disconnect safety mechanisms is 9.9 N-m (7.3 lb-ft, 88 lb-in, 101 kg-cm), however, if the locking mechanism rotates in relation to the quick disconnect, tighten slowly until it does not do so.

    (Step Details)
