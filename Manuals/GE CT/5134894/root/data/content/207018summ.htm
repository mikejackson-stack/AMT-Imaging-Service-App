3.2   Patient Touch Leakage Current Test Procedure

These instructions have been validated only with a Dale 600/601 meter. Due to the unique nature of this meter, GE Healthcare cannot guarantee the accuracy of these procedures when performed with any meter other than the Dale 600/601.
 
 

  1. Move the table to ISO elevation.

  1. Remove the Footswitch covers and the Gantry left side cover.

  1. Refer to the Dale 600 or Dale 601 Operator’s Manual for instructions on the use of the Dale 600/601 meter for measuring leakage current (or refer to Illustration 2 for a quick overview).

    (Step Details)

  1. Plug thndicators on the meter.

  1. Connect one end of the shorter black lead to the chassis plug and connect the other end to the table ground bus.

  1. Connect the longer red test lead (or the longer black lead) to the external plug on the top of the Dale 600 meter.

  1. Set the function switch on the Dale to external. Use the external lead to touch the meter’s test terminal, to test that the meter is operational.

  1. The black lead will be connected to the table base ground bus, and the read lead will be connected to the devices (components) under test.

    (Step Details)

  1. Beware: Leakage current cannot exceed the following and is tested with power ON:

    1. Critical care areas (invasive) - 10µa CT Systems

    1. General care areas - 20µa

    1. Not intended for patient area - 50µa

  1. Testing must be completed between the system reference ground point (Table Base) to unit reference ground points (i.e. Gantry and Table, see Illustration 3).

    (Step Details)

  1. Test all conductive surfaces and components within patient reach or within 6 feet (1.9m) of the table and 7 feet (2.1m) above.

    (Step Details)

  1. Test all Optional components such as in room monitors, injector, overhead monitor suspensions, and table options.

    (Step Details)

Be Aware of Static Discharge from: scan window, keypads, display, touch pads, or other plastic surfaces.
 
 

  1. Procedure Hints:

    1. Look for items that have abnormal measurements, high or low. These could indicate mis-wiring, loose connections, or poor connections due to corrosion, painted surfaces, etc.

    1. High leakage could indicate a wiring error such as a neutral connected to the ground.

    1. Fluctuating ground currents could indicate a short, poor connection, or facilities ground problem causing leakage currents from other areas of the facility to flow though our system grounds.

    (Step Details)

3.3   Ground Resistance Checks Procedure (done during mechanical install)

  1. Use a Fluke 87 or equivalent RMS Meter to verify the presence of less than 0.5 ohm of resistance between each of the following points:

    (Step Details)

  1. Connect the meter leads to the Fluke 87 meter.

  1. Set the function switch to resistance ohms.

  1. The black and red leads will be connected per table (data sheet) below.

  1. All ground resistance testing is done with power OFF. Ground current will cause errors in the resistance readings.

  1. Use lockout/tag out procedures.

  1. Maximum ground resistance is 0.5 ohms impedance.

  1. Using the table footswitch ground bar as a reference, check the ground resistance to all five (5) system grounds.

    (Step Details)
