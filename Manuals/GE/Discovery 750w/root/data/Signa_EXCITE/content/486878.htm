Oxygen Monitor Safety

NOTE:Since March 1, 1994, the oxygen monitor is no longer delivered with GE MR systems. Oxygen monitors are still available as an accessory from GE Healthcare through Diagnostics Imaging Accessories (DIA).

For a one-year sensor, the oxygen monitor sensor element should be replaced every six to twelve months. (Beginning in 1994, a new five-year oxygen sensor element became available.) The sensors are shipped in transparent airtight packages, and should be inspected upon receipt. The cells contain a strong caustic potassium (KOH) solution. If the cell membrane is punctured, do not open the package.

The alarm levels were changed in 2011 from 18.0% to 19.5% to comply with OSHA standards. If this is the first time you are making the change from 18.0% to 19.5%, a single-shot FSE scan with TG set to 200 should be performed to confirm the new setting does not cause the alarm to sound during a scan. If the alarm is triggered, try the following:

  1. Confirm that the oxygen monitor is reading 20.9% as shown on the display readout. If it is not, perform a recalibration.

  2. Verify that all grounds are present, especially on cable shielding in scan room.

  3. Relocate the oxygen sensor box further away from the RF and magnetic field. For best positioning, check the gauss map in the site planning documentation for the magnetic field.

  4. Figure-eight any excess cable and store away from magnet.

    NOTE:Perform the above steps before cutting any cabling in the next step!

  5. If the above steps don’t address the issue, cut the cabling as follows to find the length of cable that attenuates RF noise the best.

    1. Cut excess cable to within 4’ (1.22 m) extra.

    2. Perform a single-shot FSE scan with TG set to 200. If the alarm sounds, cut off an additional 5 to 6” (127 to 152 mm), reconnect, and rescan.

    3. Repeat step b until the alarm no longer sounds.

This information applies to all models of oxygen monitors. Call for support if problem persists.
