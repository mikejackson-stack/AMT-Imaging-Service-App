Gradient Troubleshooting

NOTICE
If any gradient cables are swapped between different axis or polarities for troubleshooting, perform DQA-II procedure to verify that the gradient cables are returned to their original position.
 
 

1  Running Gradient Diagnostics

NOTE:For the Gradient Block Diagram, select the Gradient option in the Interactive Block Diagrams document. For the Gradient Output Cable diagram, see Illustration 13.

Gradient Diagnostics offers three tests that should all be run: Gradient Hammer, Gradient Fidelity, and Gradient Static. To locate the tests on the Customer Service Desktop, navigate to:

    NOTE:Accepting all defaults means do not clear any Test Selection checkboxes or make any changes to the Test Length from Short (default) to Long.

3.4  Gradient Cable Swapping

Before swapping cables, perform LOTO for the PGR PDU/Gradient Subsystem

  • Be aware that swapping gradient data fibers will apply Eddy Current Compensation (ECC) to the wrong coil axis, which could potentially result in SPT Eddy Current failures or even Gradient Current distortion faults. It may be necessary to disable ECC in these cases.

    NOTE:ECC can be disabled by performing the following:

    1. Verify that you have good SaveInfo media.

    2. Go into Grafidy3 tool and select [Zero Out Grafidy values].

    3. Perform a TPS Reset.

    4. Perform the necessary steps to isolate possible failure.

    5. When completed, perform [Restore Info] and perform a TPS Reset.

  • Perform a TPS Reset after swapping any two gradient data fiber cables.

Illustration 10:  X, Y, and Z Gradient Data Flow

To help solve image problems, try using some of the following recommended hardware solutions:

  • Swap output cables from Cronus XGA control board (J11/J14, J12/J15, J13/J16).

  • Swap input cables at the XGA amplifiers J1/J2 between axis.

  • Swap amplifiers and power supplies (outers to inners, x to y, and y to z), all shown below.

    Illustration 11:  Sample of Y and Z Swap

  • Swap outputs at the Bus Bar or at top of PGR Cabinet shown below.

    Illustration 12:  Top of Gradient Cabinet

    The Illustration above depicts X, Y, and Z at the top of the Gradient Cabinet to swap positions if necessary. Swapping gradient output cables will apply ECC to the wrong coil axis, which could potentially result in SPT Eddy Current failures or even Gradient Current distortion faults. It may be necessary to disable ECC in these cases.

    NOTE:Do not swap output cabling at the XGA terminal lugs, nor swap LEMs between XGAs .

4  Known Gradient Issues

There are three identical sets of components to operate the X, Y, and Z functions within the Gradient Cabinet. These components can be swapped to aid in the troubleshooting process.

4.1  Over Slew-Rate Bit and Over Temp Errors

This type of error indicates that the over slew-rate bit (ovrSR) was set during the start of the scan.

Table 1:  Gradient Messages Error Log - Over Slew-rate Example

For This error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
12267827 - XGD hardware warning detected.The ovrSR bit in the DSP_FAULT register was detected on the XGD XGA-CB board X, Y, or Z-axis during start of a scan or diag.

In some of the scans gradient + ECC, compensation will exceed the slew-rate limit and DSP firmware will detect and report a warning. As long as the hardware is properly functioning, this will be logged to the system (messages above), but system scanning will not be inhibited or affected.

Run one or more passes of Gradient Fidelity Diagnostic to confirm proper operation of the gradient hardware. If this diagnostic passes, these can be classified as nuisance messages.

4.4  System Intermittently Stop Errors

The system intermittently stops and the only way to recover is by rebooting the system. This type of error is seen in the log when an interruption occurs.

Table 4:  Gradient Messages Error Log - System Intermittently Stop Examples

For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
12267943 - Z axis XGA is reporting a power off fault.Check EMO reset and perform a TPS reset. If this error occurs again, contact your service representative.
NOTE:Notice that all axes are reporting power loss; however, visual inspection of all units shows that they have power. Common factor that would result in all three reporting power loss is an unstable 48 V PS2 power supply operation. (The PS2 power supply is located inside the PDU.) Check for the presence of the green PS2 LED (two LEDs: upper is PS1 and lower is PS2) by viewing through the vent holes located above the Magnet Power Supply connection on the front faceplate of the PDU. A dark LED means that the PS2 unit must be replaced. See the PDU vendor manual replacement section for more details.

22267944 - Z axis XPS is reporting a power off fault.
32267945 - Y axis XGA is reporting a power off fault.
42267946 - Y axis XPS is reporting a power off fault.
52267947 - X axis XGA is reporting a power off fault.
62267948 - X axis XPS is reporting a power off fault.
72267857 1 - A gradient distortion fault occurred on the X axis.Error indicates that small signal input to XGA does not match large signal output by XGA.

Look for other errors prior to this one that occur at about the same time, which might explain why this error occurred. There are many reasons this error would appear alone in the error log. In order of most to least probable, reasons include:

  • Faulty XGA or XPS fiber-optic transceiver module.
  • Faulty XGA (likely) or XPS (much less likely).
  • Poor gradient coil circuit connections.
  • Poor calibration - Usually only seen during installation or troubleshooting (when XGAs are swapped).
  • Faulty XGA or XPS fiber-optic transceiver module (SFP).Refer to the information in Section 4.3 or Illustration 15 for the process to troubleshoot a defective SFP module.If faulty XGA, try the following:
  • Swap fiber-optic cables from failing XGA module to a nonfailing XGA.
  • Repeat failing sequence (scan or gradient diagnostic) and see if problem moves.If problem moves - Try swapping suspected bad XGA to another axis and see if problem follows the XGA. If the problem moves with XGA, replace it.If problem does not move - XGA seems OK, but calibration or circuit connections may be suspect. Proceed to the next troubleshooting step.
  • Faulty XPS - Swap both black power cable lines between XPS on failing axis and XPS on another axis. See Gradient Troubleshooting service methods document if details are required.
  • Repeat failing sequence (scan or gradient diagnostic) and see if problem moves.If problem moves - Replace XPS.If problem does not move - Proceed to the next troubleshooting steps as XPS appears OK.
  • Poor gradient coil connections - Inspect all circuit connections starting at XGA terminal lugs, and check every connection in the path out to the Bus Bar connections at the rear of the XRMB.
  • Poor calibration - Perform a SaveInfo, then perform Grafidy.

4.5  System Will Not Scan - Initial Error Messages

Table 5:  Gradient Messages Error Log - Initial Message Examples

For This error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
12267833 - XGD reports no 420 V power.
  1. Check Gradient 420 V power breaker and EMO reset button.
  2. Check condition of 420 V PDU power circuit breaker and confirm system is not in E-Stop.
  3. Check the XPS Fault LED on each of the XPS units. Unit with illuminated XPS Fault LED has failed and requires replacement. This should normally be a single unit.
    NOTE:If none of the XPS Fault LEDs are illuminated and XPS units have power, recheck the error log for earlier messages that will help identify the real problem. If necessary, call the On-Line Center for assistance.

22267962 - X axis XGA-SB is reporting a PLL failure.
  1. Check the integrity of the connections at the XGA Control Board and at each XGA unit.
    NOTE:Observe the following:
    • A PLL is a Warning; it will not stop the scan. Most of the time, this error will show up as a collapsed image, which is a dark line in the image. The direction will depend on the axis that failed and type of scan being completed.
    • The PLL is derived from the signal on the single strand clock fiber from the XGA Control Board inside the GMC (Cronus) Chassis to each XGA. A loose or damaged clock fiber or fiber-optic connection at the XGA Control Board inside the GMC (Cronus) Chassis or at the XGA unit could cause this fault.

  2. Swap failing clock fiber-optic cable at the XGA Control Board (inside the GMC Chassis) with neighboring fiber-optic cable.
    NOTE:The clock signals on each clock line from the XGA Control Board to each XGA unit are identical.

  3. Perform action that causes fault, then check error log.
    NOTE:If problem moves, the XGA Control Board is faulty. Replace the XGA Control Board. If problem does not move, swap clock fiber-optic cable at the failing XGA with neighboring XGA unit. (This unit should be on same line as what was swapped at XGA Control Board.)

  4. Swap failing SFP fiber-optic cable with neighboring cable.
  5. Perform action that results in fault and check error log.
    NOTE:If problem moves, the fiber-optic line is faulty. Replace or repair it. If problem does not move, the XGA is faulty. Replace the XGA unit.

32267885 - Y axis XPS is reporting a PLL Warning
  1. Check the integrity of connections at the XPS Control Board and at each XPS unit.
    NOTE:The PLL is derived from the signal on the single strand clock fiber from the XPS Control Board inside the GMC (Cronus) Chassis to each XPS. A loose or damaged clock fiber or fiber-optic connection at the XGA Control Board inside the GMC (Cronus) Chassis or at the XPS unit could cause this fault.

  2. Swap the failing clock fiber-optic cable at the XPS Control Board (inside the GMC Chassis) with neighboring fiber cable.
    NOTE:The clock signals on each clock line from the XPS Control Board to each XPS unit are identical.

  3. Perform action that causes fault, then check error log.
    NOTE:If problem moves, the XPS Control Board is faulty; replace it. If problem does not move, swap the clock fiber-optic cable at the failing XPS with neighboring XPS unit. (This unit should be on same line as what was swapped at the XPS Control Board.)

  4. Swap failing sfp fiber optic cable with neighboring cable.
  5. Perform action that results in fault and check error log.
    NOTE:If problem moves, the fiber-optic line is faulty. Replace or repair it. If problem does not move, the XPS is faulty. Replace the XPS unit.

4.9  System Running Errors

This type of error is reported in the log, but does not inhibit scanning.

Table 9:  System Running - Temperature Error Example

For This error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
12267873 - Y axis XGA-SB is reporting a TEC out of temperature range fault. TEC temperature is 124C.

A Thermo-Electric Device (TEC) is present on the internal system board of the XGA to control the temperature stability of critical compensation circuits. The TEC can become dislodged or malfunction causing this error. The TEC can only compensate (by adding heat) within a certain temperature range of the XGA coldplate.

Confirm that the PE cooling loop of the Heat Exchange Cabinet is stable and operating in the specified range (30 to 35°C).

NOTE:Observe the following:
  • If the TEC is within range, the TEC is operating properly. If the TEC is out of range, the TEC is faulty. The TEC is not serviceable. Replace the XGA unit.
  • The XGA will operate without temperature stable TEC. A consequence is FMRI scans may exhibit ghosting artifacts.

4.10  Unable to Scan Errors

Extended error report recommends swapping the pair of black power cables between Z XPS and neighboring Y XGA and Z XGA and neighboring Y XPS. Early sites do not have black power cables long enough to swap. May need to physically swap the XPS modules and repeat experiment. FMI 60769X provides longer length power cables to these early sites.

Table 10:  Unable to Scan Error - High Voltage Example

For This error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
12267889 - Z axis XPS is reporting a high voltage I-Bus over current fault.
  1. Swap the pair of black power cables between Z XPS and neighboring Y XGA and Z XGA and neighboring Y XPS.
    NOTE:Early sites do not have black power cables long enough to swap. FMI 60769X will provide longer length power cables for these sites.

  2. Repeat the scan or run either the Gradient Static or Fidelity Diagnostic.
    NOTE:If the same Z axis XPS faults, the Z XPS is faulty. Replace the XPS. If the Y axis XPS faults, the Z XGA is faulty. Replace the XGA.

4.11  TPS Reset Failure

 For This error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
12267728 (first in error log) - Unexpected communication loss with XGD; perform TPS Reset and try again.
  • Check for power to the Cronus Chassis components (GMC Board, XGAControl Board, XPS Control Board).
  • If Cronus power is missing, check for presence of green PS1 LED by viewing through vent holes located above the Magnet Power Supply connection on the front faceplate of PDU.
  • Two green LEDs are visible, one above the other, which means PS1 (upper LED) and PS2 (lower LED) are both OK.
  • If Cronus power OK, LEDs OK, then review extended error message and perform recommended checks.

5  Finalization

NOTICE
If any gradient cables are swapped between different axis or polarities for troubleshooting, perform the DQA-II procedure to verify that the gradient cables are returned to their original position.
 
 

  1. Verify all system interconnects are returned to normal configurations.

  2. Run the DQA II Tool and verify that the image orientation is correct.
