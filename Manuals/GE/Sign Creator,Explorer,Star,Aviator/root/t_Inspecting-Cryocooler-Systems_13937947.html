About this task

This document covers inspection procedures for the following systems:

  • Sumitomo HC-10 (see Inspection of Sumitomo HC-10 Kelvin Systems (Shield Cooler))

  • Sumitomo CSW-71 (see Sumitomo CSW-71 Compressor 4 Kelvin System (Zero Boil-Off))

  • Sumitomo F-50 (see Sumitomo F-50 Compressor (Zero Boil-Off))

  • Leybold (see Leybold System (GE Magnets Only))

  • Balzers (see Balzers System (GE Magnets Only))

  • Sumitomo CSA-71A (see Sumitomo CSA-71A (Shield Cooler))

Note:

The site can have either a SHeM (Magnet Monitor 2) or Magnet Monitor 3.

Inspection of Sumitomo HC-10 Kelvin Systems (Shield Cooler)

Procedure

  1. Notice
    Adsorber must be replaced after 13,000 hours of compressor run time.
    Check the compressor hour meter on front of the compressor.
  2. Review the records to verify the adsorber was replaced on schedule.
    1. If the adsorber is not scheduled for replacement, go to Step 5, or
    2. If the adsorber should be replaced, order a replacement adsorber (2172241) and schedule an immediate adsorber replacement.
  3. Calculate when the next replacement is due. (Replace after 13,000 hours.)
  4. Record the calculated adsorber replacement date on the Site Log and on your schedule. Mark the next replacement due date on an external tag on the compressor.
  5. Connect the Lakeshore Diode Temperature Current Source Meter (46-317543G1) to the cable connected to the coldhead diode port. (Plug is located on coldhead mounting sleeve.)
  6. Temperature should read:

    • 32° to 60° K (first stage shield temperature)

    • 7° to 17° K (second stage shield temperature)

  7. Record the first stage and second stage shield temperatures on Magnet PM Report and on the Cryostat Performance Log 243612.pdf in the Site Log Book.
  8. If the shield temperature is out of spec, the helium boil-off, cryostat pressure and F1 and F2 flow rates can all increase. To find the cause of temperature increase, check the following:

    • Is the operator intermittently turning the coldhead OFF during scans?

    • Is something wrong with the compressor cooling water temperature, flow rate, or flow direction?

    • Is low equalized helium pressure indicated on the compressor gauge?

    • Are the coldhead mounting bolts tightened correctly? (Refer to the CD Direction 2180500-1, GE Magnet Subsystems, for the correct coldhead procedures.)

    • Is the coldhead contaminated?

  9. Check and record the compressor dynamic pressure. (Pressure should be between 2.1 and 2.3 mPa.)
  10. Turn the compressor OFF, and allow the static gas pressure to equalize on the supply gauge located on front of compressor.
  11. Record the equalized pressure on the PM Report and in the Cryostat Performance Log 243612.pdf in the Site Log Book. Pressure should be 217 ±3 psig at 70° F (21° C). If the pressure is not as specified, refer to the HC-10 Helium Compressor Operating Manual.
    Note:

    Charging of the compressor must be performed with 99.999% pure helium gas.

Sumitomo CSW-71 Compressor 4 Kelvin System (Zero Boil-Off)

Inspection of Sumitomo CSW-71 Compressor 4 Kelvin System

Procedure

  1. Notice
    Adsorber must be replaced after 20,000 hours of compressor run time.
    Check the compressor hour meter on front of compressor.
  2. Review the records to verify the adsorber was replaced on schedule.
    1. If the adsorber is not scheduled for replacement, go to Step 5, or
    2. If the adsorber should be replaced, order a replacement adsorber (2172241) and schedule an immediate adsorber replacement. (Refer to Replacement of Sumitomo CSW-71 Adsorber for the adsorber replacement procedure).
  3. Calculate when the next replacement is due. (Replace after 20,000 hours.)
  4. Record the calculated adsorber replacement date on the Site Log and on your schedule. Mark the next replacement due date on an external tag on the compressor, and indicate the replacement adsorber part number, 2172241.
  5. Connect the laptop to the magnet monitor per direction 2236995 (SIGNA HORIZON LCC Magnet Monitor Proactive Software GE Exclusive Use Only Installation and Troubleshooting Manual) for a SHeM or Direction 5212670 (Magnet Monitor 3 Installation and Service) for a Magnet Monitor 3.
  6. Use the graphing function to plot the magnet vessel pressure for several hours. Verify that vessel pressure is cycling between 3.9 psi and 4.1 psi.
  7. Select HDC (Heater Duty Cycle). Plot the duty cycle for the maximum number of days.

    • A consistent duty cycle indicates that the magnet is thermally stable.

    • A decreasing duty cycle indicates that the magnet thermal performance is decreasing. (A slow loss of helium will also affect the HDC.)

  8. If the HDC is decreasing or near zero, check the following:
    1. Shield temperature should read 35 to 42 K.
    2. RUO temperatures.
      Note:

      The magnet monitor is not accurate; there can be an error margin of 15%. Use the RUO meter (2171219) for accurate readings. Actual values should be between 4.1 and 4.5 K. The delta “T” between coldhead and recondenser RUO should be less than 0.20 K.

      If the shield, coldhead or recondenser temperatures are out of specification, a thermal issue may result. Refer to the "High Pressure and/or Helium Loss Troubleshooting" section in Direction 2192624 to resolve any potential issues.

    3. Use the graphing function to plot the helium level for the maximum number of days permitted. Verify a consistent helium level. If the helium level is decreasing, there may be a leak in the magnet. Perform leak troubleshooting as documented in the LCC Magnet Manual, Direction 2192624.
    4. Use the graphing function to plot the water temp and water flow for the maximum number of days. Verify that the water temp and flow to the compressor is consistent. Report any abnormalities to the site maintenance staff.
    5. Use the table function to view the last few months of data for CDC (Compressor Duty Cycle). Verify that the compressor was on for >95% of the time and within the manufacturer's requirements. Report any abnormalities to the site maintenance staff.
    6. Check and record the compressor dynamic pressure. (Pressure should be between 2.1 and 2.3 mPa.)
    7. Turn the compressor OFF, and allow the static gas pressure to equalize on the supply gauge located on the front of the compressor.
    8. Record the equalized pressure on the PM Report and in the Cryostat Performance Log (Table 10-1) in the Site Log Book. (Pressure should be 236 ±3 psig at 70° F (21° C). If pressure is not as specified, refer to the CSW-71 Compressor Operating Manual.)
      Note:

      Charging of the compressor must be performed with 99.999% pure helium gas.

Topic ID: id_SL2115386-640582

Replacement of Sumitomo CSW-71 Adsorber

Procedure

  1. Warning
    Electrical shock hazard!
    performing maintenance while compressor is connected to an electrical power source.
    Disconnect the compressor unit from power source before performing any maintenance procedure to avoid electrical shock.
    Notice
    The oil mist adsorber must be replaced every 20,000 hours of operation.
    Calculate when the next replacement is due.
  2. Shutdown the cryocooler.
  3. Disconnect the input power cable from the compressor unit.
  4. Disconnect the supply and return flex lines from the compressor unit.
  5. Loosen the screws that hold the compressor side panel and remove the side panel.
  6. Notice
    The Aeroquip fittings will usually bleed off 3 bars (45 psi) of helium gas pressure each time the lines are disconnected and reconnected to the coldhead or compressor. This requires a helium gas recharge with 99.999% pure helium gas any time the gas line connections are removed and reconnected.
    Using two wrenches, disconnect the adsorber self-sealing coupling.
  7. Using two wrenches, remove the nut securing the adsorber to the rear panel.
  8. Remove the nut and washer securing the adsorber to the base panel of the compressor unit.
  9. Remove the used adsorber from the compressor frame.
  10. Place the new adsorber in the compressor unit.
  11. Secure the adsorber to the base panel of the compressor unit by tightening the nut and washer.
  12. Secure the adsorber to the rear panel by tightening the nut.
  13. Notice
    The Aeroquip fittings usually bleed off 3 bars (45 psi) of helium gas pressure each time the lines are disconnected and reconnected to the coldhead or compressor. This requires a helium gas recharge with 99.999% pure helium gas any time the gas line connections are removed and reconnected.
    Connect the adsorber self-sealing coupling.
  14. Notice
    Check the flat rubber gasket of the self-sealing connector to make sure it is clean and properly positioned before proceeding. Make the first turns by hand and firmly seal the connection using three wrenches. Make the connection quickly to minimize minor gas leakage.
    Reinstall the side panel and secure it by tightening the screws.
  15. Ensure the pressure gauge indication is in the specified value for the type of coldhead, and charge the helium gas according to the steps in CTI System.
  16. Warning
    Personal injury hazard!
    the adsorber is under system pressure of 230 psi when disconnected from the chassis. this could cause serious personal injury if not depressurized before disposal.
    depressurize adsorber before disposing of it.
    To depressurize the adsorber:
    1. Slowly screw depressurization fitting (#12 Aeroquip) onto top of Aeroquip fitting of used adsorber.
    2. Point the bleed fitting away from personnel.

Replacement of Sumitomo F-50 Compressor Adsorber

Procedure

  1. Warning
    potential injury hazard!
    The cryocooler (coldhead, compressor unit, compressor adsorber, and flex lines) contain highly pressurized helium gas. if punctured, an explosion or escape of gas could occur.
    Keep all sharp objects away from the cryocooler. being sure to open the purging valve gradually, purge the helium gas from all components before disposing.
    Warning
    Potential Injury Hazard!
    Compressor is relatively unstable.
    To prevent injury or damage to equipment, do not place objects or stand on top of the compressor.
    CAUTION
    Heavy load!
    The adsorber is moderately heavy, weighing approximately 24 lb (11.5 kg).
    Be careful when handling to avoid injuries.
    Locate the tools shown in the table below.
    Table 5. Required Tools for Adsorber Replacement
    Tools Purpose
    11 in. open-end wrenchFor Aeroquip coupling
    21-1/8 in. open-end wrenchFor Aeroquip coupling
    31-3/16 in. open-end wrenchFor Aeroquip coupling
    41/2 in. open-end wrenchFor fixing nut on adsorber
    5Phillips screwdriverFor side panel of compressor unit
  2. Shut down the cryocooler.
  3. Disconnect the input power cable from the compressor unit.
  4. Disconnect the supply and return flex lines from the compressor unit.
  5. Loosen the screws that hold the compressor side panel and remove the panel.
  6. Disconnect the adsorber self-sealing coupling by using three wrenches.
  7. Remove the nut securing the adsorber to the rear panel by using two wrenches.
  8. Loosen the nut and washer securing the adsorber to the base panel of the compressor unit.
  9. Remove the adsorber from the compressor frame.

Installation of New Adsorber

Procedure

  1. Place the new adsorber in the compressor unit.
  2. Secure the adsorber to the front panel by tightening nut with two wrenches.
  3. Secure the replacement adsorber to the base panel of the compressor unit by tightening the nut.
  4. Connect the adsorber self-sealing coupling using three wrenches.
  5. Ensure that the pressure gauge indicates the specified value for the type of coldhead.
  6. Charge the helium gas in case of a low pressure indication.
  7. Reinstall the panels and secure them by the tightening the screws.
  8. Warning
    Personal injury hazard!

    depressurize adsorber before disposing of it.

    2. Point the bleed fitting away from personnel.

    • Is the compressor phase incorrect?

Replacement of Leybold Adsorber

Procedure

  1. Disconnect electrical power to the compressor.
  2. Remove both the supply and return gas lines at the compressor to isolate the coldhead from the compressor.
  3. Disconnect the Aeroquip fitting between the adsorber and compressor.
  4. Loosen the locking screw that holds the adsorber to the compressor frame, and remove the adsorber.
    Note:

    Certain Leybold absorbers have two extra bolts that secure the absorber to the compressor. Nuts that must be removed, to replace the absorber, are accessed underneath the compressor. For more details on the absorber, see the Direction 2213170 Errata Notes for Certain GE Magnet Systems.

  5. Inspect the Aeroquip fittings to ensure they are clean and that all gaskets are in place.
  6. Position the replacement adsorber, connect the compressor Aeroquip fitting, then tighten the locking screw. (Because the adsorber is shipped with a small helium charge, some sound may be heard as higher pressure gas from the compressor enters the adsorber.)
  7. Tighten the Aeroquip fittings completely.
  8. Attach both the supply and return gas lines to the compressor.
  9. Check the static charge of the compressor. Lower pressure within the adsorber may have reduced the overall pressure of the system. If so, recharge the system to the required pressure.
  10. Warning
    personal injury hazard!
    THE ADSORBER IS UNDER SYSTEM PRESSURE OF 230 PSI WHEN DISCONNECTED FROM THE CHASSIS. THIS COULD CAUSE SERIOUS PERSONAL INJURY IF NOT DEPRESSURIZED BEFORE DISPOSAL.
    DEPRESSURIZE ADSORBER BEFORE DISPOSING OF IT.
    To depressurize the adsorber:
    1. Slowly screw the depressurization fitting (#8 Aeroquip) onto top of Aeroquip fitting of used adsorber.
    2. Point the bleed fitting away from personnel.

Replacement of Balzers Adsorber

Procedure

  1. Disconnect electrical power to the compressor.
  2. Remove both the supply and return gas lines at the compressor to isolate the coldhead from the compressor.
  3. Remove the cover and skin from the compressor.
  4. Loosen and remove the two hex-head screws that attach the adsorber to the compressor frame.
  5. Loosen the top and bottom Aeroquip fittings of the adsorber at the same time until both are completely free and the adsorber can be removed.
  6. Inspect the Aeroquip fittings on the tubes that lead to and from the replacement adsorber to ensure they are clean and that all gaskets are in place.
  7. Position replacement adsorber, connect the top and bottom Aeroquip fittings of the adsorber at the same time. (Since adsorber is shipped with a small helium charge, some sound may be heard as higher pressure gas from compressor enters adsorber.)
  8. Tighten the Aeroquip fittings completely, stopping when threads bottom out.
  9. Record the date and compressor hours on the new adsorber and also in the Site Log.
  10. Bolt the adsorber in place using two hex-head screws.
  11. Check the static charge of compressor. Lower pressure within the adsorber may have reduced overall pressure of the system. If so, recharge system to required pressure.
  12. Warning
    personal injury hazard!
    THE ADSORBER IS UNDER SYSTEM PRESSURE OF 230 PSI WHEN DISCONNECTED FROM THE CHASSIS. THIS COULD CAUSE SERIOUS PERSONAL INJURY IF NOT DEPRESSURIZED BEFORE DISPOSAL.
    DEPRESSURIZE ADSORBER BEFORE DISPOSING OF IT.
    To depressurize the adsorber:
    1. Slowly screw the depressurization fitting (#12 Aeroquip) onto top of Aeroquip fitting of used adsorber.
    2. Point the bleed fitting away from personnel.

Replacement of Sumitomo CSA-71A Compressor Adsorber

About this task

Replace the oil mist adsorber every 20,000 hours of operation. Following are steps to remove the used adsorber.

Procedure

  1. Warning
    explosion/escape of gas!
    This cryocooler (coldhead, compressor unit, compressor adsorber, flex lines) contains a high pressure (about 1.62 MPa (16.5 kgf/cm2G, 235 psig)) helium gas sealed in. Hitting the equipment with a sharp edge or touching it with a pointed object may cause explosion or escape of gas. Handle the equipment with extreme care.
    Do not disassemble the equipment for purposes other than maintenance specified in this service manual under any circumstances. Disassembling the equipment may result in electric shock, explosion or escape of gas. The cold head, compressor unit, compressor adsorber and flex lines are pressurized with helium gas. Purge the helium gas from all pressurized components before disposing. Open the purging valve gradually or it may result in serious injury.
    Warning
    rotating part!
    For CSA-71A (air cooled, low voltage type), a venting fan is provided in the exhaust section at the top of the compressor unit.
    Do not insert foreign substances from the exhaust port under any circumstances. Failing to observe this precaution may result in injury or malfunction.
    CAUTION
    Heavy object!
    The adsorber weight is about 11.0 kg.
    Be careful when handling so no injury occurs when replacing the adsorber.
    CAUTION
    Personal injury!
    Do not get on the compressor unit or put an object on top of it.
    Failing to observe this precaution may prevent the cryocooler from operating normally or may cause injury.
    Review part number and tools information in the following tables before replacing the adsorber.
    Table 8. Adsorber for Compressor Unit
    Item Description Qty Part Number Remark
    1Adsorber1RE71TN0408OD155×H447
    Table 9. Required Tools for Adsorber Replacement
    Item Tools Remark

    4Snoop liquidFor leak check

    7Screwdriver (Phillips)For side panel of compressor unit

  3. Disconnect the input power cable from the compressor unit.

Cleaning of Sumitomo CSA-71A Compressor Cooler

About this task

Periodic cleaning for the air-cooled heat exchanger for the lube oil/gas cooler of the compressor unit is an essential step to maintain cryocooler performance and reliability. The cooler for the compressor unit has a minimum operating life of around one year in the computer/equipment room. The frequency of cleaning will depend on the environmental conditions of the compressor unit.

Procedure

  1. Warning
    Personal Injury!
    Touching the fin may cause injury.
    Do not touch the cooler fin of the Compressor Unit during fin cleaning.
    Loosen the screws that hold the cooler cover panel and remove the panel.
  2. Remove dust on the surface of the compressor cooling fins using a portable vacuum cleaner.
  3. Replace the cooler cover front panel and secure by tightening the screws.
