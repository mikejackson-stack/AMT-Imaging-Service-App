Magnet - Hazard Statements

Prerequisites

Safety

Before working in any GE Healthcare MR suite or performing any GE Healthcare service procedure, you must:

  • Have read and understood all hazard conditions and safety requirements in the latest revision of the GE Healthcare MR Service Safety Manual (5452735).
  • Have successfully completed all relevant GE Healthcare Environmental Health and Safety (EHS) courses (or for non-GE employees, equivalent workplace training courses).
  • Comply with all site-specific training and workplace safety requirements.

If you have any safety concerns at any time, do not begin work or immediately stop work and move to a safe location. Immediately contact your supervisor or site safety officer for instructions on how to proceed.
  • notice
  • Equipment modification prohibited
  • No modification of this equipment (either MRU or Remote MRU) is allowed.
  • notice
  • Possible component damage
  • This procedure will cause loss of cooling fluids and possible magnet damage.
  • Use this procedure only in an emergency.
  • notice
  • Component damage risk
  • Improper transportation can result in damage to the magnet.
  • Tasks in this section are to be performed by riggers, not by GE Service Personnel.
  • Weights referenced in section Equipment requirements are rigging weights (rounded up from actual weights) and are not actual shipping weights. Refer to magnet shipping documents for actual weights of the magnet and crate.
  • Do not use a crane to lift a magnet that is on a pallet or inside a crate. Crane lifting can only be done using the magnet lifting rails, which are not accessible while the magnet is inside the crate.
  • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • Improper removal of the shipping cage can result in damage to the magnet.
  • Do not use a crane to move the magnet if it is still in the crate. If you are using a crane, you need clear access to the orange lifting beams, which are not accessible while the magnet is inside the crate.
  • Remove the magnet's shipping crate while the magnet/crate/pallet package is on the trailer/transport.
  • Damage to the magnet and/or magnet enclosures may result if the crate is removed while the crate's side panels are in their closed (shipping) position, or if the crate is disassembled while the magnet is inside the crate.
  • Do not remove any lag screws on the crate or pallet until the crane is positioned, and the slings/cable bridles are attached to the lifting rings on the crate.
  • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • Improper transportation can result in expensive internal damage to the magnet.
  • Avoid sudden jolts. Lift/move/lower the magnet smoothly. Do not allow the magnet to bump or hit anything forcefully.
  • Avoid tilting the magnet more than the maximum tilt (30° from the horizontal level).
  • Do not apply any force to the magnet enclosures.
  • Do not crane lift a magnet that is on a pallet or inside a cage or crate.
  • Crane lifting can only be done using the magnet lifting rails, which are not accessible while the magnet is inside a cage or crate.
  • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • Care must be taken not to scrape or hit the sides of the magnet. The magnet is shipped inside plastic bubble wrap.   • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • Improper loads on enclosure cover parts can result in damage to the enclosure.
  • Do not apply any loads to enclosure cover parts.
  • Do not allow straps, cables, or chains to scrape enclosure cover parts.
  • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • Attempting to reset the alarm on the compressor without resolving the problem can result in damage to the compressor.
  • If there is any alarm or warning on the compressor, find the root cause for the alarm or warning and resolve the problem before turning on the compressor.
  • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • The connector insertion removal tool can freeze inside the vertical penetration if you take too long to grab the LEMO connector.
  • Work quickly and accurately to reach the LEMO connector.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • Do not press the RUNDOWN button throughout this process.
  • Do not reconnect the MRU cable if you find it disconnected. GEHC strongly recommends that you stop using the system and immediately call your qualified service representative.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • Do not attempt to measure resistance by connecting the Ohmeter across magnet heater connections.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • Failure to heed this warning may cause a magnet quench.
  • Before connecting P2 to J2, make sure that the AC supply main's power has been applied to the MRU for at least 3 minutes and that the RUNDOWN button has not been actuated for at least 3 minutes. The heater voltage is off before connecting to the magnet.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • Make sure the magnet heaters are not connected to J2 to prevent inadvertent magnet quench.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • The magnet must be ramped down prior to pressurizing the magnet with helium gas to 4 psig to prevent inadvertent magnet quench.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • A magnet quench during the procedure could result in rapid expulsion of liquid helium through the vertical penetration.
  • Make sure the magnet is ramped down to zero field before starting this procedure.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • Heating the coldhead sleeve to > 275 K may damage instrumentation wiring inside the magnet or lead to a magnet quench.
  • Do not heat the coldhead sleeve to > 275 K.
  • notice
  • Potential magnet quench
  • Exceeding the gas helium flow rate of 130 CFH may cause a magnet quench.
  • Do not exceed the gas helium flow rate of 130 CFH.
  • notice
  • Accidental activation risk
  • An improper mounting location can result in an accidental activation of the guarded MRU switch by cleaning staff and so on.
  • Use care when selecting the mounting location.
  • warning
  • Electric shock
  • To avoid the risk of electric shock, this equipment must only be connected to an AC supply mains with protective earth.
  • warning
  • Electric shock
  • Contact with connectors leading to an energized compressor can cause electrical shock.
  • Disconnect input power to the compressor and follow LOTO procedures to make sure power is not supplied to the compressor.
  • notice
  • LOTO
  • Follow all local safety codes for Lock Out-Tag Out.
  • warning
  • Hazardous voltages
  • Hazardous voltages are accessible at components F1, TB1, S3, RV1 and RV2, and at the lower terminals of T1.
  • To avoid electric shock, use caution when the MRU cover is open to make sure that contact is not made with any of these components.
  • warning
  • High temperature
  • The 20 OHM power resistor will become hot during tests.
  • danger
  • Death or serious injury
  • It is critical you take proper precautions when servicing highly ferrous components in a magnetic environment.
  • Failure to do so may result in death or serious injury to service engineers.
  • warning
  • Potential projectile hazard
  • Aeroquip fittings, which are ferrous materials, can become dangerous projectiles if disconnected in the presence of the magnetic field that the magnet produces.
  • Do not disconnect Aeroquip fittings in the presence of a magnetic field.
  • danger
  • Potential asphyxiation or cold burn
  • Internal cryostat pressure buildup of > 5.25 psig will open the 5.25 psig relief valve.
  • Do the following before removing either ramp or fill port caps, or loosening any component resulting in the release of cryogen pressure:
    • Observe the internal cryostat pressure gauge reading.
    • Set the Magnet Monitor pressure to 0.5 psig.
    • Vent the cryostat down to 0.2 psig through vent valve V2, or through vent valve V3 if your site has plumbing assembly 5555227.
  • danger
  • Potential asphyxiation hazard
  • Loss of magnet vacuum will result in the rapid expulsion of helium gas, which can cause asphyxiation in enclosed areas.
  • Use extreme caution and do not contact or damage the vacuum vessel during magnet transit or siting.
  • danger
  • Potential asphyxiation hazard
  • Loss of magnet vacuum will result in the rapid expulsion of helium gas, which can cause asphyxiation in enclosed areas.
  • Make sure that hospital personnel are aware of this situation prior to the magnet being moved within the enclosed areas. Use extreme caution and do not contact or damage the vacuum vessel during magnet transit or siting.
  • caution
  • Potential cold burn
  • Exposure to cryogens and contact with cold objects is possible during this procedure.
  • Wear protective clothing, nonabsorbent gloves, and goggles while doing this procedure.
  • notice
  • Flow meter orientation
  • The flow meter must be mounted horizontally with the inlet and outlet piping located at the top. This orientation allows any trapped air in the lines to pass through the meter cavity.
  • Do not remove the 5-inch length of copper pipe attached to inlet and outlet ports of the meter. This straight length of pipe is required to minimize turbulent flow through the meter, which impacts the performance of the meter.
  • The flow meter is bi-directional; it does not matter which end is used for the inlet and outlet.
  • caution
  • Hazardous chemicals
  • If water from the compressor spills, clean it up following hospital procedures; the water may contain chemicals, such as glycol.
  • warning
  • Poisonous substance
  • Antifreeze is poisonous to aquatic, animal, and human life. Because of its sweetness, animals are drawn to it. It is classified as hazardous both for shipment and as waste, and cannot be shipped by untrained people.
  • Cryocooler compressors that contain antifreeze must be drained before being returned to the supplier. Make arrangements with the hospital maintenance department for proper disposal.
  • warning
  • Potential injury
  • A bending force can cause Aeroquip stems to bend or break, and will create difficulty in the rapid engagement or disengagement required to prevent helium loss and system contamination.
  • Flexlines with broken Aeroquip fittings may disconnect and move erratically. Do not put any bending force on Aeroquip fittings while connecting or disconnecting helium flexlines.
  • warning
  • Potential injury
  • Magnet is an unbalanced load. Tilting can result in a hazardous condition that can cause magnet damage or serious personal injury.
  • Do the following to avoid tilting:
    • Make sure that the lifting apparatus (crane, spreader beam, and so on) meets the specifications stated in this manual.
    • Orient the spreader beam parallel to the lifting rails.
    • Adjust the lifting cables/slings and spreader beam lift point to level the magnet before fully lifting the magnet off the surface.
    • Make sure that the entire area where lifting will occur is free of obstructions and unauthorized personnel.
    • Make sure the surface where the magnet will be placed after lifting is flat.
    • Do not crane lift a magnet during dangerous weather conditions.
  • caution
  • Potential personal injury
  • Uneven jacking of the magnet's corners could result in the magnet shifting on the jacks, which may lead to personal injury or magnet damage.
  • Keep the magnet level at all times during any jacking operation.
  • warning
  • Potential serious injuries
  • Moving a magnet through a raised opening in an exterior wall can result in serious injuries and magnet/equipment damage.
  • Do the following before moving the magnet:
    • Make sure a complete walk-through of the crane lift process was performed prior to the actual event to make sure all process details are covered.
    • Make sure all necessary equipment is on-site and inspected for safety and load ratings.
    • Make sure all necessary personnel are trained and ready.
    • Make sure that the entire area where lifting will occur is free of obstructions and unauthorized personnel.
    • Make sure the surface where the magnet will be placed after lifting is flat.
    • Do not crane lift a magnet during dangerous weather conditions.
  • warning
  • Potential injury hazards
  • Improper lifting of the magnet may be hazardous to personnel and can result in damage to the magnet and equipment.
  • Make sure that the entire area where lifting will occur is free of obstructions and unauthorized personnel.
  • Make sure that the surface where the magnet will be placed after lifting is flat.
  • caution
  • Potential injury hazard
  • Pushing magnet enclosures may result in bodily injury to personnel.
  • Do not push magnet enclosures. Follow the directions on the six "Do Not Push" signs (one on each side, and two on each end).
  • caution
  • Potential health hazard and spike noise
  • Loose metal filings produced during drilling could become a health hazard and a source of spike noise.
  • Make sure to clean up after drilling.
  • Wear appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) during drilling operations: gloves, safety glasses, and safety shoes.
  • danger
  • Fatal explosive hazard
  • Damage or rupture to the gas cylinder or opening the cylinder valve quickly will release 2400 PSI gas from cylinder.
  • Make sure gas cylinders are secure from falls and away from moving objects before using. Always open cylinder valve slowly.
  • warning
  • Potential high pressure gas release
  • Potential explosive release of high pressure (approximately 1.62 MPA, 16.5 KGF/CM*CM*G, 235 psig) helium gas if compressor system components are hit with sharp edge or touched with pointed object.
  • Handle equipment with care. Keep sharp edged and pointed objects away from the compressor system.
  • notice
  • Potential image interference
  • Improper magnet setup can result in image problems.
  • The magnet must be connected to the helium exhaust gas vent as soon as possible but within 24 hours.
  • Complete contact between the bottom of the magnet feet and the floor or vibroacoustic mat is important to minimize magnet motion/vibration.
  • warning
  • High water pressure
  • Coolant is circulating through the gradient coil at high pressure.
  • Follow LOTO procedures while performing services to the coolant circulation lines.
  • caution
  • Heavy equipment
  • The bridge weighs 85 pounds.
  • Two people are required to lift the bridge.
  • warning
  • Ferrous material hazard
  • The pulley assembly contains parts made with ferrous components. Holding this assembly too close to the magnet bore will forcibly attract it to the magnet.
  • To prevent possible bodily injury or damage to components, remove the assembly directly. Do not move it into the magnetic field more than is necessary.
  • warning
  • Ferrous material hazard
  • The front bridge support assembly contains parts made with ferrous components. Holding this assembly too close to the magnet bore will forcibly attract it to the magnet.
  • To prevent possible bodily injury or damage to components, remove the assembly directly. Do not move it into the magnetic field more than is necessary.
  • warning
  • Ferrous material hazard
  • The dockable table string encoder assembly contains parts made with ferrous components. Holding this assembly too close to the magnet bore will forcibly attract it to the magnet.
  • To prevent possible bodily injury or damage to components, remove the assembly directly. Do not move it into the magnetic field more than is necessary.
  • warning
  • Ferrous material hazard
  • The mounting bracket, encoder, and drive motor contain ferrous material.
  • Holding this assembly too close to the magnet bore will forcibly attract it to the magnet. To prevent possible bodily injury or material damage, use two people to promptly remove mounting bracket assembly from magnet room.
  • warning
  • Ferrous material
  • The rotary pump's primary substance is steel and poses extremely dangerous projectile hazard if working in magnet room.
  • Make sure you are doing the procedure outside of the magnet room.
  • warning
  • Ferrous material hazard
  • The Portable RuO Temperature Monitor contains ferrous materials.
  • Keep meter away from the magnet bore.
  • warning
  • Personal injury possibility
  • When doing maintenance on the table in its up position, severe personal injury can occur if the table is accidentally lowered during maintenance.
  • Make sure the lock safety bar is safely in place.
  • warning
  • Back injury
  • The dock is heavy, weighing more than 50 pounds, and it contains ferrous material, making it strongly attracted to the magnet.
  • Two people are necessary to move the dock out of the magnet room to perform maintenance. From this point in the procedure, downward pressure must be continuously applied to the dock to prevent it from being pulled into the magnet bore.
  • warning
  • Possible personal injury
  • To oppose any pull from the magnet, field engineers must apply continuous downward pressure to the top of the dock, even when lifting the dock in this step.
  • Do not place any part of the human body in the path between the dock and the magnet.
  • warning
  • Possible personal injury
  • The dock contains highly ferrous material.
  • Never lift the front of the dock (the portion of the dock farthest from the magnet) more than 3 inches (7.6 cm) from the floor. The rear of the dock (the portion of the dock closest to the magnet) should always remain in contact with the floor. Never lift the rear of the dock off the floor in the magnet room.
  • warning
  • Possible personal injury
  • This procedure requires movement of ferrous material in close proximity to the magnet.
  • Two field engineers are required at all times.
  • caution
  • Potential injury
  • When rotating the ice coring tool, cold gas and pieces of ice can eject from the tube.
  • Keep clear of the ice coring tool.
  • notice
  • Equipment damage risk
  • If you use a tool other than the Connector Insertion Tool (46-281934P1) from the SavCon and Instrumentation Lead Installation and Removal Kit (46-294872G3), it may become lodged inside the turret.
  • Make sure you use the Connector Insertion Tool (46-281934P1) from the SavCon and Instrumentation Lead Installation and Removal Kit (46-294872G3) when replacing the SavCon connector.
