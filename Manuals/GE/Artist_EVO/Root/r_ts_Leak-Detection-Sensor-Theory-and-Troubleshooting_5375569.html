Leak detection sensor theory and troubleshooting

Topic ID: id_SL2190580-1067795

Theory

Leak detection consists of a series of cable segments positioned in different areas of the PGR cabinet and joined by interconnecting cables and connectors. Each cable segment is a two-conductor, ladder-line design whereby the parallel conductors are separated a set distance apart the length of the cable by an insulating dielectric. These cables are located in front of the PDU, above the PDU, and under the RF amplifier. They are interconnected as shown below.

Cabinet monitor connects to one end of interconnected string of cable segments and constantly measures the total line impedance. Any moisture on Leak sensor 1 results in an impedance that causes the Cabinet monitor to shunt trip the PDU main breaker, which removes power from the input of the PDU.

DANGER
Electric shock hazard
Lethal voltages are present in the PGR Power Distribution Unit (PDU) or gradient cabinet.
Lock out and tag out the power at the main disconnect panel (MDP) before proceeding.

Opening any of the connections between line segments, however, causes the Auxiliary board or PEM to latch into failing state whereby an error is logged and scanning is inhibited. Opening a line segment does not result in PDU main breaker trip. Recovery from latched state is only possible by reconnecting segments and then cycling power to the Auxiliary board or PEM (Gradient 48V Control on front of PDU).

Opening any of the connections between line segments and moisture on leak sensor 2/3/4, however, causes the cabinet monitor to latch into failing state whereby an error is logged and scanning is inhibited. And trigger PDU into E-stop condition.

The terminating block contains a special diode so that the line measures as a continuous loop to the Cabinet Monitor.

Failure mode 1 and Failure mode 4 – PDU main breaker trip

Refer to Figure 1, and visually inspect for moisture on lines. LOTO incoming power to PGR cabinet and repair any leaks. Use absorbent material to thoroughly dry any moisture from above, below, and around leak detection lines.

Topic ID: id_SL2190718-1067795

Failure mode 2 – PDU main breaker trip, lines dry

DANGER
Shock hazard and death by electrocution
Standing water inside cabinets poses an electrocution risk.
Under no circumstances should you proceed unless you have visually inspected and confirmed that no standing water is present inside the cabinet.
  1. On the PDU, turn off the breaker labeled Gradient 420V Power.
  2. Disconnect leak sensor route 1 in figure 1 shown, and then reset the PDU main breaker. It is normal for a disconnect error to be logged when this is done.
  3. The point at which the breaker does not trip indicates a faulty line segment in the previous section.
  4. Replace or repair the faulty line segment.
  5. If all cables are disconnected and the PDU main breaker is still tripping, then replace Cabinet monitor.
  6. Confirm reattachment of all circuit connectors.
  7. After replacement, cycle power to cabinet monitor to reset it from latched fault condition.
Topic ID: id_SL2190972-1067795

Failure mode 3 – leak sensor disconnect error; scanning inhibited, no main breaker trip

There are several possible causes for this failure mode. Follow the steps in the sections below to determine the cause of the error.

Topic ID: id_SL5599834-1067795

Terminator connection

  1. Sometimes the terminator on the leak strip is bumped and disconnected when the PDU front cover is removed for service. It may appear connected when it is not.
  2. Verify that the terminator is connected by pushing down the tabs on top with a small screwdriver, and simultaneously forcing the wire into its opening.
Topic ID: id_SL5599838-1067795

Cable connections

  1. Check that the cable is attached at at J6 on the cabinet monitor.
  2. Trace the other end of the cable and confirm that it is labeled Leak Sensor 1-4.
