About this task

Overview

This document contains a method for measuring ground resistance using different brands of safety analyzers. Only one of the safety analyzers listed in Tools and Test Equipment is required for this procedure.

Ground resistance checks are required at every site. Ground Leakage Test is only done when local regulation or code requires it.

Ground resistance checks are required for the following cabinets.

  • PEN cabinet
  • Host GOC
  • HEC
  • Secondary PEN panel or SPW
  • Brainwave cabinet (optional equipment)
  • MNS cabinet (optional equipment)

Review and understand the required conditions before beginning this procedure.

Ground resistance checks using Microguard

Procedure

  1. DANGER
    Fatal electric shock hazard
    Lethal voltages are present in the power distribution unit (PDU) even if all PDU breakers are off.
    Disconnect and complete LOTO on the power from the system at the facility's main disconnect. See the MR Service Safety Manual (5452735).
    Apply MDP LOTO. See Applying LOTO - Main Disconnect Panel (MDP).
  2. Notify all Field Service Engineers working at the site that the MDP is being shut off, locked out, and tagged out.
  3. Locate and shut off the MDP which supplies power to the PDU and HEC.
  4. Check that the MDP and PDU indicator lights are off, and that the power switches are inactive.
  5. With a digital voltmeter (DVM), check that all energy has dissipated.
  6. Plug the milliohm adaptor module into the power outlet of the Microguard.
    Note Because the Microguard is being used solely as a voltmeter in this application, any other meter capable of resolving 0.1 millivolts can be substituted in the next step. Meters other than the Microguard are currently in use. Refer to the appropriate tool catalog for further information.
  7. Attach the Microguard red and black leads to the meter posts of the milliohm module.
  8. Set the Microguard Patient Lead Tester switch to All Other Tests.
  9. Set the Microguard Selector switch to 20 Microamps/Millivolts.
  10. Set the Microguard Input switch to Red and Black Probes.
  11. Attach the Kelvin leads. Connect one lead to the PDU ground located on the upper left side of the PGR cabinet and the other lead to the frame of the subsystem cabinet under test.
  12. The meter reads voltage across ground resistance in millivolts. To obtain the resistance in milliohms, multiply the meter reading by 2.
  13. Check that the measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms (corresponding to a meter reading of 50 millivolts). If this fails, recheck and reposition the lead connections and inspect the power cable connection from PGR J16 to the PEN cabinet. If the reading fails, add an additional ground cable to the cabinet that is failing.
  14. Complete Step 11 through Step 13 for the remaining cabinets:
    • Host GOC
    • HEC
    • Secondary PEN panel or SPW
    • Brainwave cabinet (optional equipment)
    • MNS cabinet (optional equipment)
    Note Additional cable may be needed to measure ground resistance to the selected cabinet, because the distance between the PGR and the selected cabinet can vary.
  15. Restore the system:
    • Turn the Microguard power off.
    • Remove the leads and the milliohm adapter from the Microguard.
    • Close all subsystem cabinets and the PGR cabinet.

Ground resistance checks using Dale (sites equipped with Dale 600, 600E, 601, or 601E Safety Analyzer)

Procedure

  1. DANGER
    Electrocution Hazard!
    Lethal voltages are present within the MDP even when all MDP breakers are off.
    Check that power at the main disconnect is off, locked out, and tagged out before proceeding.
    Apply MDP LOTO. See Applying LOTO - Main Disconnect Panel (MDP).
  2. Notify all Field Service Engineers working at the site that the MDP is being shut off, locked out, and tagged out.
  3. Locate and shut off the MDP which supplies power to the PDU and HEC.
  4. Check that the MDP and PDU indicator lights are off, and that the power switches are inactive.
  5. With a digital voltmeter (DVM), check that all energy has dissipated.
  6. Connect one clamp-type lead to the EXTERNAL jack on the top of the Dale safety analyzer. Connect the other clamp-type lead to the CHASSIS jack on the top of the Dale safety analyzer.
  7. On the Dale safety analyzer, set the main selector switch to W - RESISTANCE.
  8. Plug the Dale safety analyzer into an AC outlet.
  9. On the Dale safety analyzer, make sure that the two OK LEDs light up to indicate that the AC outlet is properly wired.
  10. With the leads not touching each other, the display should read 1.00. With the leads touching (copper to copper), the display should read 0.00 to 0.02.
  11. Attach the leads. Connect one lead to the PDU ground located on the upper left side of the PGR cabinet (see PDU ground stud) and the other lead to the bare frame of the subsystem cabinet under test.
    Note The Dale safety analyzer reads resistance in ohms. For example, a reading of 0.50 equals 500 milliohms.
  12. Make sure that the measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms (corresponding to a meter reading of 50 millivolts). If this fails, recheck and reposition the lead connections and inspect the power cable connection from PGR J16 to the PEN cabinet. If the reading fails, add an additional ground cable to the cabinet that is failing.
  13. Complete Step 11 through Step 12 for the remaining cabinets:

    • Host GOC

    • HEC
    • Secondary PEN panel or SPW
    • Brainwave cabinet (optional equipment)

    • MNS cabinet (optional equipment)

    Note Additional cable may be needed to measure ground resistance to the selected cabinet, because the distance between the PGR and the selected cabinet can vary.
  14. Restore the system:
    1. Unplug the Dale safety analyzer from the AC outlet.
    2. Remove the leads from the Dale safety analyzer.
    3. Close all subsystem cabinets and the PGR cabinet.

Ground resistance checks using Fluke ESA612

Procedure

  1. DANGER
    Electrocution Hazard!
    Lethal voltages are present within the MDP even when all MDP breakers are off.
    Check that power at the main disconnect is off, locked out, and tagged out before proceeding.
    Apply MDP LOTO. See Applying LOTO - Main Disconnect Panel (MDP).
  2. Notify all Field Service Engineers working at the site that the MDP is being shut off, locked out, and tagged out.
  3. Locate and shut off the MDP which supplies power to the PDU and HEC.
  4. Check that the MDP and PDU indicator lights are off, and the power switches are inactive.
  5. With a digital voltmeter (DVM), check that all energy has dissipated.
  6. Note At facilities with 110-120 VAC, a fault error may appear when you power the meter on. Press F4 OK to clear the error, and continue.
    Turn on the Fluke meter and select the point to point option on the meter.
  7. Select resistance or F2.
  8. Attach the leads into the red and black input jackets and short the leads together.
  9. Select zero leads or F4.
  10. Connect one lead to the PDU ground located on the upper left side of the PGR cabinet (see PDU ground stud) and the other lead to the bare frame of the subsystem cabinet under test.
  11. Make sure that the measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms. If this fails, recheck and reposition the lead connections and inspect the power cable connection from PGR J16 to the PEN cabinet. If the reading fails, add an additional ground cable to the cabinet that is failing.
  12. Repeat Step 10 through Step 11 for the remaining cabinets:
    • Host GOC
    • HEC
    • Secondary PEN panel or SPW
    • Brainwave cabinet (optional equipment)
    • MNS cabinet (optional equipment)
    Note Additional cable may be needed to measure ground resistance to the selected cabinet, because the distance between the PGR and the selected cabinet can vary.
  13. Restore the system.
    1. Turn off the Fluke safety analyzer.
    2. Remove the leads from the analyzer.
    3. Close all subsystem cabinets and the PGR cabinet.
  14. Remove MDP LOTO. See Removing LOTO - Main Disconnect Panel (MDP).
