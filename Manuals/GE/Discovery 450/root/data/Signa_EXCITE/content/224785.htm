2  Overview

All TRC 7.5T750 Main Coil Power Supplies (46-260776G3) should be checked in conformance with the TCR 7.5T750 Main Power Supply Tests procedure at least once a year. In addition, use the TCR 7.5T750 Main Power Supply Tests procedure when a TRC 7.5T750 Power Supply is suspected of faulty operation and the supply cannot be sent to an approved calibration/repair facility before it is needed. This document is intended only for MAC Team members who have been trained by a member of the “Power Supply Team."

4  Procedure

4.1  Heater Current Meter Zero Calibration

  1. Disconnect and lockout/tagout input power to the power supply to be tested. Verify that no voltage is present by using a DVM or equivalent measuring device.

  1. Remove the cover plates to access the meter's mechanical Zero Adjustment Screw (located adjacent to each meter). See Illustration 1.

    Illustration 1:  750 Amp TCR 7.5T750 Superconducting Main Coil Service Power Supply Cabinet (46-260776G3)

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “O" (off).

  1. Adjust each meter's mechanical Zero Adjustment Screw as required to position the meter indicator needle at “0" (zero).

  1. Replace the cover plates.

4.2  Heater Calibration

  1. Disconnect and lockout/tagout input power to the power supply to be tested. Verify that no voltage is present by using a DVM or equivalent measuring device.

  1. Connect the P3 test connector to the heater outputs J3, located on the rear of the power supply. See Illustration 1.

  1. Remove the eight mounting screws holding the Control Panel to the power supply.

  1. Pull out the Utility Drawer, then place the Control Panel face down on the drawer to expose the component side of the panel.

  1. Connect an external DVM (set to “DC Volts") to the Heater 1 Main Standoffs, located on the component side of the Control Panel. Use the set of standoffs nearest the heater voltmeter. See Illustration 2.

    Illustration 2:  Rear View, Power Supply Control Panel

  1. Remove lockout/tagout and connect 208 VAC to the AC input connector.

  1. Set the Main Power and Power On Switches to “ON."

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main Switch to “I" (on).

  1. Adjust the #1 Current Adjustment Potentiometer, located on the rear of the power supply, until the external voltmeter reads 24.3 VDC. See Illustration 1.

  1. Adjust the Heater 1 Main Meter Offset Potentiometer, located on the component side of the Control Panel (Illustration 2), until the Heater 1 Main Current Meter reads 810 mA DC.

  1. Set the Heater 2 Shim Axial Switch to “I" (on).

  1. Connect an external DVM (set to “DC Volts") to the Heater 2 Shim Axial standoffs, located on the component side of the Control Panel. See Illustration 2.

  1. Adjust the #2 Current Adjustment Potentiometer, located on the rear of the power supply (Illustration 1), until the external voltmeter reads 24.3 VDC.

  1. Adjust the Heater 2 Shim Axial Meter Offset Potentiometer, located on the component side of the Control Panel (Illustration 2), until the Heater 2 Shim Axial Current Meter reads 810 mA DC.

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “I" (off).

  1. Set the Main Power and Power On Switches to “OFF."

4.3  Voltage Calibration

Voltage Calibration is checked under “no load" condition. Make sure the Ramp Leads are disconnected for this test.

  1. Turn off the power supply. Then disconnect and lockout/tagout input power to the power supply. Verify that no voltage is present by using a DVM or equivalent measuring device.

  1. Remove the TB2 Access Cover Plate. See Illustration 3.

    Illustration 3:  Main Power Supply Rear View

  1. Make sure the P3 test connector is connected to heater outputs J3, located on the rear of the power supply.

  1. Set the DVM to the “DC Volt" scale.

  1. Connect the DVM's positive lead to TB2 pin 1 and its negative lead to TB2 pin 7.

    NOTE:TB2 is numbered from left to right.

  1. Adjust the Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer to minimum (full CCW) and both Current Adjustment Potentiometers to maximum (full CW).

DANGER
Fatal Electrical Shock Hazard!
With the TB2 Access Cover Plate removed, 208 VAC 3-phase on TB1 is exposed.
Be extremely careful not to come in contact with TB1.

  1. Remove lockout/tagout and reconnect input power to the power supply.

  1. Set the Main Power and Power On Switches to “ON."

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “I" (on).

  1. Adjust the Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer to maximum (full CW). The DVM should display within the range of 7.0 VDC - 8.0 VDC.

    NOTE:The ideal DVM reading is 7.5 volts. Voltages < 7.5 volts could increase ramp up time. Voltages > 8.0 volts could damage output capacitors. If the voltage < 7.0 VDC or > 8.0 VDC, return the power supply for repair.

  1. Adjust the Current Coarse Adjustment Potentiometer to minimum (full CCW).

  1. Adjust the Current Adjust Fine Potentiometer to minimum (full CCW). As the dial on the Current Adjust Fine Potentiometer is adjusted to within 5 index markings of full counterclockwise, as shown in Illustration 4, the DVM reading should drop to within the range of 0.0 VDC and -0.6 VDC. If necessary, adjust the R605 potentiometer, located on the A600 PC board behind the power supply Control Panel (Illustration 2), to the point at which the DVM drop occurs.

    NOTE:This adjustment is a step function. The DVM reading will not vary continuously as R605 is adjusted. The function of R605 is to set the threshold at which no voltage is available at the power supply output when the Current Adjustment Potentiometers are set to minimum. If R605 is adjusted much beyond the threshold point, there will be a delay between the point at which the Current Adjustment Potentiometers are adjusted, from minimum, until there is a noticeable current output of power supply.

    NOTE:It takes approximately 2 minutes for the DVM reading to decay to between 0.0 Volts and -0.6 Volts. A few trials adjusting the Current Adjust Fine Potentiometer must be done to insure the power supply output voltage drops and rises as the Current Adjust Fine Potentiometer is adjusted in and out of the range. See Illustration 4.

    Illustration 4:  Current Adjust Potentiometers

  1. Adjust the Current Adjust Fine Potentiometer in and out of the range stated in Step 12 to insure proper adjustment of R605.

    NOTE:The Current Fine Adjustment Potentiometer is very sensitive.

  1. Reinstall the Control Panel if previously removed.

    NOTE:The Control Panel will have to be removed again later the TCR 7.5T750 Main Power Supply Tests procedure. It is only necessary to replace a couple of the Control Panel mounting screws at this time.

4.4  Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer Noise Test

The Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer is checked under “no load" condition. Make sure the Ramp Leads are disconnected for this test.

  1. Disconnect and lockout/tagout input power to the power supply. Verify that no voltage is present by using a DVM or equivalent measuring device.

  1. Verify that the Ramp Leads are disconnected.

  1. Measure the resistance, using an ohmmeter, from the Negative Main Terminal to any point on the power supply chassis. The resistance will increase to well within the Megohm range as the output capacitor is being charged by the ohmmeter. Send the power supply to a repair facility if the resistance check indicates a shorted or leaky output capacitor.

  1. Remove the mounting screws securing the TB2 Access Cover Plate. The plate is located between the main terminals at the bottom rear of the power supply and protects a fan and terminal board TB2. See Illustration 3.

  1. Connect the X1 Probe to the Oscilloscope.

  1. Connect the X1 Probe's ground lead to TB2 pin 7 and its signal lead on TB2 pin 3.

    NOTE:TB2 is numbered from left to right.

  1. Set the Oscilloscope's “Time Base" Control to 5 msec/div (X1 Probe).

  1. Set the Oscilloscope's “VOLT/DIV" Control to 5 mV/div.

  1. Set the input coupling to “AC."

  1. Plug the Oscilloscope power cable into an AC outlet and turn the Oscilloscope power switch on.

  1. Remove lockout/tagout and reconnect input power to the power supply. Turn all power supply breaker switches on.

    NOTE:The Oscilloscope beam will move upward and downward as the Voltage Adjustment Control is adjusted.

  1. Adjust the Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer through its full range of operation while observing the Oscilloscope. The signal should be free from spikes. A bad potentiometer will intermittently display large spikes as the potentiometer is adjusted through its full range. See Illustration 5 for an example of a bad pot. Do not confuse spikes or noise seen while the pot is not being adjusted with potentiometer noise.

    NOTE:A storage type oscilloscope, if properly used, will greatly aid in determining if a pot is excessively noisy.

    NOTE:Spikes seen riding on the baseline at regular intervals (baseline noise) are caused by switching circuits in the main power module. Do not confuse these spikes as potentiometer noise.

    Illustration 5:  Potentiometer Noise

  1. If the potentiometer fails this test, turn off and disconnect input power to the power supply and replace the pot. The Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer (46-281468P12) is 5K 10-turn and is included in Power Supply Calibration Kit 2101360.

  1. Adjust the Voltage Adjustment Potentiometer to minimum (CCW).

  1. Set the power supply Main Power and Power On Switches to “OFF."

4.5  Current Calibration and Current Potentiometer Noise Check

The external Current Shunt (2101358) should be replaced or calibrated at least once a year. If dropped, it could be forced out of tolerance and should be replaced. The resistance tolerance of the shunt is ± 0.1%. This means that the power supply output could be 750 amps ± 0.75 amps at rated current.

  • Use a Fluke 87 4.5-digit Digital Voltmeter or equivalent voltmeter (DVM).

  • Current calibration is checked under “no load" condition. Make sure the Ramp Leads are disconnected for this test.

  1. Verify that the Ramp Leads are disconnected.

  1. Plug the Oscilloscope power cable into an AC outlet and turn the Oscilloscope power switch on.

  1. Connect the P3 test connector to heater outputs J3, located on the rear of the power supply (Illustration 1).

  1. Set the Main Power and Power On Switches to “ON."

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “I" (on).

  1. Adjust the Current Adjustment and Voltage Adjustment potentiometers to minimum (full CCW).

  1. Remove the snap-on cover plate from the power supply's Current Meter.

  1. Adjust the Current Meter Zero “trim pot" on the lower left corner of the meter to 000.0 Amps. This potentiometer is located behind the snap-on cover plate removed in Step 7. See Illustration 6.

    Illustration 6:  Power Supply Current Meter Adjustment Potentiometers

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “O" (off).

  1. Set the Main Power and Power On Switches to “OFF."

  1. Connect 2 red 4/0 Positive Ramp Cables (46-260723G1) to the power supply's Positive (+) Main Terminal on the supply's rear (shown in Illustration 1) and 2 black 4/0 Negative Ramp Cables (46-260723G2) to its Negative (-) Main Terminal.

CAUTION
Potential Burn Hazard!
The power supply's high current capacity can cause heat in excess of 60W at the Ramp Cable to 1000A, 100mV Current Shunt junction if the Ramp Cables are not tightly connected to the Current Shunt.
Do not wear rings or other jewelry while performing this portion of the checkout procedure. Make sure the Ramp Cables are tightly connected before performing the following two steps.

NOTICE
Place the Current Shunt on brick or ceramic material to avoid heat damage to tile or carpet.
 
 

  1. Tightly bolt the other end of the Positive (+) Ramp Cables to one side of the 1000 amp 100mV Current Shunt. See Illustration 7.

  1. Tightly bolt the other end of the Negative (-) Ramp Cables to the other side of the 1000 amp 100mV Current Shunt. See Illustration 7.

    Illustration 7:  Current Shunt Connections

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “O" (off).

  1. Adjust the Voltage Control Potentiometer to maximum (full CW). Make sure the Current Adjustment Potentiometers are set to minimum (full CCW).

  1. Install the Meter Lead Posts, fasteners and washers to the external Current Shunt as shown in Illustration 7. Make sure the Meter Lead Post fasteners are tight.

    NOTE:Erroneous readings will result in Step 25 through Step 27 below if the Meter Lead Post fasteners are not tight. All materials used for the Meter Lead Posts, fasteners, and washers were chosen to avoid the possibility of a thermocouple action.

  1. Connect a 4.5-digit true RMS Digital Voltmeter (DVM) to the Meter Lead Posts on the Current Shunt. See Illustration 7.

  1. Set the DVM to read DC millivolts.

  1. Connect an Oscilloscope to TB2 pins 9 (+) and 7 (-).

  1. Set the Oscilloscope “Time Base" Control to 5 msec/div.

  1. Set the Oscilloscope “VOLT/DIV" Control to 5 mV/div.

  1. Set the input coupling to AC.

  1. Set the power supply Main Power and Power On Switches to “ON."

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “I" (on).

  1. Adjust the Current Adjust Fine/Coarse Controls to maximum (full CW). The external DVM should indicate a minimum of 75.0 mVDC.

    NOTE:Each millivolt increment on the DVM corresponds to 10 amps. Make sure the meter used in the TCR 7.5T750 Main Power Supply Tests procedure is capable of reading to three decimal places.

    NOTE:If this voltage is low, maximum rated current (750 amps) will not be achievable, and the power supply should be returned for repair. Include a written description of the problem when returning power supplies.

  1. Adjust the Current Adjustment Controls to obtain a reading of 75.0 mVDC on the external DVM. This corresponds to a power supply output of 750 amps.

  1. Adjust R613, located on the A600 board, to display a reading of 750 amps on the power supply's digital Current Meter.

    • If a reading of 750 amps cannot be achieved, adjust the power supply's Current Meter's “Full Scale" Trim Potentiometer for 750 amps. See Illustration 7.

    • When a reading of 750 amps is achieved, continue with Step 28.

  1. Adjust the Current Adjustment Potentiometers through the full range of operation while observing the Oscilloscope. The signal should be free from spikes. See Illustration 5 for an example of a bad pot.

  1. If either potentiometer fails this test, turn off and disconnect the power supply input power and replace the potentiometer. The Coarse Current Adjustment Potentiometer is a 5K 10 turn pot (46-281468P12). The Fine Current Adjustment Potentiometer is a 100 ohm 10 turn pot (46-281468P2). Extra potentiometers are included in the Power Supply Checkout Kit (2101360).

  1. Adjust all Current and Voltage Adjustment Potentiometers to minimum (full CCW).

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “O" (off).

  1. Set the Power On and Main Power Switches to “OFF."

  1. Reinstall the power supply's Control Panel.

4.6  Voltage Ripple Check

Typical voltage ripple values, using the TCR 7.5T750 Main Power Supply Tests procedure, are ≤ 30mV RMS. To achieve the lowest voltage ripple reading the power supply being tested must be warmed up for at least 15 minutes before checking and/or adjustment. This test is to be performed using a True RMS Voltmeter.

  1. Connect 2 red 4/0 Positive Ramp Cables (46-260723G1) to the power supply's Positive (+) Main Terminal on the supply's rear (shown in Illustration 1) and 2 black 4/0 Negative Ramp Cables (46-260723G2) to its Negative (-) Main Terminal.

  1. Connect the P3 test connector to heater outputs J3, located on the rear of the power supply.

WARNING
Potential Burn Hazard
Direct contact with joint between positive and negative ramp cables may cause skin burns.
Make sure the connection between the positive and negative ramp cables is tight to minimize resistive heat buildup.

  1. Bolt the other ends of the positive and negative ramp cables together. Make sure this connection is tight.

  1. Set a True RMS Voltmeter to its AC millivolt scale.

  1. Connect the Voltmeter to the power supply's main terminals.

  1. Set the Main Power and Power On Switches to “ON."

  1. Set the Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “I" (on).

  1. Adjust the Current Adjustment and Voltage Adjustment Potentiometers to maximum (full CW).

  1. Monitor the Voltmeter ― after at least 15 minutes to allow power supply to sufficiently warm up ― for a “voltage ripple" indication.

  1. If the voltage ripple is > 35 millivolts, send the main power supply for repair.

  1. Adjust the Voltage Adjustment and Current Adjustment Potentiometers to minimum (CCW).

  1. Set the Heater 1 Main and Heater 2 Shim Axial Switches to “I" (off).

  1. Set the power supply Main Power and Power On Switches to “OFF."
