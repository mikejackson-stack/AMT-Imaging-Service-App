Coil Shock Sensor Check

(Click Here to Return to Detailed Procedure)

1  Procedure

NOTE:Only coils manufactured and/or distributed by GE should have a shock sensor sticker, and each coil should have only one shock sensor installed. Look at the rating plate on the coil for this information. Coils not purchased through GE (E-Cats) are not included and should not have shock sensors installed.

  1. Check if the site has any of the coils listed in Table 1 or Step .

    1. Look for physical damage to the coil that could affect its functional effectiveness. Refer to the coil operator manual for more details if necessary. If there is physical damage, such as a cracked or broken piece off the coil, proceed directly to Step 6.

    1. Check the coil for a shock sensor sticker:

    (Step Details)

  1. Check if the shock sensor has been triggered (center tube turns red – right side of Illustration 1).

    (Step Details)

  1. Remove the triggered shock sensor from the coil and dispose of it using local procedures.

  1. Verbally educate the customer on safe use of coils and implications to the warranty for mishandling of coils.

  1. Requalify the coil by running the Multi-Coil Quality Assurance Tool test.

    (Step Details)

NOTICE
Remove the shock sensor before returning the damaged coil.
 
 

  1. Replace the coil according to replacement procedures. Fill in the Coil Data Collection Sheet, indicating what failed on the coil, and include a copy inside the shipping container with the damaged coil. Charge the customer for repair/replacement of the damaged coil per local procedures. Verbally educate the customer on safe use of coils and implications to the warranty for mishandling of coils.

2  Finalization

  1. Make sure the above procedure is completed for all coils at the customer site listed in Table 1 or Step . Return the coils for use to the customer.

  1. Reuse the original packaging of the coil shock sensors for transportation. Order a new box of shock sensor stickers through SSP purchasing if running low. Order a couple weeks ahead of the next PM to allow time for delivery.
