Emergency Stop Bypass Troubleshooting

(Click Here to Return to Detailed Procedure)

1  Safety

CAUTION
ELECTRICAL SHOCK HAZARD
CONTACT WITH CONNECTORS LEADING TO AN ENERGIZED POWER SUPPLY CAN CAUSE ELECTRICAL SHOCK.
FOLLOW LOCKOUT/TAGOUT PROCEDURE WHILE PERFORMING SERVICES TO ELECTRICAL CONNECTION COMPONENTS.

2  Required Conditions

ConditionReferenceEffectivity

Power to the PGR cabinet is turned off and LOTO procedures are implemented.

-

3  Procedure

3.1   System Emergency Stop Bypass Test

This test is performed to determine condition of the emergency stop circuitry in the PGR cabinet or the magnet enclosure.

  1. Verify system has been shut down and LOTO procedures are implemented for the PGR cabinet.

  1. Disconnect cable E3002 from connector J103 located on the connector panel located on the top of the of the PGR cabinet. See Illustration 3.

    (Step Details)

  1. Connect 15-pin Sub-D female jumper to J103 (see Illustration 4) on the PGR cabinet connector panel.

    (Step Details)

  1. Restore power to the PGR cabinet.

  1. Press green EMO Reset button on front of PDU to reset E-Stop circuit.

  1. Verify operation of E-Stop circuit and confirm that RF amplifier and PHPS powers on. See Illustration 5 for a flow chart of troubleshooting activities.

    (Step Details)

3.2   Auxiliary Board Bypass Test

This test is performed to determine condition of the emergency stop circuitry in the PGR cabinet.

  1. Verify system has been shut down and LOTO procedures are implemented for the PGR cabinet.

  1. Disconnect cable E3017 from connector J9 of the AUX PWB Enclosure Assembly. See Illustration 6.

    (Step Details)

  1. Connect male Sub-D jumper to cable (see Illustration 7) E3017 from connector J9 (disconnected from the AUX board enclosure).

    (Step Details)

  1. Restore power to the PGR cabinet.

  1. Press green EMO Reset button on front of PDU to reset E-Stop circuit.

  1. Verify operation of E-Stop circuit and confirm that RF amplifier and PHPS powers on. See Illustration 5 for flow chart of troubleshooting activities.

    (Step Details)

3.3   Resistance Test

This test is performed to determine condition of the emergency stop circuit.

  1. Using a multimeter, check that the resistance between pins 7 and 15 of cable E3002 (disconnected from J103 on the connector panel located on the top of the PGR cabinet) is 105 kΩ or less.

    (Step Details)

  1. Using a multimeter, verify the resistance between pins 1 and 20 of cable M3302 (disconnected from connector J40 on the PEN panel) is 105 kΩ or less.

    (Step Details)

  1. If system is equipped with the trackball option, verify that cable M3337 is connected to J11 in the Trackball Control Panel board. On the back of the control panel, the E-stop cable connection and the Trackball cable connection are of the same size and gender, and thus can be accidentally interchanged. Confirm that the cables are connected properly, otherwise the E-stop circuit will not be resettable. See Illustration 8. If system does not have the trackball option, verify that cable E3349 connects to J4 on the 7-segment display panel. If connections are correct, proceed to Step 4.

    (Step Details)

  1. Using the block diagram as a guide (see Illustration 9), start at J40 of the PEN panel and work your way through the emergency stop circuit to each of the emergency stop enclosures looking for the open circuit.

    (Step Details)

  1. Using a multimeter check continuity of emergency stop paths between J9, J11, and J40 connectors on PHPS. Refer to complete E-Stop diagrams located in Power section of System Interactive Block Diagrams for relevant connector pins.

    (Step Details)

  1. Using a multimeter, check continuity of emergency stop connections through the Patient Handling Power Supply.

    (Step Details)
