3  Procedure

3.1   Ground Resistance Checks Using Microguard

  1. Notify all Field Service Engineers working at the site that the Main Disconnect is being shut off, locked out, and tagged out.

  1. Locate and shut off the Main Disconnect which supplies power to the Power Distribution Unit (PDU) and Heat Exchanger Cabinet (HEC) .

  1. Check that the Main Disconnect and PDU indicator lights are off and that the power switches are inactive.

  1. With a digital voltmeter (DVM) check that all energy has dissipated.

  1. Plug the milliohm adaptor module into the power outlet of the Microguard (see Illustration 1).

    (Step Details)

  1. Attach the Microguard red and black leads to the meter posts of the milliohm module.

  1. Set the Microguard Patient Lead Tester switch to All Other Tests.

  1. Set the Microguard Selector switch to 20 Microamps/Millivolts.

  1. Set the Microguard Input switch to Red and Black Probes.

  1. Attach the Kelvin leads. Connect one lead to the PDU ground bus bar and the other lead to the frame of the subsystem cabinet under test (see Illustration 1).

  1. The meter reads voltage across ground resistance in millivolts. To obtain the resistance in milliohms, multiply the meter reading by 2.

  1. Verify that the measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms (corresponding to a meter reading of 50 millivolts). If this fails, add an additional ground wire from the failing cabinet to the central ground stud.

  1. Perform Step 10 through Step 12 for each subsystem cabinet.

  1. Restore the system:

    (Step Details)

3.2   Ground Resistance Checks Using Dale (For Those sites equipped with a Dale 600, 600E, 601, or 601E Safety Analyzer)

  1. Notify all Field Service Engineers working at the site that the Main Disconnect is being shut off, locked out, and tagged out.

  1. Locate and shut off the Main Disconnect which supplies power to the PDU and HEC.

  1. Check that the Main Disconnect and PDU indicator lights are off and that the power switches are inactive.

  1. With a DVM, check that all energy has dissipated.

  1. Connect one clamp-type lead to the EXTERNAL jack on the top of the Dale safety analyzer. Connect the other clamp-type lead to the CHASSIS jack on the top of the Dale safety analyzer (see Illustration 2).

    (Step Details)

  1. On the Dale safety analyzer, set the main selector switch to W - RESISTANCE (see Illustration 3).

    (Step Details)

  1. Plug the Dale safety analyzer into an AC outlet.

  1. On the Dale safety analyzer, verify that the two OKLEDs (light emitting diodes) light up to indicate the AC outlet is properly wired (see Illustration 3).

  1. With the leads not touching each other, the display should read 1.00. With the leads touching (copper to copper), the display should read 0.00 to 0.02.

  1. Attach the leads. Connect one lead to the PDU ground bus bar and the other lead to the bare frame of the subsystem cabinet under test (see Illustration 4).

    (Step Details)

  1. Check that the measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms. If this fails, add an additional ground wire from the failing cabinet to the central ground stud.

  1. Perform Step 10 through Step 11 for each subsystem cabinet.

  1. Restore the system:

    1. Unplug the Dale safety analyzer from the AC outlet.

    1. Remove the leads from the Dale safety analyzer.

    1. Close all subsystem cabinets and the PGR cabinet.

4  Finalization

  1. Perform a Check Scan.

  1. Return to the main procedure (Grounding and Leakage Current Checks) or proceed to the Ground Leakage Current Test.
