⚠️ GE MRI Spatial Gradient (SG) Reference
AMT Imaging Solutions — Implant Safety Quick Reference

Safety Note: Spatial gradient (dB/dZ) is the rate of change of the static magnetic field with distance from isocenter. High spatial gradient creates translational force on ferromagnetic objects. Use this reference to assess safe entry zones for patients with implants, devices, or metallic foreign bodies.

What Is Spatial Gradient?

Spatial gradient (SG or dB/dZ) is measured in Gauss/cm (G/cm). It is highest near the bore entrance, not at isocenter. SG is what pulls ferromagnetic objects — the stronger the spatial gradient, the greater the translational force. Most implant safety specifications reference a maximum allowable SG for safe use.

General Spatial Gradient Rules

RuleDetail
3T vs 1.5T3T systems have approximately 2× the spatial gradient of equivalent 1.5T systems at the same bore position. Implant safety limits are more restrictive at 3T.
70 cm vs 60 cm boreWide-bore (70 cm) systems have approximately 20% higher SG at the bore wall compared to standard 60 cm bore systems at the same field strength. The additional space means the gradient drops off over a greater distance.
Peak SG locationSpatial gradient is highest near the bore entrance ends, not at isocenter. Isocenter (magnet center) has the lowest SG. The peak occurs in the fringe field region — typically 20–40 cm outside the bore face.
Safe cylinder conceptFor implant screening, assess whether the implant will pass through high-SG zones during patient entry. Keeping the implant within the central cylinder of the bore (smaller diameter) limits peak SG exposure during entry.
Implant labelingMR Conditional implants specify a maximum dB/dZ in G/cm. Compare this to the system's SG at the relevant bore position. If system SG exceeds implant limit at any point during patient entry — do not scan.

Implant Safety Screening Checklist

StepAction
1. Identify the implantGet exact model, manufacturer, and version from patient records, device card, or operative report. Manufacturer name + model number are essential.
2. Verify MR labelingLook up in MRI manufacturer database (GE MedBase, Medtronic IMD Ref, MHRA, or www.mrisafety.com). Device must be labeled MR Safe or MR Conditional — not MR Unsafe.
3. Check conditional conditionsMR Conditional devices specify: field strength, SAR limit, scan time limit, and maximum spatial gradient (G/cm). All conditions must be met simultaneously.
4. Compare SG to system valuesUse tables above to verify that peak SG the patient/implant will experience during entry does not exceed the implant's labeled maximum SG.
5. Physicist or radiologist sign-offFor complex or uncertain cases, require MR physicist or radiologist to review and approve before scanning. Document all screening decisions.
Tip: The most common implant safety error is checking only the field strength (1.5T vs 3T) and missing the spatial gradient condition. A device labeled "MR Conditional at 1.5T with dB/dZ ≤ 200 G/cm" cannot be used at 1.5T on an MR450w because the bore entrance SG is 293 G/cm — exceeding the implant limit even though the field strength matches.
Tip: When a site is upgrading from a 60 cm to 70 cm bore system (e.g., MR450 to MR450w), their existing implant screening protocols must be reviewed. The wider bore has a meaningfully higher minimum entry SG, which can affect devices that were previously cleared.
