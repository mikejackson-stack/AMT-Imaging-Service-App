2  Overview

This procedure describes the removal and replacement of the Twin Resonator Module (TRM) in systems with LCC and WideOpen Enclosure Magnets. The TRM uses the BRM-D parts for installation/removal.

Required Tools:

  • Refer to TRM LCC Gradient ISO Installation Kit for the tools required from that kit.

  • Refer to Nonmagnetic Tools for a list of the nonmagnetic tools.

3.3  Safety

WARNING
Personal injury or equipment damage may result if taken too close to the magnet.
The jack on the Tube Jacking Assembly is ferrous.
Keep as far away from magnet bore as possible when removing from magnet room. Always keep the tube jacking assembly mounted and screwed to the Aluminum coil cradle when used in the magnet room.

WARNING
Personal injury and equipment damage!
Strong Magnetic Field.
When servicing any magnetic equipment, it is critically important that the service engineer consciously plan the path to be taken when moving highly ferrous devices in the magnet environment. the path should be as far from the magnet as practical and avoid high flux density fields.

Safety Requirements:

  • Movement of ferrous material in the magnet room must follow the GE service procedure for that device. When exiting, move away from the magnet in the most direct manner possible. Except when moving ferrous material to and from its native location on or near the magnet, the static magnetic field in any portion of the service path shall not exceed 200 Gauss.

  • Two (2) MR safety trained personnel must be present at all times when servicing highly ferrous devices in the areas of magnetic fields.

When planning a service path, it is critical that the path be clear and sufficiently wide. Ensure that there are no trip hazards, obstacles, clutter, slippery surfaces or other items even partially restricting the path. If there are portable obstacles in a path, remove them from the area and replace them after the service action is completed. It is required to walk the path prior to beginning service to ensure that there is sufficient space through which to pass for yourself and the object being serviced.

CAUTION
Dangerous Work Area!
Use of heavy equipment for coil removal and replacement presents hazards to all personnel in the area.

  • Set up restricted barriers and signs limiting access to the work area.

  • Notify all affected personnel in the area prior to starting the service activity. Review safety procedures and restricted areas for the job.

CAUTION
Personal Injury or Equipment Damage!
Either could result if the brakes are not properly set on the cart.
Make sure to watch and guide all cables attached to the Gradient Coil during the removal process. Damage to cables or Gradient Coil may occur if a cable is pinched, cut or snagged.

CAUTION
Personal Injury or Tool Damage!
Watch for tube deflection when adjusting the jacking screws.
Any tube deflection indicates the Gradient Coil is in contact with the warm bore, thereby resisting free motion.

CAUTION
Personal Injury or Equipment Damage!
Make sure the weight of the Gradient Coil has transferred from the tube to the cradle/cart at the appropriate place in this procedure.
Personal injury or equipment damage may result if the load was not transferred.

4  Procedure

4.1  Lock-Out/Tag-Out

Perform LOTO for appropriate PDU:

  • Lockout / Tagout for Gradient Amplifier / PDU or PHOENIX Power Distribution Unit (Lockout/Tagout).

4.2  Using Gradient Insertion Tool

Changes were made to the Gradient Insertion Tool (P/N 2164744-6 and greater). Use the parts described below when replacing either the BRM or TRM.

  1. Attach the Mounting Plates (P/N 5161983 and 5161985) to either the BRM or TRM Gradient Coil, and secure them to the Gradient Coil using the M10 x 120 studs. Thread the studs (located as shown below) at least 1/4 inch (6 mm) into the Gradient Coil.

    NOTE:The CRM Gradient Coil use two separate Mounting Plates (P/N 2187590). When inserting studs, make sure they are inserted 3/8 inch (12 mm).

    Illustration 1:  Gradient Mounting Plates

  1. Attach the Rear Plate to the magnet as shown, and secure it to the magnet using M10 x 80 mm studs, washers and M10 nut. (There are standoffs on this plate that should remain in place.)

    Illustration 2:  Attaching Rear Plate to Magnet

  1. Retrieve the Male Insertion Tube (2284929) and XRMB Tube Standoff (5191626).

    1. Attach the Tube Standoff to the Male Insertion Tube using the 4 inch, 0.375-16 UNC Hex Socket Screw (5303993) included in the Gradient Coil Insertion and Lift Kit.

    1. Once the Standoff is secured to the Tube, attach the Tube Pilot Shaft to the Standoff as shown.

      Illustration 3:  Attaching Standoff to Insertion Tube

  1. Proceed to the next section.

4.3  Gradient Coil Replacement

  1. Remove the bridge and the front and rear endbells.

  1. Disconnect the Water Chiller as described below.

    Illustration 4:  Disconnect Water Chiller

  1. Drain the coolant from the Body Coil and coolant hoses as described below.

    Illustration 5:  Drain Coolant from Body Coil and Coolant Hoses

  1. Remove the two Tube Guide Roller Assemblies from the crate as shown. (It may be necessary to exchange the rollers to the appropriate Plate Assembly; the TRM uses the BRM-D plates shown in Illustration 7.)

    Illustration 6:  Tube Guide Roller Assembly

    Illustration 7:  BRM-D Plate Assembly Installed on Patient End

    1. Install the Tube Guide Roller Assembly onto the patient end of the Gradient Coil shown above. (Each assembly requires four bolts and one stud.)

    1. Use the two M10 x 120 studs from the kit, and thread them at least 1.4 inch (6 mm) into the top hole location on the Gradient Coil. (The studs will support the Tube Guide Roller Assembly and assist the alignment of the remaining bolts.)

      NOTE:When using the BRM-D Plate Assemblies, the M10 x 120 mm studs must be used.

    1. Use the M10 x 80 mm bolts from the kit and a 17 mm socket and ratchet to attach to the studs.

  1. Remove the Tube Support Plate Assembly from the crate as shown.

    Illustration 8:  Tube Support Plate Assembly

  1. Adjust the Alignment Bearing to center position (as shown) as viewed from the side with the round cut-out.

    Illustration 9:  Alignment Bearing to Center Position

    Adjustment of the Alignment Bearing:

    • For the vertical adjustment, a clockwise rotation results in the alignment bearing moving down. Use an adjustable wrench for the vertical adjustment.

    • For the horizontal adjustment, a clockwise rotation results in the alignment bearing moving to the left. Use the 3/8 inch (12 mm) T-bar Allen wrench.

NOTICE
Do NOT apply force to any stud or bolt used to secure the insertion tool hardware to the magnet Interface Ring or Gradient Coil. The potential exists for a stud or bolt to gall the threads on these parts.
 
 

NOTE:If the magnet has the new Black Turtle Bracket shown below, spacers must be used between the Turtle Bracket and the Tube Support Plate.

  • If a Black Turtle Bracket is present, perform Step 7.a through Step 7.d.

  • If a Metallic Support Ring is present, perform Step 8 through Step 8.g.

    Illustration 10:  Black Turtle Bracket

  1. Go to the rear of the magnet and install the Tube Support Plate onto the magnet Interface Rings as follows:

    1. Remove screws from the Turtle Bracket so the Tube Support Plate can be installed as shown above.

    1. Install four of the M10 x 60 studs into the Turtle Bracket openings where the screws were removed. Apply Never Seeze (P/N 46-294151P8) to the studs that will thread into the magnet.

    NOTICE
    Bushings and washers must be used, or damage could occur.
     
     

    1. Install the washer and bushing onto the studs as shown below.

      Illustration 11:  Installation of Bushing and Washer

    1. Proceed to Step 8.d.

  1. For magnets with a Metallic Support Ring, go to the rear of the magnet and install the Tube Support Plate onto the magnet Interface Ring as follows:

    1. Remove the bolts (shown below) from the Interface Ring at the rear of the magnet so the Tube Support Plate can be installed.

      Illustration 12:  Location of Bolts on Interface Ring

    1. Install four of the M10 x 60 studs into the Interface Ring openings as shown, and apply Never Seeze (P/N 46-294151P8) to the studs that will thread into the magnet.

      Illustration 13:  Placement of Stud on Interface Ring

    NOTICE
    Bushing must be used, or damage could occur.
     
     

    1. Place the bushing on the stud a shown.

      Illustration 14:  Placing Bushing on Stud

    1. Tighten the stud that will support the weight of the Tube Support Plate Assembly as shown.

      Illustration 15:  Tightening Stud

    1. Position the Tube Support Plate Assembly to the ear of the magnet as shown.

      Illustration 16:  Tube Support Plate Assembly on Rear of Magnet

    1. Place the washer and nut on each stud from the Plate Assembly (as shown below) and secure the plate.

      Illustration 17:  Placing Washer and Nut on Stud

    1. Adjust the Alignment Bearing to the center position as viewed from the side with the square cut-out as shown below.

      Illustration 18:  Alignment Bearing to Center Position

  1. Remove the Male Tube Assembly from the crate, and remove the cotter pin from the shaft.

    NOTICE
    Be careful not to force the shaft through the alignment bearing. Make vertical or horizontal adjustments to bring the alignment bearing in alignment with the tube shaft.
     
     

    1. Slowly install the tube (shaft end first) through the front Tube Guide Roller Assembly on the patient end, and guide the shaft through the alignment bearing that is located on the Tube Support Plate on the Service end of the magnet.

    1. View the video for tube insertion of the BRM to become familiar with the process.

  1. Install the cotter pin after the shaft is in place.

WARNING
Personal injury or equipment damage may result if taken too close to the magnet.
The jack on the Tube Jacking Assembly is ferrous.
Always keep the Tube Jacking Assembly mounted and screwed to the aluminum coil cradle when used in the magnet room.

  1. Remove the Tube Jacking Assembly (shown below) from the crate.

    Illustration 19:  Tube Jacking Assembly

  1. Install the Tube Jacking Assembly onto cradle/cart, and secure with the bolt through Tube Jacking Assembly baseplate as shown below.

    Illustration 20:  Secure Tube Jacking Assembly

  1. Remove the Gradient Coil cradle fasteners, two per side, from the cradle as shown below.

    Illustration 21:  Remove Gradient Coil Cradle Fasteners

  1. Move the empty cradle/cart assembly into the Magnet Room, and position the cart in front of the magnet, centering the cart left to right with respect to the patient bore.

    1. Make sure the Tube Jack Assembly is physically centered and the left/right adjustments are at a nominal setting before starting the next step.

    1. Use a 5/16 inch right-angle Allen wrench to tighten the Tube Jack Assembly to the cart.

  1. Remove the Female Tube Assembly from the crate.

    1. Support the Female Tube Assembly on the Tube Jack Assembly, and thread it onto the Male Tube Assembly as shown below.

    1. Do not overtighten the two poles, as it may be difficult to separate after coil replacement.

  1. Illustration 22:  Tube Assembly in Tube Guide Roller Assembly

  1. Use a 19 mm socket to adjust the cart height (shown below) so the end of the cradle is the same height as the warm bore of the magnet and flush against the end flange as shown in Illustration 24.

    Illustration 23:  Tool to Adjust Cart Height

    Illustration 24:  Adjusting Cart Height to Warm Bore Height

  1. Release the hand lever on the cart handle to set the brakes so the cart will not move after the cart is aligned to the magnet bore.

NOTICE
For the next two steps, the coil is still attached to the magnet interface rings of the magnet with the gradient isolation brackets.

  • Do NOT attempt to raise the coil with the jack. These steps prepare the roller assemblies to take the Gradient Coil weight when transferred from the magnet to the rollers.

  • Do NOT attempt to loosen any of the bracket bolts when the Gradient Coil is supported by the tool. Damage to the bolts will result.

 
 

  1. Operate the jack to raise the tube, and watch for the tube to make contact with the upper roller on the front Tube Guide Roller Assembly. Make sure there is a small amount of resistance to the upward movement of the tube. (The top roller should not be able to rotate.)

  1. Go to the rear of the magnet. Adjust the Alignment Bearing in the vertical direction, and watch for the tube to make contact with the upper roller on the rear Tube Guide Roller Assembly. Continue to raise the Alignment Bearing until the back end of the Gradient Coil starts to rise off of the cradle.

NOTE:When this happens, the load at the back end of the Gradient Coil is transferred from the cradle to the Support Tube and magnet.

  1. Back out the two (one on each side) radial supports, and remove the mounting pads from the upper portion on the front of the Coil. See illustration below.

    Illustration 25:  Radial Support and Mounting Pad Location

  1. Remove the four screws shown below, and remove the Gradient Support Bracket from both ends of the magnet.

    Illustration 26:  Gradient Bracket Screw Location

    NOTE:If a Black Turtle Bracket is used, the Gradient Support Bracket mounts to the magnet not the Turtle Bracket. The illustration below shows the proper mounting method of the Gradient Support Bracket for a system with Black Turtle Bracket.

    Illustration 27:  Gradient Support Bracket for Systems with Black Turtle Bracket

NOTE:If using a newer Gradient Insertion Tool (P/N 2164744-6 or greater), the Gradient Coil Roller Assemblies are not needed as the Jack Assembly is thinner.

  1. Remove the two Gradient Coil Roller Assemblies (shown below) from the tool crate.

    Illustration 28:  Gradient Coil Roller Assemblies

  1. Remove the two screws (shown below) that attach the Water Manifold block to the inner coil face.

    Illustration 29:  Location of Screws for Water Manifold

  1. Place the Roller Assemblies on the coil as shown below, one on each side at the Patient end.

    Illustration 30:  Roller Assembly on Coil

  1. Check the tube with a level on top of Female Tube Assembly. Make any adjustments with either the jack in the front or the Alignment Bearing in the rear.

  1. At the front of the magnet: Operate the jack to raise the tube and watch for the Gradient Coil bracket to raise up off of the end flange bracket. (A gap of 1/8 inch or 2 mm is sufficient.) Make sure there is no contact with the warm bore or in bore passive shims.

  1. Go to the rear of the magnet. Adjust the Alignment Bearing in the vertical direction, and watch for the Gradient Coil bracket to raise up off of the end flange bracket. (A gap of 1/8 inch or 2 mm is sufficient.) Make sure there is no contact with the warm bore or in bore passive shims.

  1. Go to the front of the magnet. Verify there is sufficient clearance all around the Gradient Coil for removal onto the cradle and cart.

CAUTION
Personal injury or equipment damage may result if the brakes are not set properly.
Make sure the brakes are set on the cart before performing the next step.
Make sure to watch and guide all cables attached to the BRM during the removal process. Damage to cables or Gradient Coil may occur if a cable is pinched, cut or snagged.

NOTICE
The following step requires three FEs: one in the rear to push the coil and guide the cables through the bore and two in front to pull on the coil.
 
 

  1. Slowly pull the Gradient Coil forward on the tube, and watch the clearance around the coil to make sure it is concentric and level with the bore.

NOTE:Minimize the rotation of the coil on the tube. Damage to the roller assemblies could result.

  1. Make sure the roller brackets are in line with the shipping bracket holes on the cradle. (This alignment is necessary for installing the shipping blocks later in the procedure.)

CAUTION
Personal injury or damage to the tool could result.
Watch for tube deflection when adjusting the jacking screws in the next step.
Any tube deflection indicates the Gradient Coil is in contact with the warm bore, thereby resisting free motion.

  1. Slowly lower the jack to allow the Gradient Coil weight to transfer to the Gradient Coil rollers.

WARNING
Personal injury or equipment damage may result if taken too close to the magnet.
The jack on the Tube Jacking Assembly is ferrous.
Keep as far away from magnet bore as possible when removing from magnet room.

  1. Remove the bolt from the Tube Jack Assembly baseplate; remove the jack and take it out of the Magnet Room. (The cradle is now clear to allow the Gradient Coil to move forward on the cart.)

  1. With three FEs pulling from the front, move the Gradient Coil over the cradle so it is centered from front to back on the cradle.

NOTE:It may be necessary to lower the cart to allow the back end of the Gradient Coil to clear the cradle.

  1. Important: Make sure the shipping bracket holes for the gradient coil (shown below) will line up with the holes on the cradle of the cart before removing the rollers. You may have to rotate the Gradient Coil and/or move forward or back on the cradle to align the holes.

    • The BRM, BRM-D, and CRM each have unique holes in the cart for shipping, be sure to align the TRM to the “B” holes on the shipping cart.

    • See Installing the TRM, Illustration 13 for a picture of the installed shipping bracket.

    Illustration 31:  Shipping Cart Bracket Hole Locations

  1. Slowly lower the back end of the Gradient Coil onto the cradle by lowering the alignment bearing at the back end of the magnet.

CAUTION
Personal Injury or Equipment Damage!
Make sure the weight of the Gradient Coil has transferred from the tube to the cradle/cart before proceeding to the next step.
Personal injury or equipment damage may result if the load was not transferred.

  1. Adjust the jacking screws on the Roller Block for wheel removal, remove the rollers and pins, then adjust the jacking screws to lower the Gradient Coil to the cradle.

  1. Remove the front and rear spacers on the gradient coil and make a note to avoid interchanging them since the front spacer must go on the front location of the replacement coil as shown below.

    Illustration 32:  Spacer Location

  1. Remove the 15 mm spacer by removing the screw from the 90° hole as shown.

    Illustration 33:  Removing Screw from 90° Hole

  1. Remove the Gradient Coil Roller assemblies shown below.

    Illustration 34:  Roller Assembly on Coil

  1. Replace the two screws (shown below) that attach the water manifold block to the inner coil face.

    Illustration 35:  Location of Screws for Water Manifold

  1. Get the rope supplied in the kit, and route it under the support tube between the end flange of the magnet and the Gradient Coil.

    1. Attach the rope the enclosure frame brackets on the Cryostat.

    1. Adjust the height of the rope so it will provide support for the tube during the disassembly process.

  1. Three FEs are required and should be positioned as described for the following step:

    • First FE is in the rear of the magnet to remove the cotter pin and to support the tube as it is removed from the Alignment Bearing.

    • Second FE is between the coil cart and the magnet to unthread the tube sections as the first FE supports the back part of the section in the bore after the shaft is removed from the Alignment Bearing.

    • Third FE is in the front of the coil cart and pulls on the cart after the first FE removes the cotter pin, pulling just enough for the shaft of the tube to pull free from the Alignment Bearing.

  1. Unthread the tube leaving one section in the bore (supported by the rope and Tube Support Plate Assembly) and leave the other section in the Gradient Coil on the cart.

  1. Release the brake on the cart, and slowly back the cart with the Gradient Coil out of the Magnet Room.
