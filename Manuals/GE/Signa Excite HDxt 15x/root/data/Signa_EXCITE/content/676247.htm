1.5T Phased Array Shoulder Coil Troubleshooting

1  Personnel Requirements

Required PersonsProcedure
130 min.

2  Overview

The following tips can be used to troubleshoot common problems with the 1.5T HD 3-Channel Shoulder Array Coil by General Electric.

3  Preliminary Requirements

3.1  Tools and Test Equipment

The following tools and test equipment are required to troubleshoot:

  • Digital Volt Meter (DVM)

  • Phantom, P/N 2359877

  • Phantom Positioner, P/N U1-150412

3.2  Replacement Parts

Some tests may require FRUs for diagnosis.

3.3  Required Conditions

The following coil configuration name must be installed to run the MCQA tool: GE_HDx Shoulder

4  Procedure

4.1  Receiving No Signal

Problem:

Unable to pre-scan or scanning and not receiving a signal.

Possible Solution:

  • Verify the green light above Port A is illuminated.

    NOTE:This coil is only compatible with Port A with system pre-amplifiers (HDx Forward production systems). The green LED indicates the coil is properly plugged into the system.

  • Verify the system has correctly detected the coil by selecting Currently Connected on the Coil Selection screen (example shown below) to display the coil(s) prescribed.

Illustration 1:  Coil Selection Screen

  • Verify the landmark is correct. Refer to Shoulder Coil Setup for Coil MCQA for landmarking instructions.

  • Verify the scan locations and any Field of View (FOV) offsets are correct.

  • Perform a Continuity Check on the output cable ( Step ).The continuity check must be performed by a GE authorized Service Engineer.

  • Verify the coil is positioned with the cable exiting toward the bore.

  • Verify the cable is not looped or crossed.

If still not obtaining a signal, try to scan (transmit and receive) with the body coil. For this test, be sure to remove the imaging coil from the magnet bore before scanning with the body coil. If still receiving no signal, the problem is likely with the MR system.

If the scan completes successfully, there could be a problem with the 1.5T 3-Channel Shoulder Coil. Contact GE for further assistance. If unable to scan with the substitute coil, there may be a system problem related to this particular coil type.

4.2   Image Quality

Problem:

Poor image quality, shaded images, or MCQA fails.

Possible Solution:

  • Perform a Continuity Check on the output cable ( Step ). The continuity check must be performed by a GE authorized Service Engineer.

  • Verify there are no loops in the cables.

  • Verify there are no metal or ferromagnetic objects close to the coil, patient or magnet (such as a safety pin, hair pin).

  • Verify the coil is properly positioned.

  • Verify the center frequency is within the frequency adjustment range for the system.

  • Verify the R1, R2 and TG values from the pre-scan are within normally expected ranges.

If not already done, perform the coil Quality Assurance Test (found in the Operator Manual). If the values obtained do not fall within normal operating parameters, investigate further by performing a phantom scan with the body coil. For this test, remove the imaging coil from the magnet bore before scanning with the body coil.

If still experiencing the same problems, there is probably an MR system issue. If the body coil scan is satisfactory, acquire a scan using another coil of the exact same type (receive-only, phased array, nonintegrated preamps) and the same system coil selection. If the image quality is visibly improved, there may be a problem with the 3-Channel Shoulder Coil. Contact GE for further assistance. If the image quality still suffers, there may be a system problem related to imaging with this type of coil.

4.3  Artifacts

Problem:

There is a black line or signal void on the image.

Possible Solution:

Verify there is no metal present in the area being scanned or on the patient.

4.4  Output Cable Check

4.4.1  Visual Check

Before removing the cable assembly from the coil, visually inspect all coaxial connectors and pins on the coil connector. Illustration 2 shows the pin layouts of the coil connector. The connector of the Shoulder Coil has two pins (A1 and A2) in column A, five pins (J4, J5, J6, J9 and J10) in column J, five pins (K4, K5, K6, K9 and K10) in column K, four pins (M7, M8, M9 and M10) in column M, and three RF coaxial connectors in C4, D1 and D2. If there are any broken, deformed or recessed pins or coaxial connectors, perform Cable Replacement.

NOTICE
Under NO circumstances should the continuity of the center pin of the coaxial connectors be measured. They are extremely fragile when improper tools are used.
 
 

NOTE:There are two connectors in the dual connector coils.

Illustration 2:  Pin Layouts of Hypertronics Connector

4.4.2  Check for Continuity of DC Lines in External Cable

  1. Remove the cable assembly from the coil as described in Cable Replacement.

  2. There are three pins in column J (J4, J5, J6 shown in the illustration above) connecting to DC wires of the cable. Table 1 shows these pins and corresponding DC wires. Use a DVM to check the resistance between the pin in column J and the corresponding DC wire (shown in Illustration 3). The resistance should be 18.0, ±2.0 ohms. If any resistance reading is not within this range, perform Cable Replacement.
