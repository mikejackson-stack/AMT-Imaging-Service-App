3.4  Safety

DANGER
Strong Magnetic Field!
Ferrous materials can become dangerous projectiles in the presence of the magnetic field Produced by the Magnet.
Do not Bring any ferromagnetic tools or equipment into the magnet room.

CAUTION
Dangerous Work Area!
Use of heavy equipment for coil removal and replacement presents hazards to all personnel in the area.

  • Set up restricted barriers and signs limiting access to the work area.

  • Notify all effected personnel in the area prior to starting the service activity. Review safety procedures and restricted areas for the job.

4  Procedure

  1. Follow Lockout/Tagout procedures per LOTO FOR XRFD Amp and PEN Cabinet document.

  1. Remove Split Bridge. See Bridge Removal and Replacement document for details.

  1. Remove the Front End Bell. See Front End Bell Removal and Install document for details.

  1. Disconnect the two (2) air hoses at the rear end of the body coil.

  1. Disconnect the two (2) drive cables (I & Q) at the rear end of the body coil. Disconnect the DC bias line at the rear, and disconnect the Hart ID cable and DC Bias line at the front of the body coil.

    NOTE:The rear end bell does not need to be removed for RF body coil replacement.

NOTICE
Do not use the air hose manifolds to remove the RF body coil!
 
 

  1. Partially slide out the old RF Body Coil. Disconnect the Bore Temperature Sensor and tape to the magnet enclosure so it is out of the way (see Illustration 1).

    Illustration 1:  Bore Temperature Sensor cable

NOTICE
If there is no FRU case available, place the body coil on two pads, making sure they protect the drive cables of the body coil from damage. Make sure the body coil does not roll over.
 
 

  1. Fully remove the coil and place it inside the empty top half of the FRU case. See Illustration 2.

    Illustration 2:  Remove Coil

  1. Before installing the RF Body coil, attach one of the foam strips at the back bottom edge of the body coil ( the 6 o clock position where the gradient coil does not have an acoustic barrier).

  1. Slide in the new RF body coil. The blue drive cables face the rear end. See Illustration 3. Align the mounting pads on the body to the centering strips inside the gradient coil.

    Illustration 3:  Slide in RF Body Coil

  1. Before sliding RF Body Coil in all the way, reconnect the Bore Temperature Sensor to the coil (see Bore Temperature and Room Ambient Sensor Replacements).

NOTICE
Do not use the air hose manifolds to install the RF body coil!
 
 

  1. Align the blue drive cables in the rear so the middle of the two drive cables are as close to the 6 o'clock position as visually possible. Make sure the blue drive cables and the DC bias cable are routed through the cut-outs on the rear end bell. See Illustration 4.

    Illustration 4:  Center RF Body Coil

  1. Press the RF Body Coil so there is a 2.0 to 2.5 mm gap between the coil and the Rear End Bell.

NOTICE
For optimal performance of the system, the I&Q cables must be spaced a minimum of 75mm (3 inches) from cables exiting the cable tracks from the LPCA and 30mm (1.2 inches) from the Temperature Sensor cable routed from the gradient coil. See Illustration 5.
 
 

  1. Reconnect the I&Q lines, the DD Bias lines, and the Hart ID cable.

    Illustration 5:  I&Q Line Spacing

  1. Reconnect the two air hoses at the rear end of the body coil.

  1. Attach the remaining yellow foam strip at the bottom front of the body coil (the 6 o clock position where the gradient coil does not have an acoustic barrier).

  1. Replace the front end bell. Refer to Front End Bell Removal and Install. There should be a 2.0 to 2.5 mm gap between the front end bell and the RF body coil.

  1. Replace the split bridge. Refer to Bridge Removal and Replacement.

  1. Remove LOTO.

5  Finalization

  1. Run the RF Body Coil Field Tuning Procedure. If in Europe or Asia, contact the RF Body Coil tuning expert to perform this step. Otherwise, contact Florence to perform this step.

  1. Run the TR Dynamic Disable Calibration.

  1. Run the System Gain Calibration.

  1. Run the Echo Planar Test (EPT).

  1. Perform a quick head and body scan to ensure the system is functioning properly.
