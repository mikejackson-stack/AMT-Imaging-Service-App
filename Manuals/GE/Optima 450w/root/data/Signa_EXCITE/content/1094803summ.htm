1  Safety

DANGER
STRONG MAGNETIC FIELD!
FERROUS MATERIALS CAN BECOME DANGEROUS PROJECTILES IN THE PRESENCE OF THE MAGNETIC FIELD PRODUCED BY THE MAGNET.
DO NOT BRING ANY FERROMAGNETIC TOOLS OR EQUIPMENT INTO THE MAGNET ROOM.

CAUTION
ELECTRICAL SHOCK HAZARD!
CONTACT WITH CONNECTORS LEADING TO AN ENERGIZED POWER SUPPLY CAN CAUSE ELECTRICAL SHOCK.
FOLLOW LOCKOUT/TAGOUT PROCEDURES WHILE PERFORMING SERVICES TO ELECTRICAL CONNECTION COMPONENTS.

CAUTION
Electrical Shock Hazard!
Coolant circulating through the gradient coil is in contact with live electrical conductors. Using wrong type of coolant may result in electrical shock.
Always use coolant specified by DVw gradient coil service procedure.

CAUTION
Dangerous Work Area!
Use of heavy equipment for coil removal and replacement presents hazards to all personnel in the area.

  • Set up restricted barriers and signs limiting access to the work area.

  • Notify all effected personnel in the area prior to starting the service activity.

  • Review safety procedures and restricted areas for the job.

  • Personal Protective Equipment should be worn at all times.

  • Refer to Material Handling SRA before starting Coil replacement.

3  Procedure

3.1   Phase 1 Coolant Water Removal

NOTE:Nitrile Gloves must be worn when performing coolant removal.

  1. Turn off PVC Valves in coolant supply and return lines at the service-end of the XRMw Coil.

  1. Place an empty five gallon bucket close to the service-end to accept discharge coolant. (See Illustration 1.)

    Illustration 1:  Draining Manifold Coolant

  1. Remove the phenolic clamp that secures the manifold extension to the magnet.

  1. Disconnect the coolant inlet and outlet lines from the XRMw Coil. (See Illustration 1.)

  1. Attach drain hose to the water return hose on the XRMw coil.

  1. Connect pump air hose to the water supply line on the XRMw coil.

  1. Pump the air pump and observe the coolant draining from the XRMw coil. Continue to drain coolant from the XRMw coil until no significant flow appears at the outlet draining into the container. This should take about five minutes.

  1. Follow customers procedure for proper disposal of coolant.

3.2   Phase 2 Coolant Water Removal

There are a total of 14 cooling circuits; six inner bore cooling circuit, six Z inner cooling circuits, and two Z outer cooling circuits. All 14 circuits must be cleared.

NOTE:Nitrile Gloves must be worn when performing coolant removal.

NOTE:There are six inner bore cooling pipes that need to be cleared.

  1. Remove XRMw Manifolds. (See XRMw Manifold Replacement .)

NOTE:It is preferred that coolant be removed from the XRMw coil outside of the scan room. However, coolant can be removed in the scan room when another location is not available.

  1. Connect the air pump to inlet 1i of the inner bore cooling circuits. (See Illustration 2.) Connect drain hose from 1o to bucket.

    Illustration 2:  Inner Bore Cooling Circuits

  1. Blow air into inlet 1i until no more water emerges from outlet 1o.

  1. Repeat Step 2 and Step 3 between inlets 2i through 6i and 2o through 6o. (See Illustration 2.)

NOTE:There are six Z inner cooling circuits that need to be cleared.

  1. For circuit A, connect the air pump to inlet 5 of the Z inner bore cooling circuit. Connect the drain hose from outlet 1 (of the Z inner bore cooling circuit) to bucket. (See Illustration 3.)

    Illustration 3:  Z Inner Bore Cooling Circuits - Inlets and Outlets

  1. For circuit A, block inlets 6 and 8 and blow air in inlet 5 until no more water emerges from outlet 1. (See Table 1.)

    (Step Details)

  1. Repeat Step 5 and Step 6 for circuits B through F. (See Table 1.)

NOTE:The outer circuits are most likely to still contain a substantial quantity of water.

  1. There are two Z outer cooling circuits with inlets and outlets (1i, 1o, 2i, and 2o). (See Illustration 4.) To blow out the circuits, connect air pump to the inlet and drain hose (from outlet to bucket), and blow through the circuit until no more water emerges from the corresponding outlet .

    Illustration 4:  Z Outer Bore Cooling Circuits - Inlets and Outlets

  1. After all 14 circuits have been blown through with air, and no more water emerges from the outlets, the XRMw coil can be considered free from water.

  1. Follow customers procedure for proper disposal of coolant.

3.3   Cable Removal and Bus Bar Disconnection

  1. Confirm power to PGR Cabinet is turned off and LOTO procedures are implemented.

  1. At the service end, remove the safety covers (5352434) from the end of the Y and XZ Busbar cable connections. (See Illustration 5.)

    (Step Details)

NOTE:DO NOT remove the clamps on the busbar leads.

  1. Remove 6 (six) M10 brass nuts and Nord-lock washers that are securing the busbar lugs to the coil. Slide the busbar terminal lugs off from the studs on the coil. Discard Nord-lock washers since they will not be reused. (See Illustration 6.)

    Illustration 6:  Y and XZ Busbar Cable Connections

3.4   Cable Removal

Remove the following cables:

  • I & G Transmit cable at the RF coil

  • DD Bias cable at front and rear of the RF coil

  • Bore temperature sensor cable at the front of the RF coil

  • Gradient coil temperature sensor cable at the rear of the magnet

  • Gradient coil ground cable

4  Finalization

  1. Remove all parts of the gradient insertion tool from the XRMw coil. and return to crate. Properly store all components.

  1. Re-install all passive shim trays into proper slots.

  1. Connect B0 power supply to connection P304-1 on the magnet. Plug the B0 tester to AC power. Quench B0 coil in magnet by pressing and then holding the white button down for at least 1 minute.

  1. Remove the LOTO for the PGR PDU/Gradient Subsystem.

  1. Reconnect the manifold of the gradient coil to the supply and return lines.

  1. Refer to the HEC Coolant Fill and Coolant Leak Check to add coolant to the heat exchanger reservoir, turn on the heat exchanger pumps, and check the coolant system for leaks. The volume added should be close to that of the discharged coolant (refer to Step ).

  1. Restore magnet rear end bell. Refer to the Rear End Bell Removal and Install document for the proper procedure.

  1. Refer to the Bridge and Longitudinal Drive Belt Replacement to restore the rear pedestal by attaching the rear pedestal to the magnet with the two bolts.
