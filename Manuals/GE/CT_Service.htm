🔬 GE CT System Service Guide
AMT Imaging Solutions — Field Service Quick Reference

Applies to: GE Lightspeed (VCT, Pro, Plus, RT), BRivo CT315/325, Discovery CT750 HD, Discovery CT590 RT, Revolution EVO/ACT/Apex. Covers common fault codes, tube, HV, DAS, gantry, couch, and cooling issues.

X-Ray Tube Faults

X-ray tube replacement requires Radiation Safety Officer (RSO) notification and re-qualification scans. Always check tube heat unit (HU) log before concluding tube failure — thermal shutdown is not the same as tube failure.
Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
Tube heat unit limit reached (HU fault)Tube thermal capacity exceeded during heavy scan load or without warm-upAllow tube to cool (20–30 min). Run tube warm-up procedure: Service Desktop → Diagnostics → System Function → X-Ray → Tube Warm-Up. Check cooling water flow to tube housing.
Tube arcing / intermittent shutdownTube aging, vacuum failure, or HV cable issueReview tube arc count and kVp history in service logs. Run tube conditioning sequence. If arcing persists, tube replacement is likely. Inspect HV cables for tracking or damage.
Tube will not prepare (no rotation)Tube rotor failure; HV fault before prep; anode drive electronics faultListen for rotor spin-up — absence of sound indicates anode drive or electronics fault. Check rotor drive supply voltage. Inspect anode drive card. Run tube rotor test in service mode.
Poor image quality — streak artifactsTube focal spot deviation; aging detector; beam filtration issueRun air calibration. Check focal spot specification in service logs. Run DAS auto test. If streaks persist on all protocols, tube replacement or detector calibration needed.
Tube cooling faultCooling water flow low; heat exchanger fouled; coolant pump faultCheck cooling water flow rate to tube housing. Inspect heat exchanger for fouling. Verify coolant pump is running. Check coolant temperature at tube inlet (<25°C typical).

High Voltage (HV) System Faults

HIGH VOLTAGE — GE CT HV systems generate 80–140 kVp at milliamp levels capable of lethal injury. De-energize and lock out/tag out (LOTO) before accessing HV tank, cables, or generator. Confirm discharge with meter before touching.
Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
HV fault at scan startHV generator fault; HV tank oil breakdown; kV or mA out of specReview error log for sub-code. Run HV self-test: Service Desktop → Diagnostics → X-Ray → HV Test. Check DC bus voltage to HV generator. Measure HV output with non-contact HV probe per service manual.
Scan aborts with HV arc faultHV cable tracking; tube arcing; insulation failure in HV tankInspect HV cable candles and slip ring connections. Check HV tank oil level and color (should be clear, not dark or cloudy). Run tube conditioning. Replace HV cable if arc tracking found.
kVp or mA out of specHV generator calibration drift; feedback component failureMeasure kVp and mA per service manual test points. Run HV calibration procedure. Recalibrate or replace HV control board if values cannot be brought into spec.
HV tank oil leakHV tank seal failure or physical damageShut down immediately — oil leakage is an electrical and environmental hazard. Do not operate. Replace HV tank. Dispose of oil per local regulations.

Data Acquisition System (DAS) Faults

Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
DAS communication faultDAS board failure; slip ring data link fault; fiber optic link failureRun DAS Auto Test: Service Desktop → Diagnostics → System Function → DAS → Auto Test. Check DAS fiber optic connections at slip ring. Inspect DAS fan and airflow. Reseat DAS boards.
Ring artifact in imagesSingle failed or degraded detector elementRun air calibration to identify bad detector channels. Review DAS diagnostic for bad channel map. Replace detector module if specific channel consistently fails. Run quality assurance phantom after repair.
DAS overtemperature faultDAS cooling fan failure; blocked airflow; excessive scan duty cycleCheck DAS duct fans and filters — clean or replace. Verify airflow through DAS duct. Allow cooldown before restarting. Check room ambient temperature.
Data path error / recon failureDAS to reconstruction link fault; bad data segment from one view angleRun Recon Data Path test in service menu. Inspect reconstruction network connections. Review error log for specific view angle. DAS board or fiber link replacement if persistent.

Gantry Faults

Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
Gantry will not rotateDrive motor fault; belt failure; rotation encoder fault; safety interlockCheck gantry drive belt — look for breakage or slipping. Verify drive motor and inverter. Inspect rotation encoder. Check all safety interlocks (door, tilt limit).
Gantry rotation noise (grinding/squealing)Main bearing wear; insufficient lubrication; drive belt misalignmentCheck main bearing lubrication per PM schedule (grease every 2 million revolutions). Inspect belt alignment and tension. Check gantry rotation count in service logs.
Gantry tilt faultTilt drive motor fault; hydraulic tilt cylinder leak; tilt encoder faultCheck tilt drive motor and chain/actuator. On hydraulic tilt systems, inspect cylinder for fluid leaks. Test tilt limit switches. Run tilt calibration in service mode.
Slip ring arcing or contact faultWorn slip ring brushes; dirty or corroded rings; worn ring surfaceClean slip rings and brush blocks per PM schedule. Inspect brush spring tension. Measure brush wear length — replace if below minimum per service manual. Clean ring surface with approved cleaner.
Encoder fault / position errorAxial encoder wheel dirty or damaged; encoder reader failureClean axial encoder wheel and reader. Inspect for physical damage. Run encoder calibration. Replace encoder reader if fault persists.

Patient Couch / Table Faults

Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
Couch will not move (horizontal)Drive motor fault; drive belt failure; cradle obstructionCheck cradle drive belt — inspect for wear or breakage. Verify drive motor operation. Check for mechanical obstruction. Run couch diagnostics in service mode.
Couch vertical drive faultVertical motor fault; hydraulic leak (hydraulic tables); limit switchCheck vertical drive motor and actuator. On hydraulic-assisted vertical models, inspect for hydraulic fluid leak. Test vertical limit switches.
Couch position error / limit faultPosition encoder failure; tape switch or limit switch faultClean and inspect tape switches. Check limit switch function by commanding couch to limits in service mode. Replace faulty switch or encoder.
Couch makes grinding noise during travelWorn or dry drive rails; worn drive belt; roller bearing issueLubricate drive rails per PM schedule (use OEM-approved non-magnetic lubricant near bore). Inspect rollers and rails for wear. Replace worn drive belt.

Air Calibration — When to Run

Air calibration corrects for detector channel variations and beam hardening. It must be run with no patient or phantom in the bore and with the tube at operating temperature.
TriggerAction
Ring artifact appears in imagesRun air calibration immediately. If rings persist, run DAS channel diagnostic to identify bad detector element.
After any DAS board replacementFull air calibration required before clinical use.
After tube replacement or conditioningRun air calibration after tube warm-up to baseline new tube output.
Scheduled PMAir calibration recommended at each PM (quarterly at minimum). Document and trend results.
After power outage or recoveryRun air calibration and QA phantom scan before returning to clinical use.

Tip: The most common preventable GE CT service call is thermal shutdown from fouled fans or filters. Cleaning DAS, PDU, and console fans/filters quarterly dramatically reduces site downtime and tube stress.
Tip: Always run the tube warm-up procedure before first scans of the day if the tube has been off for 4+ hours. Skipping warm-up on a cold tube dramatically reduces tube life and risks early failure.
Critical: Never access the HV tank, HV generator, or HV cables with the system energized or without confirming full voltage discharge. GE CT HV systems produce lethal voltages. LOTO before any HV work.
