4  Procedure

4.1  Ground Resistance Checks Using Microguard

DANGER
Electrocution Hazard!
LETHAL VOLTAGES ARE PRESENT WITHIN THE PDU EVEN WHEN ALL PDU BREAKERS ARE OFF.
CHECK THAT POWER AT THE MAIN DISCONNECT IS OFF, LOCKED, AND TAGGED BEFORE PROCEEDING.

  1. Ensure that no power is applied to PDU, see Lock out and tag the Main Disconnect using appropriate OSHA procedure.

  1. Notify all Field Service Engineers working at the site that PDU Main Disconnect will be shut off and locked out.

  1. Locate and shut off the Main Disconnect supplying power to the PDU.

  1. Verify that the Main Disconnect and PDU indicator lights are off, and that power switches are inactive.

  1. With a digital voltmeter (DVM), check that all energy has dissipated.

  1. Plug milliohm adapter module into power outlet of the Microguard. See Illustration 1.

    Illustration 1:  MICROGUARD CONNECTIONS

    NOTE:Since the Microguard is being used solely as a voltmeter in this application, any other meter capable of resolving 0.1 millivolts can be substituted in the next step. Meters other than the Microguard are currently in use. Refer to the appropriate tool catalog for further information.

  1. Attach Microguard red and black leads to meter posts of milliohm module.

  1. Set Microguard Patient Lead Tester switch to "All Other Tests."

  1. Set Microguard Selector switch to "20 Microamps/Millivolts."

  1. Set Microguard Input switch to "Red and Black Probes."

  1. Attach the Kelvin leads as shown in Illustration 1. (Connect one lead to PDU ground bus bar, and the other lead to frame of subsystem cabinet under test.)

  1. The meter reads voltage across ground resistance in millivolts. To obtain resistance in milliohms, multiply meter reading by 2.

  1. Verify that measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms (corresponding to a meter reading of 50 millivolts). If this fails, add an additional ground wire.

  1. Perform Step 11 through Step 13 for each subsystem cabinet.

  1. System Restoration

    • Turn Microguard power off.

    • Remove leads and milliohm adapter from Microguard.

    • Close all subsystem cabinets and PDU cabinet.

4.2  Ground Resistance Checks Using Dale (For Those sites equipped with a Dale 600 or 600E Safety Analyzer)

  1. Perform PDU LOCKOUT TAGOUT. See Lock out and tag the Main Disconnect using appropriate OSHA procedure.

  1. Verify that Main Disconnect and PDU indicators lights are off and power switches are inactive.

  1. Connect one clamp-type lead to the External jack on the top of the Dale 600/600E. Connect the other clamp-type lead to the Chassis jack on the top of the Dale 600/600E. See Illustration 2.

    Illustration 2:  TOP OF DALE 600/600E ANALYZER

  1. On the Dale 600/600E, set the main selector switch to W - Resistance. See Illustration 3.

    Illustration 3:  DALE 600/600E ANALYZER

  1. Plug the Dale into an AC outlet.

  1. On the Dale 600/600E, verify that the two OK LEDs light up, indicating proper wiring of the AC outlet. See Illustration 3.

  1. With leads not touching each other, the display should read 1.00. With the leads touching (copper to copper) the display should read 0.00 to 0.02.

  1. Attach the leads as shown in Illustration 4. (Connect one lead to PDU ground bus bar and the other lead to bare frame of subsystem cabinet under test.)

    Illustration 4:  DALE 600/600E ANALYZER

  1. The Dale 600/600E reads resistance in ohms. For example, a reading of 0.50 equals 500 milliohms.

  1. Verify that measured resistance is less than 100 milliohms. If this fails, add an additional ground wire.

  1. Perform Step 8 through Step 10 for each subsystem cabinet.

  1. System Restoration

    1. Unplug Dale from the AC outlet.

    1. Remove leads from the Dale.

    1. Close all subsystem cabinets and PDU cabinet.
