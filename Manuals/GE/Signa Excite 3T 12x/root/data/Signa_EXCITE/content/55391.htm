Switchable Gradient Amplifier (SGA) Gradient Driver Checks

All references to ACGD or SGA also apply to ACGD Lite or SGA Lite.

Description - This document combines the various gradient driver functional checks that are used on Signa systems with Advanced Control Gradient Driver (ACGD) hardware.

This material describes the status indicators on the Gradient subsystem, and the functional checks that may be used for troubleshooting.

1  Introduction

The gradient driver subsystem contains both fault indicators and a dedicated service tool to help you diagnose problems. LEDs on the control board of a Switchable Gradient Amplifier (SGA) indicate certain fault conditions; these are described in the next section. The Gradient Processor (GP) is specifically designed to help solve troubleshooting problems. The GP reports to the system error log, if gradient driver voltage, current, or temperature parameters are out of specification.

In addition to these built-in tools, a checklist describes symptoms and possible causes. The order of each list begins with possible causes most likely to occur, possible causes easiest to check, or possible causes least costly in terms of time and equipment. For items requiring the replacement of FRUs (field-replaceable unit), refer to the Replacement / Maintenance section of the documentation.

DANGER
FATAL ELECTRIC SHOCK HAZARD!!
THE SGA ACTS AS A CONSTANT LOAD SOURCE, AND WILL SEND MAXIMUM CURRENT TO ANY LOAD (INCLUDING YOU!).
TO PREVENT FATAL ELECTRIC SHOCK, USE EXTREME CARE WHEN TROUBLESHOOTING HIGH POWER COMPONENTS. ENSURE THAT POWER IS OFF TO THE ACGD BEFORE ANY COMPONENT REMOVAL, OR CONNECTOR INTEGRITY VERIFICATION.

NOTICE
Equipment damage possibility. The various modules may have static-sensitive components, such as boards, that can be damaged if not handled in a static-free environment. Take appropriate care (e.g., wear wrist grounding strap) when handling these modules.

3  Using the SGA Analog Signals

The GP monitors the following current, voltage, and temperature variables:

  • Temperature signals from four sensors in the transistor heat sinks

  • Power supply voltage (Vcc)

  • Output voltage.

4  Power Outage Checklist

During troubleshooting, determining if power is applied in an effective way to isolate power as a source of the problem. There are many modules in the ACGD cabinet that are powered. Subsystem problems are detailed in the following sections.

4.1  No Power at All

If there is no power to the ACGD, check the following:

  1. Check the circuit breakers at the AC source of the ACGD Power Supplies. The circuit breakers are located at the PDU.

  2. Check the wiring on the inside of the ACGD cabinet. Use the Gradient Driver Block Diagrams.

4.2  No Power at GP

The "Power" LED on the front panel of the GP chassis will not light if power is OFF to the GP module. If there is no power to the GP, check the following:

  1. Check to see if the auxiliary circuit breaker on the PDU is ON.

  2. Check the cable from the PDU to the GP.

4.3  No 208 VAC to SGA

There are LEDs on the front of the SGA for each main board. If these are not lit, check the following:

  1. Check the PDU Auxiliary Circuit Breakers.

  2. Check all power cables.

4.4  No LED From Individual Axes on SGA

See Section 4.3 No 208 Vac to SGA.

4.5  Circuit Breaker Trips

  1. Check for short circuits and component damage.

  2. Check circuit breaker functionality.

  3. Inspect for arcing damage inside PDU.

  4. If damage is seen, replace the PDU.

  5. If there is no damage, turn on the breaker.

5  SGA Error Checklist

5.1  One Axis Remains in Standby and Won't Go to Ready

Check the small red Enable LED on the SGA Control Board. Remove the SGA top cover to see the LED. The LED lights when the main board is receiving the standby command signal.

  1. See Section 4.2 No Power at the GP

  2. Check the cables and the connections between the GP and SGA.

  3. If the LED is not lit (i.e., not receiving the Enable from the GP), and the power module remains in standby mode, the problem is in the SGA.

  4. Check the jumpers on the main board.

  5. Replace the main board.

5.2  All SGA axes remain in Standby and Won't Go to Ready

  1. See Section 6.1 Faulty Communication – All Axes.

  2. See Section 5.1 One Axis Remains in Standby and Won't Go to Ready.

6  Status and Control Problem Checklist

6.1  Faulty Communication – All Axes

  1. Check the integrity and the connections of the fiber optic connections. Use the fiber optic light meter when performing Fiber Optic Checks. See the Procedure for Fiber Optic Checks.

  2. See section Section 4.2 No Power at GP.

  3. After the GP is switched on, allow about 15 seconds for its self-test to complete.

6.2  Faulty Communication – One Axis

  1. See Section 4.3 No 208 VAC to SGA

  2. Check the cables and the connections between the GP and SGA.

  3. Check the integrity and the connections of the fiber optic cables leading to the GP. Use the fiber optic light meter when performing Fiber Optic Checks. See the procedure for Fiber Optic Checks.

  4. Swap the control board of the faulty axis with the control board of another axis. If the problem moves, replace main board.

  5. Replace the GP.

7  Cooling Problem Checklist

7.1  SGA Modules Overheat

  1. Check that the fan is running correctly.

  2. Check that rear doors are closed. The door should pull closed due to the vacuum created by the fan.

  3. Check for clogged air filters.

  4. Check for blockage of the vents.

  5. Check that output power and load are within specification.

  6. Replace the overheated assembly.

7.2  Fan Does Not Run

  1. Check the circuit breaker on the front of the PDU.

  2. Check voltage going to the fan.

  3. Replace the fan.

8  Fan Runs Backward

If the fan runs backward, airflow is reduced. Correct the incorrectly wired AC phases. The PDU input phase may be reversed. For ACGD only, the best indication of a mis-wired fan is if the vacuum created by the fan does not pull the rear door of the cabinet shut.

For ACGD Lite the air flow pattern is from front to rear. Placing a piece of paper in front of any module is a good indication of air flow direction.
