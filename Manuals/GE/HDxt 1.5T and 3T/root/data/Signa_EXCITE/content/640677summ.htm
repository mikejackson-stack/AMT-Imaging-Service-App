2  Procedure

2.1   Signal Scan

  1. From the Scan Desktop, start new scan by selecting [New Pt]; set Patient ID to “geservice” and Patient Weight to “111” pounds. Click [Patient Position] to open protocols window.

  1. Remove all other surface coils from the cradle. Place the Split Head Coil on the patient table with the BNC connectors extending into the magnet. Lock the baseplate into the table slots. Connect the QD box/QHTR to the BNC connectors on the coil and the connector on the MR unit. The cables from the QD box/QHTR to the head coil should always be straight, with no crossovers or loops evident from the outside.

    (Step Details)

  1. At the magnet, press “Alignment Light” button to turn on the light. Move the cradle to align the coil to the alignment lights. Press “Landmark” button to landmark the alignment.

  1. Move the coil to scan position by pushing the “Move to Scan” button, ensuring cable does not get snagged.

  1. At the console, set the protocols per the Signal section from Table 1: Signal and Noise Protocols. GE Service Personnel can use the Head SNR Signal protocol in the GE / Other protocol folder on 3.0T Systems.

  1. Click [Save Series] to download the protocols, then click [Prepare to Scan].

  1. Run [Auto Prescan]. Take note of the R1, R2 and TG values.

  1. R1 should equal 11, and R2 should equal 14. If not, run [Manual Prescan] and adjust the R1 and R2 values. Click [Done].

  1. Click [Scan].

2.2   Noise Scan

A signal scan must be run prior to the noise scan as the same R1, R2 and TG values must be used for both the signal and noise scans. Do not run an Auto Prescan prior to the noise scan as the values will be changed.

  1. Copy the signal scan series. Use [Copy Series] (highlight signal series and click right mouse button) and [Paste Series] in RX Manager. GE Service Personnel can use the Head SNR Noise protocol in the GE / Other protocol folder on 3.0T Systems.

  1. Click [View Edit] and set the protocols per the Noise section from Table 2: Signal and Noise Protocols.

  1. Click [Save Series] and click [Prepare to Scan].

  1. Open [Display CVs] menu under [Research Operations]. Set the “rhformat” and “do_noise” CVs to “1”.

  1. Run [Manual Prescan], Do Not make any changes, and click [Done].

  1. Click [Scan].

    (Step Details)

2.3   SNR Image Analysis

  1. A rectangular ROI should be used for the signal and noise measurements for 1.0T/1.5T. The actual ROIs should be similar in shape and size to those in the examples below. For 3.0T circular ROI should be used.select ROI as 80% of the phantom image for signal scan.For Noise scan choose 100% of Rectangular ROI.

    (Step Details)

  1. ROIs in both signal and noise images can be measured directly in the image browser. Click the user interface button [MEASURE], select the rectangular shape, and adjust its size and orientation so that the ROI is similar to those found in the examples above. select the circular ROI as 80% of the phantom image. The ROI appear in the lower right corner of the image. Mean, standard deviation, and area of the ROI will appear in the lower right corner of the image.

  1. Record the values for mean signal and mean noise and SNR in Table 3.

  1. Individual receiver SNR is defined as the mean of data within the signal ROI divided by the mean of data within the noise ROI (with a correction factor).

2.5   External Cable Check

  1. No cable continuity checking is recommended for the connector because of pin damage concerns.

2.6   PIN Diodes Check

  1. Not Applicable

2.7   Mechanical Hardware Check

  1. Check the BNC cables for mechanical damage and shorted center pins.

  1. Inspect the Bendix/Hypertronics connector for damage from improper insertion.

  1. Banana plug on the 1.5T Bendix Quick Disconnect plug will damage pins if the operator attempts to insert the connector upside down. At this time also inspect the Bendix/Hypertronics connector for foreign material and remove as appropriate.

  1. Make sure cradle latches and top latches are operating and still have adequate range of motion.
