RRF Chassis Theory

  • The RF-DIF board receives synchronous digital commands for control of two Exciter Boards (the 2nd Exciter board is for MNS), one eight channel Receiver Board, one eight channel UTNS Board, and the MUX Board.

  • The RF-DIF Board translates register commands and data (Rho, Theta, and Omega sequences, plus B0 compensation commands) to drive digital waveforms (two 14 bit data busses at a 20MHz rate) across the backplane to the Exciter Board(s).

  • The Exciter Board clocks the digital waveform through Digital to Analog Converters. It then up-converts the analog results by 144MHz followed by a programmable down convert to provide the local oscillator output and RF output.

  • When the Exciter Board is unblanked, the RF output is sent to the RF Amplifier where it is gained up and sent into the transmit coil. The exciter output attenuation (TG) is determined during prescan to provide the optimal return in MR signal.

  • When the MR signal is returned, the signal goes through the TNS Module(s) to the MUX Board where it is routed to the appropriate receive channel.

  • Each receive channel passes through the UTNS Board. The present UTNS-II Board provides only signal gain. Spike noise suppression is done on the old style standalone TNS modules in the bottom of the cabinet. In system that have a UTNS-II, this situation is different than on system that have a UTNS3. The UTNS3 provides both receive gain and spike noise suppression.

  • From the UTNS Board, the receive signal passes into the Receiver Board where it is filtered and a programmable attenuation is applied (R1). UTNS3 boards also provide spike noise suppression.

  • The signal is then mixed down to an IF sample frequency using the local oscillator from the Exciter Board, and then an anti-alias filter is applied for the +250KHz bandwidth.

  • From here, the Receiver Board converts each channel’s analog data to 16 bit digital data at a conversion rate of 1MHz.

  • The RF-DIF Board reads converted data from all eight receive channels every 1MHz period.

  • The digital receive data is then passed through the fiber interface (“Gigalink”) to the MGD Chassis for processing. Along with the receive data, the RF-DIF Board constantly loops through status of the RRF Chassis and passes this to the MGD Chassis for monitoring.

  • The “Gigalink” fiber interface to the MGD Chassis also transfers the 20MHz system reference clock and a continuous checksum to monitor transfer integrity.

  • The RF-DIF Board also provides a CAN interface link that can be used to diagnose the state of the RRF Chassis in the event that the “Gigalink” fiber interface fails.

Differences Between RRF Chassis Hardware and LX-TPS/ISE Chassis Hardware

This section briefly describes some of the major hardware differences between the new RRF Chassis and the previous generation TPS/ISE Chassis:

  • Exciter, Receiver and RF Multiplexer functions have been packaged separately in the new RRF (Remote RF) Chassis to reduce susceptibility to noise.

  • Two independently programmable Exciters can be populated in the RRF Chassis.

  • The RRF Receiver Board uses Super-Nyquist sampling; the Imaging Frequency is centered at 2.25 MHz rather than baseband.

  • The RRF Chassis provides ups to 16 channels at +/-250KHz compared to the CERD with four channels at +/-125KHz.
