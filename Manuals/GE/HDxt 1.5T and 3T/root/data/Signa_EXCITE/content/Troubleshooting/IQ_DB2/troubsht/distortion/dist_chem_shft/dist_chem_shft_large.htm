Chemical Shift Artifact

     

Image Info:
The first image of the shoulder was acquired with a 2.78 kHz receive bandwidth while the second image was acquired with a 16 kHz receive bandwidth. The frequency encoding direction was superior-inferior. 

Symptom:
The artifact demonstrated here is chemical shift. 

Flowchart:
Refer to the Geometric Distortion Troubleshooting Flowchart. 

Root Cause:
During readout each proton's position along the frequency encoding axis is determined by the frequency it is precessing at. Fat and water protons at the same position in the body precess at different frequencies, fat being 73 Hz slower than water at 0.5T. Since the computer assumes all protons precess at the same frequency, the signal from fat is therefore mapped to a different location corresponding to the frequency it is precessing. Narrow receive bandwidths accentuate this by assigning a smaller number of frequencies across the image. 

Solution:
In this case the image at 16 kHz has a total of 32000 Hz across the image, at 256 frequency encodes this amounts to 125 Hz per frequency encoding line. Since, at 0.5T, fat precesses 73 Hz slower than water this results in fat being shifted 0.58 pixels in the frequency direction. In the image acquired with a 2.78 kHz bandwidth there are only 5560 Hz across the image. With the same number of frequency encodes this amounts to 21.72 Hz per line. In this case fat would then be shifted 3.4 pixels from its correct location. Note how this is evident in the first image in the top right corner (arrow) where the fluid in the vessel is mapped correctly but the fat surrounding it has move upwards in the image creating a hole which should overlay the vessel. This effect is much more significant on higher field strengths. At 1.5 T fat and water precess 220 Hz apart which would result in a shift of over 10 pixels!
