Low SNR Theory

Low SNR can be caused by almost anything internal or external to the system that lowers signal (coil detuning, preamp gain, decoupling malfunction, loss of quadrature, etc.) or raises the noise floor (room noise, displaced signal, noisy preamp, noisy T/R switching diodes, etc.).

Reports of SNR problems from the service tests are straightforward.  The customer, however, may use descriptions such as "grainy", "mottled", "noisy", "don't jump out at me", "just not the way they used to be", "not crisp", etc.  The customer may only report a complaint on one type of scan,  but the problem may actually be affecting many or all scans.

Beware of prematurely identifying an overall system issue as a body, head, or surface coil only problem based on the customer complaint.  Remember, system SNR problems are often first perceived in normally low SNR scans like C-spine or Lumbar Axials.  Don't be misled into thinking the problem only exists in these scans or the particular coil being used.

SNR and Image Quality are dependent on the combined effects of hardware, tuned fields, environmental conditions, and protocols.  Many reported Image Quality / SNR problems are the result of multiple changes in the system, not just the problem that took it over the edge of acceptability for the customer.

Before troubleshooting any image quality problem, ask yourself the following questions.  They may help you to better understand the problem and aid you in troubleshooting:

1) What is the MRFWU (Most Recently Fiddled With Unit)?  Has anything recently been changed like parts, incoming power, software, scan parameters, etc.?

2) Is the problem consistent or intermittent?   (To be taken into logical troubleshooting consideration).  Intermittent Image Quality issues may require multiple passes of a given test in order to achieve confidence in the results.
