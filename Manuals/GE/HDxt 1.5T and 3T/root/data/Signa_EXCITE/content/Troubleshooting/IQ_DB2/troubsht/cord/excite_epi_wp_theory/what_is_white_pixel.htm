White Pixel - Theory

What Is White Pixel?

"White Pixel" is the term we use for transient noise events that occur in the received NMR data.  These transients are very brief in duration, typically only occurring in a few samples of the raw data.  They are usually caused by an electrical discharge of some sort which occurs anywhere in the RF tight enclosure of the magnet room.  This electrical discharge can occur due to electrical arcing of some sort, or merely the static discharge which is rampant in low humidity environments.  One of the most common sources of spike noise is loose metallic (even non-magnetic) hardware which moves due vibration.

 

When an electric discharge of some kind occurs, it causes electromagnetic fields to emanate for the very brief duration of the arc.  These fields are then received by the sensitive receive hardware of the MRI and we then see very short duration of elevated signal in the raw data.  This is the genesis of the term as it looks as if there are "white pixels" in the magnitude image of the raw data file when it is viewed.

 

A spike, or white pixel, is considered as "broadband" noise.  That is because the electromagnetic field which is generated has energy spread throughout the frequency spectrum.  When we do a Fourier Transform of the spike in time domain we see that it becomes energy widely spread throughout the frequency spectrum.  The sharper the spike, and shorter the duration, the more the energy is spread out through the spectra.  What this means, and the reason it is important to us is, that regardless of what frequency our MR receiver is tuned to, we can (and will) receive some of the undesired energy (noise) that is being radiated.  

 

This is clearly different than a correlated noise which is caused by a discrete frequency (or frequencies) of undesired signal somehow leaking into the RF screen room.  Discrete frequencies are not caused by arcing and are not transient in nature.  Discrete frequencies, due to their narrowband nature, will only be received if our receiver band pass happens to be tuned to that particular frequency.
