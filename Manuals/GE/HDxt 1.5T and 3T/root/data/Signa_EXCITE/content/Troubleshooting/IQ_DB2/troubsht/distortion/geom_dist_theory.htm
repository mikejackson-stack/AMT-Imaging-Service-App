Geometric Distortion Theory

Geometric Distortion is a misrepresentation in the relative size of all or part of the image data.  This may be exhibited as a one or two dimensional distortion across the entire image, a less severe distortion that mis-shapes the image, or a distortion that is confined to a small localized area.

The most common cause of a geometric distortion can be attributed to a failure or miscalibration in the gradient subsystem.  Any distortion or change in the expected gradient output (stuck or missing data bits, arcing gradient filters, shorted output wells, etc) could potentially lead to geometric distortion.  The entire gradient data/signal path from the IPG board to the gradient coil immediately become suspect.  Other failures of a more localized type may be attributable to patient dependent causes  (i.e. patient implants, tattoos, make-up) or foreign material in and around the field of view.  The filming camera or the image monitor can also distort images.

Diagnosing geometric distortion problems entails an initial image analysis to determine the source of the problem.  Severe failures will generally point to a failure of the gradient subsystem.  Symptoms with less severe distortions, or images that are just somewhat out of proportion may be a calibration problem or more subtle failure of the gradient subsystem.  Localized or shim distortions point toward patient dependent causes, or foreign objects in the field.  The filming camera and image monitor must also be ruled out.
