Shading Theory

Shading is the loss of an image's signal intensity from one side of the image to the other.  Some amount of shading is "normal" in MR imaging, but there are two types of hardware failures that will cause an abnormal amount of shading.  One is bad magnetic homogeneity (shim, metal in the bore, etc.) and the other is bad RF homogeneity (coil tuning quality, RF transmit, signal reception, etc.).  This flowchart will help to resolve hardware malfunctions and/or incorrect calibrations that cause abnormal shading.

Improper clinical applications will increase the severity of shading in systems that do not have hardware problems.  Most normal shading occurs in scans that use 1.5T body coil due to its low pass design.  Signa 3.0T, 1.0T, and 0.5T systems use a high pass coil which is much less likely to cause shading. 

Normal shading can be aggravated basically by two application issues.  First and most commonly is patient positioning.  Improper patient positioning causes asymmetrical coil loading which enhances diagonal shading, left to right shading, and the need for higher transmit power.  The second is ferrous material causing signal loss near the metal.  There are tips in this flowchart to help you understand some of the things a customer should avoid in patient prep to reduce shading.
