1  Procedure

1.1   Checking Prerequisites

UTNS must not be emulated out and it must be notching and counting spike noise events. To be sure the prerequisites are met, perform the following steps. Refer to the Stopping and Starting UTNS3 procedure for more information on the TNS Software control utility and UTNS commands.

  1. To Enable The UTNS:

    1. Open a C-shell.

    1. Type: gedit /w/config/mgd_stage.

    1. If there is an L in the first line of the file, delete it and click [Save]. If there is not an L in the first line of the file, continue to the next step.

    1. Exit out of mgd_stage .

    1. If the content of the mgd_stage file was changed during these steps, reset TPS.

  1. To Enable Notching:

    1. Open a C-shell.

    1. Type: mgd_tns -b m=a 1.

1.2   Counting Spike Noise Events

The UTNS antenna attached to the rear pedestal is always reading the noise in the MR Scan room. In order to get a reading of the noise count in the room at a certain time, perform the following:

  1. Open a C-Shell.

  1. View the count of spike noise by performing one of the following:

    1. To get a reading of the spike noise being detected on all channels, type: mgd_tns –j m=a.

    1. To get a reading of the spike noise being detected on any individual channel, type: mgd_tns –j m=’mask’ (where ‘mask’ is the receiver number from 1 to 32).

1.3   Troubleshooting Gain Stage

  1. Setup a localizer scan with any 8-channel coil for an 8-channel system or a 16-channel coil for a 16-channel system. For 32-channel systems, try the coil in both the B and C coil connectors to test all UTNS boards.

  1. Click [Manual Prescan], click [CFL].

  1. Make sure that the gain of R1 is all the way to 13.

  1. When the window opens, use the “Rec” button below the graph viewer window to perform the Manual Prescan on each of the channels.

  1. Note any channel on which the magnitude appears very low.

  1. Stop the Manual Prescan.

  1. At the back of the UTNS3, disconnect the connectors at J6 and J7 from the board and connect them directly together.

NOTICE
POSSIBLE EQUIPMENT DAMAGE – BE EXTREMELY CAUTIOUS WHEN REMOVING J6 and J7 CABLES. DO NOT PULL ON THE CABLES!
 
 

  1. With the cables from J6 and J7 connected directly together, click again on [Manual Prescan].

  1. Click on the [Rec] button below the graph viewer window to perform a manual prescan on all the channels. The amplitude of each of the channels should be less than before, but they should all be relatively equal in amplitude.

  1. If there were any suspect channels with low amplitude in Step 5, check to see if this channel has amplitude relatively equal to the other channels in Step 9. If so, suspect a bad UTNS.

  1. Reconnect the cables to the proper locations at J6 and J7.

1.4   Suspect Errant Notching of the UTNS 3

The UTNS could cause ghosting in images due to multiple different reasons:

  • There is real spike noise that is not suppressed.

  • UTNS is suppressing spike noise which occurs in the middle of K-Space.

  • UTNS is not working and randomly suppresses the receive signal for no reason.

  1. Open a C-shell.

  1. Type gedit /w/config/mgd_stage.

  1. In the first line of the file, type “L” to emulate out the UTNS3.

  1. Click [Save].

  1. Exit this file.

  1. Reset TPS.

  1. Setup the problem that caused you to suspect the notching capability of the UTNS3.

  1. Click [Save Series].

  1. Right click on [Research Options] and select [Display CVs]. Set rawmode to 1. Click [Accept].

  1. Right click on Research Options and select [Download].

  1. Click [Scan]:

    1. If there are noise pixels very near the center of K-Space, it is very likely that the room has a white pixel problem that needs to be resolved. Because there are so many white pixels, there are probably some in the center of K-Space that are being notched.

      1. Run a test scan with rawmode=0 and view the resultant image with the UTNS emulated out (remember to TPS reset if a change is made to the mgd_stage file).

      1. Run a test scan again with rawmode=0, this time with the UTNS not emulated out (remember to TPS reset if a change is made to the mgd_stage file).

      1. Compare the images from ( Step 11.a.i) and ( Step 11.a.ii). Leave the mgd_stage file the way it is with the best image quality until the White Pixel issue is resolved.

    1. If there are NOT noise pixels near the center of K-Space, it is possible that the UTNS is defective. Try replacing the UTNS and leave the “L” in the first line of the mgd_stage file until a new UTNS board is installed and not causing ghosting.

1.5   Suspect that the UTNS is not hearing Spike Noise

If you are getting spike noise in images and wondering if the UTNS is not detecting it, this is a good place to start.

  1. If you are hoping that the UTNS will be notching out spike noise, be sure that it is not emulated out in the mgd_stage file (there should be no “L” in the first line of the mgd_stage file).

  1. Obtain an oscilloscope with 1M-Ohm impedance.

  1. Hook-up one of the scope outputs to J30 “Detect Out” on the back of the UTNS.

  1. Hook up another of the scope outputs to J19 the “INVIEW” RF-DIF test point on the front of the MGD chassis. Trigger your signal on the low to high transition of this 0 to 3.3V signal.

  1. Setup a scan, as listed in Example Scan Setup.

  1. Click [Manual Prescan].

  1. Click [Scan TR]. Set the TR value to a minimum.

  1. If the probe is connected properly, the oscilloscope should show the RF Excitation Phase. (To be sure that you are seeing the excitation phase, switch on and off the FP-IO “RF Enable” while observing the amplitude of the J30 output signal. When “RF Enable” is switched to ON, the amplitude should be noticeably higher than when it is OFF.) If it is a flat line, check the probe path (the cabling from the system cabinet service panel through the penetration panel to the antenna).

  1. If the UTNS antenna is detecting any spike noise events, these events should be visible on the oscilloscope.
