Gradient Troubleshooting

NOTICE
If any gradient cables are swapped between different axis or polarities for troubleshooting, perform DQA-II procedure to verify that the gradient cables are returned to their original position.
 
 

1  Running Gradient Diagnostics

NOTE:For gradient block diagram, select Gradient option in the Interactive Block Diagram document. For gradient output cable diagram, see Step .

Gradient Diagnostics offers three tests and you should run all three. The three tests are: Gradient Hammer, Gradient Fidelity, and Gradient Static. To locate the tests on the Customer Service Desktop, navigate to:

Diagnostics → System Function → Gradient → Gradient Diagnostics

Diagnostics → Hardware Location → PGR Cabinet → Gradient → Gradient Diagnostics

Accept all defaults on the Gradient Diagnostics screen and click the [Run] button. (Shown below.)

3.4  Gradient Cable Swapping

Before swapping cables, perform LOTO for the PGR PDU/Gradient Subsystem

  • Be aware that swapping gradient data fibers will apply Eddy Current Compensation (ECC) to wrong coil axis which could potentially result in SPT eddy current failures or even gradient current distortion faults. May need to disable eddy compensation in these cases.

    NOTE:ECC can be disabled by performing the following:

    1. Verify that you have good Save Info Media.

    2. Go into Grafidy3 tool and select [Zero Out Grafidy values].

    3. Perform a Do TPS reset.

    4. Perform necessary tests.

    5. When complete perform [Restore Info] and perform a TPS reset.

  • Perform a TPS Reset after swapping any two gradient data fiber cables.

Illustration 10:  X, Y, and Z Gradient Data Flow

To help you solve image problems, try using some of the following recommended hardware solutions:

  • Swap output cables from Cronus XGA control board (J11/J14, J12/J15, J13/J16).

  • Swap input cables at the XGA amplifiers J1/J2 between axis.

  • Swap amps and power supplies (outers to inners, x to y, and y to z). (Shown below.)

    Illustration 11:  Sample of Y and Z Swap

  • Swap outputs at the bus bar or at top of PGR cabinet. (Shown below.)

    Illustration 12:  Top of Gradient Cabinet

    The Illustration above depicts X, Y, and Z at the top of the Gradient Cabinet where you can swap positions if necessary.

    Swapping gradient output cables will apply eddy compensation to wrong coil axis which could potentially result in SPT eddy current failures or even gradient current distortion faults. May need to disable eddy compensation in these cases.

    NOTE:Do not swap output cabling at the XGA terminal lugs and do not swap LEMs between XGAs .

4  Known Gradient issues

Within the gradient cabinet there are three identical sets of components to operate the X, Y, and Z functions. These components can be swapped to aid in the troubleshooting process.

4.1  Over Slew-rate Bit and Over Temp Errors

This type of error indicates that the over slew-rate bit (ovrSR) was set during the start of the scan.

Table 1:  Gradient Messages Error Log - Over Slew-rate Example

For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
1

2267827

XGD hardware warning detected

The ovrSR bit in DSP_FAULT register was detected on the XGD XGA-CB board X, Y, or Z-axis during start of a scan or diag.

In some of the scans gradient + ECC compensation will exceed the slew-rate limit and DSP firmware will detect and report a warning. As long as the hardware is properly functioning, this will be logged to the system (messages above) but system scanning will not be inhibited or affected.

Run one or more passes of Gradient Fidelity Diagnostic to confirm proper operation of the gradient hardware. If this diagnostic passes, these can be classified as nuisance messages.

4.4  System Intermittently Stop Errors

The system intermittently stops and the only way to recover is by rebooting the system. This type of error is seen in a log when an interruption occurs.

Table 4:  Gradient Messages Error Log - System Intermittently Stop Examples

For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
1

2267943

The Z axis XGA is reporting a power off fault

Check EMO reset and perform a TPS reset. If this error occurs again, contact your service representative.
NOTE:Notice that all axes are reporting power loss, however, visual inspection of all units shows that they have power. Common factor that would result in all 3 reporting power loss is an unstable 48 V PS2 power supply operation. The PS2 power supply is located inside the PDU. Check for the presence of the green PS2 LED (you should see two LEDs: the upper is PS1, the lower is PS2) by viewing through the vent holes located above the magnet power supply connection on the front faceplate of the PDU. A dark LED means that the PS2 unit must be replaced. See the PDU vendor doc replacement section for more details.

2267857 1

A gradient distortion fault has occurred on the X axis

Error indicates that small signal input to XGA does not match large signal output by XGA.
  • Look for other errors prior to this one and occurring at about the same time that might explain why this error occurred.
  • There are many reasons why this error would appear alone in error log and not in presence of any others. List of possible reasons in order of most to least probable include:
    • Faulty XGA or XPS fiber-optic transceiver module.
    • Faulty XGA (likely) or XPS (much less likely).
    • Poor gradient coil circuit connections.
    • Poor calibration - usually only seen during installation or troubleshooting (like when XGAs swapped).
  • Faulty XGA or XPS fiber-optic transceiver module (SFP):Refer to the information in Step  or Step  for the process to troubleshoot a defective SFP module.
  • Faulty XGA:
    • Swap fiber optic cables from failing XGA module to non-failing XGA.
    • Repeat failing sequence (scan, gradient diagnostic) and see if problem moves.
    • Problem moves: Try swapping suspect bad XGA to another axis and see if problem follows XGA.
    • Problem moves with XGA: Replace XGA.
    • Problem does not move: Calibration or circuit connections appear suspect. Proceed to next section.
    • Problem does not move: Proceed on to next troubleshooting steps as XGA appears OK.
  • Faulty XPS:
    • Swap both black power cable lines between XPS on failing axis and XPS on another axis. See Gradient Troubleshooting service methods document if details for doing this needed.
    • Repeat failing sequence (scan, gradient diagnostic) and see if problem moves.
    • Problem moves: Replace XPS.
    • Problem does not move: Proceed on to next troubleshooting steps as XPS appears OK.
  • Poor gradient coil connections:Inspect all circuit connections starting at XGA terminal lugs and check every connection in path out to bus bar connections on rear of XRMB.
  • Poor calibration – perform a saveINFO and then perform Grafidy.

4.5  System Will Not Scan - Initial Error Messages

Table 5:  Gradient Messages Error Log - Initial Message Examples

For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
1

2267833

XGD reports no 420 V power

  1. Check Gradient 420 V power breaker and EMO reset button.
  2. Check condition of 420 V PDU power circuit breaker and confirm system is not in E-Stop.
  3. Check the “XPS Fault” LED on each of the XPS units. Unit with illuminated “XPS Fault” LED has failed and requires replacement. This normally should be a single unit.
    NOTE:If none of “XPS Fault” LEDs are illuminated and XPS units have power, recheck error log for earlier messages that will help identify the real problem. Otherwise, call the On-Line Center for assistance if needed.

2

2267962

The X axis XGA-SB is reporting a PLL failure

  1. Check the integrity of connections at XGA Control Board and at each XGA unit.
    NOTE:Observe the following:
    • A PLL is a Warning, it will not stop the scan. Most times this error will show up as a collapsed image, which is a dark line in the image, direction will depend on the axis that failed and type of scan being completed.
    • The PLL is derived from the signal on the single strand clock fiber from the XGA Control Board inside the GMC (Cronus) Chassis to each XGA. A loose or damaged clock fiber or fiber optic connection at the XGA Control Board inside the GMC (Cronus) Chassis or at the XGA unit could cause this fault.

  2. Swap failing clock fiber cable at the XGA Control Board (inside the GMC Chassis) with neighboring fiber cable.
    NOTE:The clock signals on each clock line from the XGA Control Board to each XGA unit are identical.

  3. Perform action that causes fault, then check error log.
    NOTE:If the problem moves, the XGA Control Board is faulty. Replace the XGA Control Board. If the problem does not move, swap clock fiber cable at the failing XGA with neighboring XGA unit. This unit should be on same line as what was swapped at XGA Control Board.

  4. Swap failing sfp fiber optic cable with neighboring cable.
  5. Perform action that results in fault and check error log.
    NOTE:If the problem moves, the fiber optic line is faulty. Replace or repair the faulty fiber optic line. If the problem does not move, the XGA is faulty. Replace the XGA unit.

4.9  System Running Errors

This type of error is reported in log but does not inhibit scanning.

Table 9:  System Running - Temperature Error Example

For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
1

2267873

Y axis XGA-SB is reporting a TEC out of temperature range fault.

TEC temperature is 124C

A thermoelectric device (TEC) is present on the internal system board of the XGA to control the temperature stability of critical compensation circuits. The TEC can become dislodged or malfunction thus causing this error. The TEC can only compensate (by adding heat) within a certain temperature range of the XGA cold-plate.

Confirm that the PE cooling loop of the HEC is stable and operating in the specified range (30-35°C).

NOTE:If the TEC is within range, the TEC is operating properly. If the TEC is out of range, the TEC is faulty. The TEC is not serviceable. Replace the XGA unit.

NOTE:The XGA will operate without temperature stable TEC. However, a consequence is that FMRI scans may exhibit ghosting artifact.

4.10  Unable To Scan Errors

Extended error recommends swapping pair of black power cables between Z XPS and neighboring Y XGA and Z XGA and neighboring Y XPS. Early sites don’t have black power cables that are long enough to swap. May need to physically swap the XPS modules and repeat experiment. FMI 60769X will provide long length power cables to early sites that don’t have them.

Table 10:  Unable to Scan Error - High Voltage Example

For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
1

2267889

Z axis XPS is reporting a High Voltage I-Bus Over-current Fault

  1. Swap pair of black power cables between Z XPS and neighboring Y XGA and Z XGA and neighboring Y XPS.
    NOTE:Early sites don’t have black power cables that are long enough to swap. FMI 60769X will provide long length power cables to early sites that don’t have them.

  2. Repeat scan or run gradient static or fidelity diagnostic.
    NOTE:If the same Z axis XPS faults, the Z XPS is faulty. Replace the XPS. If Y axis XPS faults, the Z XGA is faulty. Replace the XGA.

4.11  TPS Reset Failure

 For this error:Conditions and Potential Issue:
1

2267728 (first in error log)

Unexpected communication loss with XGD. Please TPS reset and try again.

  • Check for power to the Cronus Chassis components (GMC Board, XGAControl Board, XPS Control Board).
  • If Cronus power missing check for presence of green PS1 LED by viewing through vent holes located above Magnet Power supply connection on front faceplate of PDU.
  • Two green LEDs visible one above the other means PS1 (upper LED) andPS2 (lower LED) are both OK.
  • If Cronus power OK, LEDs OK, then review extended error message and perform recommended checks.
