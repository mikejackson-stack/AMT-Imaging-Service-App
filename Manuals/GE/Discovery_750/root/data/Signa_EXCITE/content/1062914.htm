2  Overview

This tool allows a quick and easy check for gross deviations in RF power levels (inputs and outputs), PSU voltages, and FET junction temperatures for the RF Amplifier. Testing can be done using appropriately loaded body or head coil or using the 3.0T fan-cooled dummy load.

NOTE:Currently, this procedure applies only to 3.0T systems. It is not compatible with 1.5T MR450 systems.

4  Procedure

4.1  Setting Up Phantoms

NOTICE
Currently, this procedure applies only to 3.0T systems. It is not compatible with 1.5T MR450 systems. This test requires appropriate sphere and loader assembly landmarked at the proper location on cradle.
 
 

  1. Set up the appropriate phantom.

    1. If selecting the Body RF Chain, set up the 3.0T Body Sphere and Loader Phantom assembly in the center of patient cradle, and landmark the phantom.

    1. If selecting the Head RF Chain, set up the 3.0T Head Sphere and Loader Phantom assembly on the head load positioner inside the head coil, and landmark the phantom.

  1. If a problem is suspected in the transmit chain resulting in a poor RF load that is causing RF Amp faults, substituting the fan-cooled 3.0T dummy load from the 3.0T RF Power Measurement in place of the normal body or head transmit loads may confirm this. Otherwise, proceed to Launching RF Amp Power Check Tool.

    1. Confirm the phantom is landmark as described in Step 1.a or Step 1.b.

    1. Obtain a DVM reading and configure to measure resistance.

    1. Measure the resistance between the center pin and the shield of the RF Connector on the 3.0T fan-cooled dummy load from the 3.0T RF Power Measurement Kit, and confirm it measures 50 ohms, ±5 ohms.

    1. Disable RF input to the amplifier by placing the RF ENB Switch on the DTX1 Exciter module, located in the Penetration Cabinet, in the Disabled (down) position as shown below.

      Illustration 1:  RF Enable/Disable Switch

    1. Connect the 3.0T fan-cooled dummy load to the body or head ports on the RF Amplifier as shown below.

      Illustration 2:  Dummy Load Connection - Body

      Illustration 3:  Dummy Load Connection - Head

    NOTE:Connect the N to 7/16 in. adapter between J3 and the dummy load cable as shown above.

    1. Disable TR Driver Module fault monitoring (shown below).

      Illustration 4:  Driver Module Front Switches

    1. Enable RF input to the amplifier by placing the RF ENB Switch in the Enabled (up) position.

  1. On the second line, type: ls *.results -ltr and press <Enter>.

  1. On the third line, type: more rfamp_10_4_2008.results, using the date the test was run, then press <Enter> as shown below.

Check the test setup and landmarking, and check for any RF Amplifier faults in the GE System log.

    Test Execution Hints:

    • The power readings are most accurate at a TG setting of 200. Since the power readings are used to estimate the S11 and VSWR of the attached load, these values also are most accurate at a TG setting of 200.

    • The ability to test at other TG levels is provided for assistance in troubleshooting issues that seem to correlate to peak output levels.

    • The results are most consistent when using a dummy load for the setup.

    • The test results from multiple tool passes will be appended in a single file for the day to allow reviewing of results under different test conditions.

    • UPM1 and 2 errors indicating head forward and reflected power levels of 2 W or less may be logged during body stress power test. These do not interrupt test execution and can be ignored.

    • This tool is not a substitute for the 3.0T RF power measurement kit.

    • Watch the <Stop Scan > button after starting the test to confirm that it 'backlights' after approximately 40 seconds. Failure to 'backlight' means that the RF amp did not pulse.

    • The <Stop Scan> button should remain backlit for approximately 180 seconds for each test selected. If the backlight extinguishes too soon, check the error log, as the amp has likely faulted. Wait for the test to end, reduce TG by 5 to 30 counts and begin the test again. Repeat TG reductions as necessary until the test completes.

    • Some failures only happen at higher power levels. Try running the test with TG levels just below the point where the amp faults.

    • At a TG setting of 170, peak output power should be half of the output seen when measured at a TG setting of 200. Example: If the output power was adjusted to 35kw for a TG = 200, then at a TG setting of 170, the reported body peak forward power output should be approximately 17.5kw. Reported average forward power and peak reflected power should likewise scale the same way.

    • Tests run with a dummy load attached to body or head output usually result in reported VSWR closer to 1.00. The closer the load matches the transmission line and amplifier, the closer the reported VSWR will be to 1.00.

5  Finalization

  1. Click [EXIT] to quit the tool.

  1. Remove all coils and phantoms from the patient table.

  1. Remove dummy load if connected.

    1. Disable RF input to amplifier by placing the RF ENB Switch in Disable (down) position.

    1. Remove load and reattach transmit line to RF amplifier.

    1. Enable TR and DD fault monitoring at driver module. (See Illustration 4.)

    1. Place RF ENB Switch in Enable (up) position.

  1. Perform Head or Body scan to confirm system functionality.
