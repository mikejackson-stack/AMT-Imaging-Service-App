HEC Troubleshooting

This document describes various conditions for the HEC and possible solutions for those conditions.

NOTICE
Avoid leaks by checking that the bleeder valves on the front of each pump in the bottom of the HEC are fully closed before powering pumps on for the first time.
 
 

Table 1:  HEC Troubleshooting

For this condition:Follow these steps:
Difficulty navigating or interpreting HEC screens.Section 5.3 of Heat Exchanger Theory describes how to navigate the screens. Procedures for setting parameters for the HEC are described in subsequent sections of that document.
HEC display shows zero cryogen compressor coolant flow despite customer coolant flowing through the HEC and the cryocompressor receiving adequate flow.A TPS Reset will download new firmware to the HEC. After the first successful TPS Reset, recheck the HEC display for the coolant flow value.
The HEC display shows dashes instead of values for Gradient or Power Electronics coolant flow.Check the red VFD display of the pump on the circuit in question. Make sure the display is not dark and that the circuit breaker above it is not turned off.

If the VFD display is turned on, the issue most likely is with the PE or GC pressure sensors:

  • If the HEC display shows either the Gradient or Power Electronics coolant flow value, the sensor for the value that is being shown is functioning. Swap the PE (J4) and GC (J3) pressure sensors at the signal box and see if the values change.
  • The pressure sensors are FRUs and are listed in the FRU manual.
  • Check the wire crimps at the connectors of any suspected faulty sensors.
The system displays messages saying that the HEC coolant reservoir is low. The HEC display appears to confirm this.There are upper and lower pressure-sensitive level sensors mounted on the right side of each reservoir tank. These sensors close (turn on) when the coolant level is high enough over the sensors (usually three inches, but sometimes the height must be as much as five inches).

You can measure the switch continuity with a DMM (switch closed, low resistance/switch open, very high resistance).

Add coolant to the reservoir until the coolant level is four to five inches above the sensors. See HEC Coolant Fill and Coolant Leak Check. PE or GC should stop reporting the warning when the increase in coolant pressure causes the switches to engage.

Rarely, the pressure sensors become stuck. Stuck sensors can be freed by tapping them with a finger. Do not use anything with a greater mass to tap the sensors.

The system displays messages saying coolant resistivity is too low.New systems have a coolant resistivity between 1.5 kOhm-cm and 15.0 kOhm-cm. The system reports a warning when resistivity falls below 3.5 kOhm-cm and inhibits a scan below 2.5 kOhm-cm.

Deionizing the coolant will restore the proper resistivity. See Heat Exchange Cabinet Coolant Deionization.

Deionizing the coolant removes coolant contaminants, rust inhibitors, and biocide additives. This increases coolant resistivity up to 950 kOhm-cm or higher. Re-adding the chemical additives to the deionized coolant will decrease the resistivity back to the normal 1.5 kOhm-cm to 15.0 kOhm-cm range.

The system displays pressure or cooling errors for the HEC.Any deviations from specifications for pressure or cooling are stored in the system error log.

See the Facility Coolant Requirements section of Direction 5500101, Discovery MR750 3.0T Preinstallation Manual or Direction 5500109, Discovery MR450 1.5T Preinstallation Manual for the HEC pressure and cooling requirements.

If the system returns error 2268904:

  • The error is displayed if the temperature exceeds the upper temperature limit or falls below the lower temperature limit. These are ± 2° C from a set-point of 14° C in MR750/450 and ± 2° C from a set-point of 18° C in MR450w.
  • See the HEC block diagram. The temperature sensor that is detecting this error is located in the top manifold of the chilled-air HEC blower assembly. This sensor rarely fails.
  • This error occurs commonly if the facility coolant cooling capacity is inadequate. Check the facility parameters on the HEC to make sure they are within specification.
    NOTE:See Heat Exchanger Theory for information about navigating the HEC screens.

    1. From the Main Menu of the HEC LED screen, select Monitor → Facility.
    2. Record the Facility Temperature, Inlet Pressure, and Differential Pressure values.
    3. See the Facility Coolant Requirements section of Direction 5500101, Discovery MR750 3.0T Preinstallation Manual or Direction 5500109, Discovery MR450 1.5T Preinstallation Manual for the HEC pressure and cooling requirements.
    4. If the Facility parameters do not meet specifications, have the customer fix the coolant flow or temperature issue. (The in-line filter may require replacement.)
    5. If the Facility parameters meet specifications, check the mixing valve values
      1. From the Main screen of the HEC LED display, select Diags → PID Monitor.
      2. Record the Blower Mixing Valve Percent Open value.
      3. Typically, this valve is at around 50% open. If the value is at one extreme or the other (around 0% or around 100%), check that the mixing valve is not stuck. (It is more likely that the facility coolant temperature is not well controlled by the customer's chiller.) Call the OLC before ordering parts for this problem.
    6. If the facility parameters are within specification and the mixing valve is operating normally:
      1. Check to see whether the error condition always corrects within 1 to three seconds, or whether a TPS Reset is always needed to correct the error condition.
      2. If either of these conditions is true, contact the OLC and request that the cooling.xml file be examined for spiking.

Refer to HEC Data Viewer for additional information on analyzing trends.

Refer to the Lytron vendor manual (GE part number 5161931) for vendor specifications.

Suspected bad pressure sensor.It is important to determine whether the sensor or the Signal Box (PLC) is actually faulty.
If you are testing an HEC facility coolant inlet or outlet sensor:
  1. Note the facility inlet and outlet pressure readings from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Swap J5 and J6 on the Signal Box and confirm the facility inlet and outlet pressure readings are swapped on the HEC LCD screen. If they are swapped, the sensor is faulty.
If you are testing a GC pressure sensor:
  1. Note the GC inlet pressure reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Swap J3 and J6 on the Signal Box and confirm the same pressure reading on the Facility Inlet Pressure on the HEC LCD screen.
If you are trying to test a PE pressure sensor:
  1. Note the PE inlet pressure reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Swap J4 and J6 and confirm the same pressure reading on Facility Inlet Pressure on the HEC LCD screen.
    NOTE:Alternatively, you can test GC and PE pressure sensors by swapping J4 with J5 (Facility Outlet Pressure) instead of swapping J4 and J6.

Suspected bad temperature sensor.It is important to determine whether the sensor or the Signal Box (PLC) is actually faulty.
If you are testing the HEC facility coolant inlet sensor:
  1. Note the Facility inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Note the GC inlet temperature reading from HEC LCD screen.
    NOTE:The HEC will stop pumping when the GC sensor is disconnected. Pumps will restart when the sensor is reconnected.

  3. Swap J18 (Facility) and J17 (GC) on the Signal Box, and confirm that the Facility temperature is now visible for GC on the HEC LCD screen, and that the GC temperature is now visible for Facility on the HEC LCD screen.
If you are testing the GC temperature sensor:
  1. Note the Facility inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Note the GC inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
    NOTE:The HEC will stop pumping when the GC sensor is disconnected. Pumps will restart when the sensor is reconnected.

  3. Swap J18 (Facility) and J17 (GC) on the Signal Box, and confirm that the Facility temperature is now visible for GC on the HEC LCD screen, and that the GC temperature is now visible for Facility on HEC LCD screen.

If you are testing the PE temperature sensor:

  1. Note the Facility inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Note the PE inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
    NOTE:The HEC will stop pumping when the PE sensor is disconnected. Pumps will restart when the sensor is reconnected.

  3. Swap J18 (Facility) and J16 (PE) on the Signal Box, and confirm that the Facility temperature is now visible for PE on the HEC LCD screen, and that the PE temperature is now visible for Facility on HEC LCD screen.
If you are testing the Blower temperature sensor:
  1. Note the Facility inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  2. Note the Blower inlet temperature reading from the HEC LCD screen.
  3. Move the connector at J18 (Facility) to J28 (Blower) on the Signal Box, and confirm that the Blower temperature is now visible for Facility temperature on the HEC LCD screen.
    NOTE:You can move the connector at J28 to J18 to make the temperature check the other way. However, this may require cutting the tie wraps for the J28 cable to reach the J18 connector.

Circuit breaker trips when power is applied to the HEC. Circuit breaker or fuses in Main Disconnect Panel may trip at same time.Examine the HEC for short circuits:
  1. LOTO the HEC.
  2. Open the HEC Power Box, and disconnect pump or blower wiring from the VFD.
  3. Use a DVM to measure resistance between the pump or blower lines (phase-to-phase) and from each line to ground (phase-to-ground) for short circuits.
  4. Use a DVM to measure resistance between the VFD input and output connections (phase-to-phase) and from each line to ground (phase-to-ground) for short circuits.
  5. Use a DVM to measure resistance between the BLW CB circuit breaker input and output connections (phase-to-phase) and each connection to ground (phase-to-ground) for short circuits.

If there are no short circuits observed, order and replace the HEC Power Box.

NOTE:GC and PE pumps may be swapped between VFDs for troubleshooting purposes only.

Location of the cryogen compressor flowmeter is different than in previously encountered HECs.Previous HECs measured cryogen flow from the inlet line. Current production HECs measure cryogen flow from the outlet line. This change results in no difference in the ability of Magnet Monitor 3 units to measure cryogen flow accurately.
There is insufficient water flow to or from components in the PGR cabinet.
  1. Check for kinks in the supply and return hoses, especially where hoses are behind units. You can often quickly clear a kink by twisting a pressurized hose a full rotation in one direction and then twisting it in the opposite direction.
  2. Use a discharge hose and a flask or bucket as troubleshooting tools to check for coolant flow on the supply and return hoses and through components.
    NOTICE
    The water cooling system for the PGR cabinet is rated to 65 psi.
     
     

  3. Use a high volume pump to try to clear blockages in components. Try the return side first, then the supply side. You may need to repeat this a few times to get the blockage to clear.
Need to view HEC parameters in real-time.The system uses event-based polling to monitor the HEC, storing the data it receives to the cooling.xml log file.

Open a c-shell and type:

more /usr/g/service/log/paramdata/cooling.xml

Each output describes units in which the value is expressed. You must pay attention to the units and scaling.

Refer to HEC Data Viewer for additional information on analyzing trends.

At power up, one of the GC or PE coolant pumps is not operating. The VFD for the pump displays 0.Check to see if a pump or the Signal Box has failed:
  1. Swap pump connections between VFDs.
  2. If previously non-working pump now operates with other VFD, swap input Signal Box control lines between VFDs.
  3. If after swapping the control lines the pump is not operating, replace the Signal Box.
  4. If the pump never operates during these tests, replace the pump.
After initial power up, the system reports the GC coolant resistivity is out of range (too low or too high).If the GC resistivity value is less than 2.5 kOhm-cm and greater than 1 kOhm-cm, de-ionize the coolant. See Heat Exchange Cabinet Coolant Deionization.

A GC resistivity value greater than 20.0 kOhm-cm may mean an FE forgot to add rust inhibitor and biocide chemicals after performing the last de-ionization procedure. Perform de-ionization again and insure these are added per procedure. If values are unchanged after adding additives then proceed to “If Fuse OK” section below.

A GC resistivity value less than 1 kOhm-cm may indicate faulty hardware:

  • Check the PLC code version:
    1. From the main Signal Box PLC screen, press Diags. The code version is a date code and will look like G3.MM.DD.YYYY.
    2. If the code version is anything earlier than G3.02.16.2009 and the HEC only has one DI sensor in the GC tank, wait until the MR host computer has been connected to the HEC and the first successful TPS Reset is completed. This will download version G3.02.16.2009 or newer.
    3. Recheck resistivity value.
  • Check the condition of the DI circuit board fuse.
    1. Remove the HEC Signal Box cover and check the condition of the fuse located in the upper left corner of the DI circuit board.
      NOTE:A spare fuse is usually provided in a plastic bag inside the Signal Box. If the fuse is unavailable, order 5266827 Fuse FRU Kit.

    2. If the fuse is open:
      1. View the voltage selector toggle switch setting through the view holes on front of the HEC Power Box (this may require a flashlight).
      2. Confirm that the switch is in the correct position for incoming voltage to the HEC. See Section 4.3 of Power-On Sequencing.
    3. If the fuse is intact:
      1. Note the resistivity value reported for the Gradient circuit cooling circuit on the HEC LED screen.
      2. Disconnect the resistivity sensor DI1 from J23 on the side of the Signal Box.
      3. Recheck the resistivity value on the HEC LED screen. It should read 0.01kOhm-cm.
      4. If removing resistivity sensor DI1 does not result in a significant change in the resistivity value, replace the Signal Box.
        NOTE:During operation, a good working DI sensor will measure approximately 1.2 VDC (corresponds to ~12.0 kOhm-cm resistivity) across the white (+) and black wires (-). A faulty, open sensor will measure ~2.5 VDC. A faulty, shorted sensor will measure much less than 1.2 VDC.

        Early units have a second sensor (J10 on the Signal Box), later units have only a single sensor. The second sensor is not measured (not used) in systems with 20.0 M4 software and later (Signal Box PLC code versions G3.02.16.2009 and newer. Check the code revision on HEC Signal Box PLC by pressing Diags on the Main Screen).

The cable dressing for the HEC Signal Box appears different.There are two versions of the cable dressing for the HEC Signal Box. The newer version is used in HEC version 11 and higher and Signal Box version 2 and higher.

To determine the version of the HEC, locate the rating label for the HEC behind the left side front door on the floor of the cabinet.

To determine the version of the Signal Box, locate the label on the side of the Signal Box. If there is no label, the Signal Box is an early model.

The mixing valve reading on the Signal Box LCD screen is at 100% or at 0%.A reading of 100% means:
  • The maximum amount of coolant is being passed through the heat exchanger.
  • The stem on the mixing valve is completely down so that the stem is almost flush with the mixing valve case.
  • The PLC PID value for the mixing valve position is 4094 (this value is not displayed).

A reading of 0% means:

  • The minimum amount of coolant is being passed through the heat exchanger (the maximum amount of coolant is being passed around the heat exchanger).
  • The stem of the mixing valve is at the maximum height above the mixing valve case.
  • The PLC PID value for the mixing valve position is 0 (this value is not displayed).
HEC Blower circuit breaker trips and needs to be manually reset.The blower may be drawing excessive current because of a blockage. Check the condition of the air filter on the PEN wall in the scan room. Clean or replace if it is dirty.

Additionally, there are hoses internal to the HEC that connect to the blower. It may be necessary to remove the suction hose (the one not connected to the liquid to air heat exchanger) and check if the internal wall of the hose is still intact and not delaminated or clogging airflow.
