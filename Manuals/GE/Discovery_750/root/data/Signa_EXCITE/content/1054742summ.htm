1  Procedure

1.1   AMP Diagnostics

  1. The system status of the AMT amplifier is displayed on the front panel of the AMT. The locations of the front panel light emitting diode (LED) indicators supply are shown in Illustration 1.

    (Step Details)

  1. The amplifier front panel indicators are:

    1. POWER: Indicates the presence of DC voltage in the control module. This indicator is lit when the circuit breaker is in the ON position.

    1. STANDBY: When lit (and the READY LED is unlit), the amplifier is in the standby state.

    1. READY: Indicates the amplifier is in an operational state.

    1. UNBLANK: The amplifier is receiving UNBLANK commands from J1 or J2.

    1. FAULT: Indicates the unit detects a fault. Refer to the status LEDs and/or the status code.

1.3   Problem / Solution Table

  1. In many cases, problems with the AMT display through fault codes. Six STATUS CODE LED indicators, labeled left-to-right 5 to 0, are on the front of the cabinet. These indicators (as pictured in Illustration 1) display status and fault codes.

  1. Within five seconds of power-up, the correct fault status displays. During this five second power-up interval, the fault status may not correctly display. All faults cause the system to shut down by going into the standby mode. In response to a fault, readback from the amplifier the controlling system may assert a Standby condition through the J1 connector. Therefore, the status LEDs mask out this condition during a fault and the only means of recovering from a fault condition is to RESET the system by cycling the AC power.

  1. Table 1 shows fault codes, a description of each fault, the recommended action and, if applicable, necessary replacement parts to overcome the fault that can be seen via the Fault Code LEDs. Table 2 shows a list of problems, probable causes and solution recommendations

1.4   Troubleshooting for Low Gain

This section provides troubleshooting help to find a failed FRU that does not show an LED fault code. This type of failure produces system symptoms of low gain or no RF output. Follow this procedure only after completing the steps listed in Table 3 and Table 2.

NOTE:Use a 100 MHz scope (and 1.5T RF Power Measurement Kit) for this troubleshooting procedure.

NOTE:Do not use the 50-Ohm feed-through terminator in the oscilloscope. Use the 50-Ohm selectable termination from the kit.
