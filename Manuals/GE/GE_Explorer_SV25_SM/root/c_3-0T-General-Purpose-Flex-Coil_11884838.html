Introduction

Topic ID: id_SL2164264-1139718

Product Identification and Shipping List

The 3.0T General Purpose (GP) Flex Coil, M1385AR, consists of one major assembly. The unit is 56 cm (22 inch) long (not including straps) by 22 cm (8.7 inch) wide. It has two mesh windows that indicate the open center of the two coils and aid in positioning. Two 76 cm (30 inch) straps are attached to one end of the coil fabric. The undersides of these straps are covered with Velcro that attach to a Velcro hook strip at the other end of the coil and to four Velcro hooks located above and below the mesh windows. A 183 cm (72 inch) RF cable exits the material on one of the long sides and has a 3.0T surface coil quick disconnect (QD) box that is connected to the MR system.

Note:

The QD box is located at the end of the RF cable.

Theory of Operation

The GP flex coil design is a linear, receive-only flexible coil. It consists of two 13 cm x 17 cm (5 inch x 6.5 inch) loops that are serially connected to create a co-rotating, "saddle coil" pair. If the loops are positioned in a more open fashion (move farther away from each other toward a planar or flat configuration), the image uniformity decreases. For close positioning, do not overlap the coil ends. Doing so will not damage the coil, but performance will suffer.

When aligned properly, this saddle design provides exceptional uniformity across the sensitive volume of the coil and a minimum of bright spots or high-intensity areas. For optimal positioning, the circular shape should be maintained with the two mesh windows 10 cm (4 inch) apart and the coil ends 5 cm (2 inch) apart. Some deviation from the optimal position will not significantly degrade the uniformity. Therefore, this coil can be used in anatomical areas such as the hip, brachial plexus, larger knees and ankle.

  • The coil consists of one major assembly. The two loops are in a flexible material covered with fabric. This allows the GP flex coil to bend in one direction, making it easy to wrap around the anatomy of interest.

  • The block diagram of the antenna electronics is shown below. The two flex PCB wings on either side of the rigid PCB form the loops. The rigid PCB has a matching network that matches the loaded antenna to 50 ohms and an active blocking network. The blocking network consists of a single PIN diode and a tuned LC network. The diode is forward biased whenever the body coil transmits. The external cable is connected to the antenna via a right angle SMA jack. The flex PCB has passive blocking networks that consist of a Schottky diode and a tuned LC network.

Explanation of Procedure

SNR measurements for the GP flex coil require sets of signal and noise scans. Refer to the data sheet in SNR Data Sheet to understand the data required to calculate the SNR.

The Image Quality Check uses two different protocols for signal and noise image acquisition. The signal scan is an FSE sequence used to minimize susceptibility and B0 inhomogeneity effects. The noise scan is a GRE sequence that has a Control Variable (do_noise) to eliminate the transmit RF completely during the scan. The signal scan must be run before the noise scan because the R1, R2, and TG values from the signal scan are used for the noise scan.

SNR Measurement

Regions of interest in both signal and noise images can be measured directly in the image browser. Click Measure, select the circular or rectangular shape, and adjust its size and orientation when the shape is displayed in the selected image. Mean, standard deviation, and area of region of interest (ROI) display in the lower right corner of the image.

Note:

The SNR calculation uses the mean of the signal image and standard deviation of the noise image. SNR is measured for each element, not on the composite image.

For the signal measurement, choose an ROI covering approximately 80% of the phantom. Also measure the noise with an ROI covering 80% of the field of view (FOV). ROI should be taken as circle centered at the phantom. Examples of typical ROIs are shown below.

External Cable and Pin Diode Check

There is only one PIN diode in this antenna. The following procedure indicates if either the PIN diode or external cable is defective.

CAUTION
Safety Hazard!
Due to safety hazards associated with soldering in the field, PIN diodes are no longer FRUs.
If the coil has a defective PIN diode, the respective component MUST be replaced.

  1. Select the Diode Test function on the digital multimeter (DMM).

  2. Connect the positive lead of the DMM to the center pin of pin A1 on the QD box connected to the RF cable, and connect the negative lead to the RF shield/ground of pin A1 on the QD box. Observe the reading on the DMM.

    • If the reading is between 0.400 and 0.600 with no instability or fluctuations, thecable and pin diode are good.

    • If there are fluctuations between 0.400 and 0.600, the cable is defective.

    • If the reading is less than 0.400, either the output cable is shorted or the pin diode is defective.

    • If the reading is greater than 0.600, the pin diode is defective.

  3. Connect the positive lead of the DMM to the lead of the RF shield/ground of pin A1 on the QD box connected to the RF cable, and connect the negative lead to the center pin of pin A1 on the QD box.

    1. Observe the reading on the DMM. If the DMM displays INFINITY with no instability or fluctuations, the cable and pin diode are good.

    2. Swap the positive and negative leads. If a reading of INFINITY is observed in both directions, either the output cable or pin diode is open.

  4. If the cable fails any of the above tests, replace it. Refer to External Cable Replacement.

Maintenance

Topic ID: id_SL2164323-1139718

Coil Care

Warning
Detach the coil connector from the scanner before attempting to clean it.
do not reattach after cleaning until the coil dries completely.
having the coil attached to the system during cleaning or when it is wet may result in electrical shock.
CAUTION
Do not spray or pour cleaning solution directly on the coil.
Do not submerge the coil in solution.
The coil contains sensitive electronics components that could be damaged by the solution.

If necessary, a 10% bleach solution can be used for more rigorous cleaning; however, some discoloration of the strap, cover, or pad may occur.

Replacement

Simple removals that are clearly obvious are not described here. Unless otherwise noted, the steps for reassembly are the reverse of the steps for disassembly.

Topic ID: id_SL2164242-1139718

Assembly/Disassembly of Coil

Refer to the illustrations that follow to disassemble the coil.

To reassemble the coil:

  1. Place the cover back on the posts and press on it until it snaps into place.

  2. Push the foam flap under the jacket.

  3. Carefully put the fabric flap into place, ensuring the Velcro on the edges lines up with the Velcro on the jacket. Tuck the end of the flap under the jacket.

  4. Press on the Velcro while ensuring that no edges are left open.

Topic ID: id_SL2164221-1139718

External Cable Replacement

  1. Disassemble the coil as instructed above. Unscrew the two nylon screws and remove the strain relief upper cover.

  2. Using two 8 mm wrenches, one on the circuit board female connector and one on the male cable connector, unscrew and remove the male SMA cable connector from the circuit board. Be careful not to twist the female SMA connector from the circuit board.

  3. Remove the external cable from the coil.

  4. Install the new cable. Stabilize the circuit board and the connector on the circuit board.

    Note:

    Too much torque (greater than 2 kgf-cm/8 in.-lb) can cause damage.

  5. Reattach the strain relief, and reassemble the coil.
