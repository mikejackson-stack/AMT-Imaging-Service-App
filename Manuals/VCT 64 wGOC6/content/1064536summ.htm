No Load kV Test

(Click Here to Return to Detailed Procedure)

1  Safety

WARNING
Potential for ShocK.
Voltage may be present.
Use proper lockout/tagout procedures before replacing components.

2  Required Conditions

ConditionReferenceEffectivity

PPC control board alive and running : DS17 - DS24 LEDs are lit successively or a combination of them blink.

-

-

3  Procedure

  1. First run the no-load kV test with the tube connected. This will not harm the system. If it passes, move on to the next troubleshooting step. If the test FAILS, continue performing the procedure to isolate between tank and tube.

WARNING
Potential for ShocK.
Voltage may be present.
Use proper lockout/tagout procedures before replacing components.

  1. Remove right side gantry cover.

  1. Turn OFF the 120 VAC, AXIAL DRIVE ENABLE, and HVDC ENABLE switches on the Service Switch Panel.

  1. Remove the right top gantry cover.

  1. Remove HV cable candlestick from tank receptacle.

NOTICE
Equipment damage possible. You must fill the receptacle with oil to avoid arcing.
 

  1. Fill tank receptacle with oil.

  1. Power on the generator by turning ON the 120 VAC and HVDC ENABLE switches on the Service Switch Panel. Also press ESTOP DRIVES RESET.

WARNING
Ensure that the 120 VAC Axial Drive Enable Switch is in the off position.

  1. If desired, at this point you may check the following:

    1. HVDC input to the Inverter in an acceptable range: 380 to 750 VDC

    1. PPC control board working: DS17-DS24 LEDs are lit successively or a combination of them blink

  1. Launch the [GENERATOR TOOL]. See tools section for more information.

  1. Launch the [No Load kV Diagnostic] tool.

  1. Run the diagnostic.

4  Finalization

  1. It may be necessary to combine the results of the No Load kV test with the two other major JEDI tests (Shorted Inverter / Gate Command tests). This is because the Inverter and controls the HV tank and Tube and problems in either the Inverter or Aux Box could cause the No Load kV test to fail. However, on its own, the No Load kV test can indicate:

    1. No Load kV test FAILS (with Candlestick disconnected) - likely HV Tank problem

    1. No Load kV test first FAILS (with Candlestick connected) then PASSES (with Candlestick disconnected) - likely X-Ray tube problem.

  1. Securing HV Cable
