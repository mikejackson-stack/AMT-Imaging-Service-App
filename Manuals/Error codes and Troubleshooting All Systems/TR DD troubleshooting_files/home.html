3.1   TR Open Circuit Fault – Body

If the system is reporting a T/R Open Circuit Fault along the Body line, troubleshooting must occur to isolate it to the failed component. Illustration 1 shows a path for the TR signals.

NOTE:In the unlikely situation both a Body and Head TR Open occur at the same time, verify the T/R cables have been connected throughout the chain. If Failure still occurs, check the power supplies in the Driver Module.

  1. Swap the Head (J6) and Body (J7) T/R output cables at the Driver Module, and rerun the Diagnostic to determine if the failure changes.

    NOTE:You will continue to have a Body T/R open fault in this scenario due to the difference in Head to Body output signals. The important factor is what happens with the Head T/R line.

    1. If the Body still reports a T/R Open, and the head does not report any failure, the problem is within the Driver Module. Replace the driver module, refer to the Driver Module Replacementprocedure.

    1. If the Head now reports a T/R Open, the problem is somewhere further along the Body T/R path. Do NOT replace the Driver Module – continue to troubleshoot.

    1. Return the Driver Module Head and Body T/R output cables to their proper connection points.

  1. Swap the Head (J9) and Body (J10) T/R Input cables at the RF Amplifier, and rerun the Diagnostic to determine if the failure changes.

    1. If the Body still reports a T/R Open, and the head does not report any failure, the problem is within the cable or connection between the Driver Module and RF Amplifier. Verify the connection integrity, and if all connections are good, but still getting an error, replace the cable.

3.2   TR Open Circuit Fault – Head

If the system is reporting a T/R Open Circuit Fault along the Head path, troubleshooting must occur to isolate it to the failed component. Illustration 2 shows a path for the TR signals. There are differences between an upgraded system, which still has the Legacy connector, and a forward production system with the new LPCA. An upgraded system utilizes a RF TX switch on the inside of the Pen-Panel to split the head signal into two. One supplies the Head Port, while the other supplies Connector A, as shown in Illustration 2. In a forward production system, the splitter is bypassed, and not used, so there is only one head signal in the Magnet room. See Illustration 3. Also the Head TR Switching circuitry is built into the Split Head coil cable connector.

  1. Swap the Head (J6) and Body (J7) T/R output cables at the Driver Module, and rerun the Diagnostic to determine if the failure changes.

    NOTE:You will also receive a Head and Body TR Cable Swap error when swapping the cables, if the true open is further down the Head T/R line.

    1. If the Head still reports a T/R Open, the problem is within the Driver Module. Replace the driver module, refer to the Driver Module Replacement procedure.

    1. If the Body now reports a T/R Open, and the Head does not report any failure, the problem is somewhere further along the Head T/R path. Do NOT replace the Driver Module – continue to troubleshoot.

    1. Return the Driver Module Head and Body T/R output cables to their proper connection points.

    (Step Details)

  1. Swap the Head (J9) and Body (J10) T/R Input cables at the RF Amplifier, and rerun the Diagnostic to determine if the failure changes.

    NOTE:You will also receive a Head and Body TR Cable Swap error when swapping the cables, if the true open is further down the Head T/R line.

    1. If the Head still reports a T/R Open, the problem is within the cable or connection between the Driver Module and RF Amplifier. Verify the connection integrity. Trace connection back to Driver Module and confirm that this really is Head TR cable and not Body TR cable. If all connections are good, but still getting an error, replace the cable.

    1. If the Body now reports a T/R Open, the problem is somewhere further along the Head T/R path. Continue to troubleshoot the other components.

    1. Return the Head and Body T/R RF Amplifier input cables to their proper connection points.

3.3   TR Open Circuit Fault – MNS

    1. If the MNS line still reports a T/R Open, and the body does not report any failure, the problem is within the cable or connection between the Driver Module and MNS RF Amplifier. Verify the connection integrity, and if all connections are good, but still getting an error, replace the cable.

    1. If the Body path now reports a T/R Open, the problem is somewhere further along the MNS T/R path. Continue to troubleshoot.

    1. Return the MNS and Body T/R RF Amplifier input cables to their proper connection points.

3.12   MC Receive Fault

If the diagnostic is detecting a MC Receive Fault, it is detecting current of greater than +100 mA during receive mode. The most likely cause of this failure is a MC Driver in the Driver Module. Replace the Driver Module, refer to the Driver Module Replacement procedure.

  • NOTE:In rare instances this error has been received due to instability in the MC bias path caused by a capacitor in the circuit. If this error only occurs on one coil, there is a small chance it could be the coil circuitry causing the fault.

3.13   MC Transmit Fault

If the diagnostic is detecting a MC Transmit Fault, it is detecting negative current of less than -50 mA during transmit mode. The most likely cause of this failure is a Drivers or Monitoring Circuit within the Driver Module. Replace the Driver Module, refer to the Driver Module Replacement procedure.

  • NOTE:In rare instances this error has been received due to instability in the MC bias path caused by a capacitor in the circuit. If this error only occurs on one coil, there is a small chance it could be the coil circuitry causing the fault.

3.14   Head and Body TR Cable Swap

The system should be able to detect if the Body and Head T/R cables have been swapped, based on the difference in voltages. If this error is received, check the cabling at both the output of the Driver Module (J6 and J7), as well as at the input of the RF Amplifier (J9 and J10). At the output of the Amplifier, the cables are no longer the same cable connector, so they will not be swapped anywhere else along the chain.

3.15   Head and Connector A Cable Swap

Upgrade systems: the Head T/R signal is split into two paths at the RF TX Switch on the Pen-panel. If this error is received, check the cabling at both the Output of the RF TX Switch (J151 and J153), as well as at the Cable I/F Panel at the Rear Pedestal (J1 and J2).

3.16   MC Cable Swap

Upgrade systems: the Diagnostic can detect potential MC Cable swaps along the MC Paths. If this error is received, check the cabling at the back of the Driver Module (J5 and J10), cables at the Pen Panel (J203 and J78), and cables at the 16-Channel Switch (J11 and J10).

3.17   MC Cable Disconnected

The Diagnostic can detect when an MC Cable is disconnected along the MC Paths. If this error is received, check the cabling at the back of the Driver Module (J5 and J10), cables at the Pen Panel (J203 and J78), and cables at the 16-Channel Switch (J11 and J10).

  1. Complete one Head, Body, and MNS (if applicable) scan to verify system functionality.
