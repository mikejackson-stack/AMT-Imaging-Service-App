X-Ray Tube Faults

Siemens CT tube faults are logged with severity levels (1–4). Level 4 faults require tube replacement — lower levels may be addressed by conditioning or cooling. Always review the full error log in syngo Service Mode (SSM) before concluding tube failure.
Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
Tube heat unit (HU) limit / thermal shutdownTube thermal capacity exceeded; WCS flow or temperature out of specAllow tube to cool per software HU indicator. Run tube warm-up: SSM → Tube → Warm-Up. Verify WCS supply temperature (<17°C typical) and flow rate. Check WCS for faults.
Tube arcing / kV fault during scanTube aging; vacuum loss; HV cable trackingCheck tube arc count in SSM → Tube History. Run tube conditioning sequence. If arcing persists above threshold, tube replacement required. Inspect HV cable candles for carbon tracking.
Tube rotor noise (rough or loud)Anode bearing wear — near end of tube lifeDocument rotor noise in service log. Check mAs count against tube life expectancy. Schedule tube replacement. Loud metallic rotor noise = imminent failure.
Mylar window damage / contaminationPhysical damage or contrast spray onto windowInspect mylar window per PM. A torn or contaminated window causes beam quality changes and image artifacts. Replace mylar window per service manual — do not operate with torn window.
Tube cool-down excessive (very slow HU recovery)WCS pump running but heat exchanger fouled; low coolant flowMeasure WCS flow rate at gantry connection. Flush heat exchanger per PM procedure. Check WCS coolant level (sight glass on WCS cabinet). Refill and bleed per WCS service manual.

High Voltage (HV) System Faults

HIGH VOLTAGE — Siemens CT HV systems generate 80–140 kVp. Capacitor banks retain charge after shutdown. Allow full discharge time (per service manual, typically 5+ minutes) and verify with meter before touching HV components.
Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
HV inverter faultHV inverter IGBT failure; DC bus fault; overvoltage protectionReview error in SSM — note sub-code. Check DC bus voltage to HV inverter. Inspect IGBT module condition. Run HV self-test in SSM → HV → Test. Replace inverter module if fault persists.
kVp out of specificationHV calibration drift; feedback sensor faultMeasure kVp with dose meter or non-contact probe per service manual. Run kVp calibration in SSM. If calibration cannot correct, HV feedback board replacement may be required.
HV fault — arc protection trippedTube arcing; HV cable tracking; HV tank insulation breakdownInspect HV cable routing and candle connections — clean or replace if arc tracking visible. Check HV tank oil level. Run tube conditioning. Replace HV cable or tank if arcing continues.
mA (tube current) faultmA regulation failure; filament circuit issue; kV/mA control card faultCheck mA feedback circuit per service manual test points. Measure filament supply voltage. Run mA calibration test in SSM. Replace mA control card if out of spec.

Data Acquisition System (DAS) Faults

Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
DAS communication faultDAS fiber optic link failure; slip ring data contact fault; DAS board failureInspect DAS fiber optic cable at slip ring junction. Check slip ring data contact condition. Run DAS self-test in SSM → DAS → Diagnostics. Replace fiber or DAS board as indicated.
Ring artifact in reconstructed imagesSingle failed detector element or channel offsetRun air calibration (Quality Constancy). Review DAS bad channel map. Persistent ring = specific detector channel failed — replace detector module. Note: single bad channel = narrow ring; multiple = thick band.
DAS overtemperature faultDAS filter clogged; cooling fan failure; high ambient temp in gantryClean DAS air filter (quarterly PM item). Check DAS fan operation. Verify scan room ambient temperature. Allow cooldown before restarting.
Detector window contaminationContrast or dust on UHR / detector windowClean detector face per PM procedure with OEM-approved cleaner. Do not use abrasive materials. Inspect mylar window integrity.

Water Cooling System (WCS) Faults

The WCS is unique to Siemens CT — it cools the tube, HV inverter, and gantry electronics using a closed-loop water circuit. WCS faults are one of the most common Siemens CT service calls and are easily prevented with proper PM.
Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
WCS low coolant level alarmGradual evaporation loss; small leak in systemCheck WCS sight glass. Refill with distilled water + OEM inhibitor to proper level. Inspect all hose fittings inside gantry and WCS cabinet for weeping. Bleed system per service manual.
WCS overtemperature faultFacility cooling water temp too high; heat exchanger fouled; low flowCheck facility water supply temperature (must be <17°C typical). Measure WCS flow rate. Flush heat exchanger if fouled. Verify WCS pump is running at correct speed.
WCS low pressure / flow faultWCS pump failure; clogged filter; internal leakCheck WCS pump operation. Inspect WCS filter — replace if clogged. Pressure-check system for internal leaks. Replace WCS pump if pressure/flow cannot be restored.
Coolant leak visible inside gantryHose fitting failure; coolant block crack in tube or inverterShut down immediately — electrical hazard. Identify and isolate leak source. Clean up coolant before any electrical measurements. Replace fitting or hose. Do not operate with active leak.
WCS inhibitor depletedInhibitor consumed or not replaced at PMTest inhibitor concentration with OEM test strip. Flush and refill with fresh inhibitor if concentration is below specification. Depleted inhibitor causes internal corrosion of WCS passages.

Gantry Faults

Fault / SymptomLikely CauseAction
Gantry rotation fault / will not rotateDrive motor or inverter fault; rotation belt broken; encoder faultCheck drive belt condition. Verify rotation motor inverter. Inspect rotation encoder. Check for obstruction in gantry rotation path. Run rotation test in SSM.
Gantry rotation noiseSlip ring brush wear; bearing wear; gantry imbalanceClean slip rings and inspect brush wear. Check gantry balance (annual PM). Lubricate main bearing per mAs/revolution count in service log.
Gantry tilt faultTilt motor or belt fault; tilt encoder; limit switchVerify tilt operation from service mode. Check tilt limit switches — gantry should stop cleanly at ±30°. Lubricate vertical drive per PM schedule. Run tilt calibration.
Slip ring arcing or brush faultWorn brush blocks; contaminated slip ring surface; high humidityClean slip ring and brush blocks per PM schedule (every 6 months). Inspect brush spring tension. Measure brush length — replace if below minimum. Use OEM-approved cleaner.
PDC protection faultPDC board fault; input power issue; varistor failureCheck PDC cabinet for fault indicators. Inspect varistor block. Verify input supply voltage. Replace PDC protection module if components show damage (burnt varistors).

Quality Constancy (QC) — When to Run

Siemens CT Quality Constancy (QC) replaces air calibration on most platforms. It must be run at the start of each day and after repairs that affect image quality.
TriggerProcedure
Daily QC before clinical useSystem may prompt automatically. If not, run SSM → Quality → Quality Constancy. Phantom is not required for standard daily QC.
After DAS board or detector replacementRun full QC. Also run QA phantom scan to verify image quality.
After tube replacement or warm-upRun QC after tube warm-up to baseline new tube output.
Ring artifact appearsRun QC immediately. If rings persist, run DAS channel diagnostic and identify bad detector.
After site power eventRun QC and phantom scan before returning to clinical use.

Tip: WCS maintenance is the single most impactful PM task on Siemens CT systems. Sites that skip WCS coolant checks consistently see tube thermal faults and shortened tube life. Annual WCS flush + inhibitor refill is non-negotiable.
Tip: Always check SSM → Tube History before concluding tube failure. Tube arc counts, mAs log, and thermal event history tell the story. A tube with 1 million mAs and increasing arcs needs replacement planning — but one with 100K mAs and a single arc event may just need conditioning.
Critical: Siemens CT HV systems retain lethal charge after shutdown. Wait the full capacitor discharge time (see system-specific service manual) and verify with a calibrated HV meter before touching any HV component.
