🌡️ MRI/CT Cooling System & UPS Service Guide
AMT Imaging Solutions — Field Service Quick Reference

Applies to: All GE and Siemens MRI/CT systems. Covers T200 (temperature warning), equipment room HVAC faults, chiller/HEC failures, gradient water cooling, and UPS battery faults. These are cross-platform issues that affect all modalities.

T200 — Temperature Warning

A temperature warning is a pre-failure indicator. Address it immediately — within hours a warning becomes a shutdown. The system is telling you something is running hot before hardware damage occurs.
System AreaCheckAction
Equipment Room HVACIs the equipment room air conditioning functioning? Check supply air temperature at AC vents. Normal equipment room temperature: 16–24°C.Notify facility HVAC if AC is failed or undersized. Temporary portable AC units can prevent shutdown while waiting for repairs. Confirm with site facilities team.
GE HEC (Heat Exchanger Cabinet)Check HEC status panel. Verify chilled water supply temperature at HEC inlet (target: <15°C supply, <20°C return). Check HEC pump is running. Check flow meter reading.If HEC pump failed: check breaker, check HEC pump fuse, inspect motor. If no chilled water supply from building: contact facilities. Clean HEC strainer if flow is low.
Gradient Water CoolingGE and Siemens systems have water cooling for gradient coils. Check gradient cooling water temperature at the system heat exchanger. Normal: supply <20°C.Check coolant deionizer if equipped (GE HEC). Replace DI cartridge if resistivity is low. Check for coolant leaks at gradient coil fittings (inspect under bore). Low flow alarm = blocked strainer or failed pump.
RF Amplifier CoolingRF amplifiers are typically air-cooled with dedicated fans. Check RF amp cabinet fans — all should be spinning. Check amp intake air temperature (<25°C target).Clean RF amp intake filter (clogged filter = reduced airflow = overtemp). Replace failed fans. Verify equipment room temp allows adequate cooling.
Gradient Amplifier CoolingHigh-power gradient amplifiers (GPA/ACGD) generate significant heat. Verify cooling fans in gradient amplifier cabinet are operating. Check for blocked ventilation paths.Clean gradient amplifier cabinet filters. Verify equipment room airflow path is not blocked by equipment placement or cable routing.
Siemens WCS (Water Cooling System)Siemens CT and some MRI systems use a dedicated WCS. Check WCS sight glass (green = normal, red = low). Check WCS pump status and water temperature on the WCS display.Add distilled water if WCS level is low. Check WCS pump — listen for cavitation. If WCS temperature is high: check facility water supply temperature and flow rate to WCS heat exchanger.

Seasonal Temperature Fault Patterns

Time of YearCommon IssueQuick Check
Summer (June–September)Equipment room HVAC undersized for summer heat load. RF amp and gradient amp thermal faults increase dramatically. Most common in older buildings.Verify equipment room temp is ≤24°C at hottest time of day. Check if building HVAC is shared with other areas that increase load in summer.
Winter (December–February)Chilled water from building may be too cold or shut off when outside temp drops — can cause over-cooling or HEC low-flow faults. Rare but happens in cold climates.Check HEC supply water temperature — should not be <10°C. Some buildings switch off chilled water in winter; HEC needs year-round chilled water supply.
After facility renovationHVAC ductwork changes, new walls, or moved equipment can redirect airflow away from equipment room. New equipment added to room increases heat load.Survey equipment room airflow after any renovation. Measure temperature at multiple points — hot spots indicate poor airflow distribution.

UPS Fault — Battery or Inverter Issues

The UPS protects the MRI/CT system from power interruptions that could corrupt data, cause an emergency shutdown, or trigger a magnet quench. A failed UPS battery means one power event can shut down the system mid-scan or worse.
Fault TypeDiagnosisAction
UPS battery low / replace battery warningBattery self-test failed or battery voltage below threshold. Most UPS systems run self-test weekly and report battery health.Check UPS front panel for battery fault code. Measure battery voltage at UPS battery terminals. GE systems use Eaton 9130 or Powerware 9130 UPS — check model for battery part number. Order and replace batteries. Typical battery life: 3–5 years.
UPS on bypass — not providing battery backupUPS has switched to bypass mode — system is running on raw utility power with no battery backup.Check UPS for fault condition that caused bypass. Common causes: overload (too many devices connected), internal inverter fault, or operator-initiated bypass. Resolve fault and return to normal UPS mode before site leaves unattended.
UPS fault alarm — system won't startUPS has a hard fault that prevents normal operation. System startup may be blocked if UPS provides startup power or if system checks UPS status on boot.Check UPS fault code. Common fault codes: input voltage out of range, battery disconnect, internal fault. Cycle UPS power (if safe to do so). Check utility power quality — low voltage or frequency out of range can cause UPS input faults.
System shuts down unexpectedly — no other faultPower micro-interruption not visible on meters but long enough to trigger UPS transfer. Or UPS transfer time exceeds system tolerance.Check UPS event log for transfer history. If transfers are frequent, evaluate utility power quality. Consider power conditioner upstream of UPS if line quality is poor.
UPS transfer time too slowSome older UPS systems have transfer times (5–10 ms) that exceed what sensitive electronics can tolerate. Results in equipment fault or reboot despite UPS being present.Verify UPS transfer time specification for the installed model. GE specifications require UPS transfer ≤4 ms for full protection. Upgrade UPS if transfer time exceeds system requirement.

Cooling & UPS PM Checklist

TaskIntervalNotes

Test HEC pump and flow rateAnnual PMCompare to installation baseline — gradual flow reduction = blocked strainer
Clean gradient amp and RF amp filtersAnnual PMCompressed air, vacuum, or replace — clogged filters are the #1 cause of summer thermal faults

Tip: The most reliable early warning for a cooling problem is temperature trending over multiple PM visits. A room that ran 20°C three years ago and now runs 22°C hasn't failed yet — but it's heading there. Document equipment room temperature at every visit so you can show the facility the trend before the system starts throwing faults.
Tip: Replace UPS batteries proactively every 4 years in clinical environments — don't wait for the battery to fail during a power event. A $300 battery replacement is far cheaper than recovering from a system crash or magnet quench caused by a dead UPS at 2am.
