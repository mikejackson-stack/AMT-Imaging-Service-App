# Exhibit C: Performance Standards

This exhibit sets AMT's service targets for each system in Exhibit A. It follows the format federal buyers use in imaging PM statements of work, so commercial and federal customers see the same standards.

**C.1 PM intervals per OEM.** AMT schedules PM at the intervals and with the procedures the OEM recommends for each system, with at least [__] visits per year (suggested [2]). CMS memo S&C 14-07-Hospital (December 20, 2013) states that hospitals must maintain imaging and radiologic equipment per the manufacturer's recommendations under 42 CFR 482.26(b)(2), and that this equipment is not eligible for an alternate equipment maintenance (AEM) program. AMT does not shorten or stretch OEM intervals. At the end of each PM, AMT runs the system through at least one full operating cycle to confirm it works.

**C.2 Uptime formula (applies only if Section 7.2 is checked).**

Uptime % = (Base Hours minus Downtime) / Base Hours x 100

- **Measurement period:** each 12 months from the Effective Date, per system.
- **Base Hours:** the covered hours for that system in Exhibit A, A-2, over 12 months. Example: covered hours of 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday to Friday, give 2,340 Base Hours a year (52 weeks of 45 hours). At 2,340 Base Hours, 1% is about 23 hours.
- **Downtime:** hours inside Base Hours when the system cannot produce diagnostic images because of an equipment failure or AMT's service performance. The clock starts when Customer reports the failure to AMT and stops when the system is back in clinical service. Hours outside Base Hours do not count.

Downtime does not include time caused by or spent on:

1. Scheduled PM, and approved software updates or upgrades
2. Cryogen fills and CT tube replacement
3. Waiting for a part when the delay is beyond AMT's control, such as OEM or supplier backorder, carrier delay, or a part available only from the OEM
4. Waiting for OEM-locked software, license keys, passwords, service tools, or OEM on-site support (Section 9)
5. Waiting for site access, remote access, MRI screening, or escort
6. Customer refusing or putting off service that AMT is ready to perform
7. Customer environment outside OEM specifications, including temperature, humidity, and room conditions
8. Power quality, power failure, or grounding problems
9. HVAC, building water, or chiller failure, unless chiller service is a checked add-on and AMT caused the failure
10. Misuse, abuse, neglect, operator error, or damage from a third party
11. Customer-supplied parts (Section 4.6)
12. Service paused for non-payment (Section 5.4)
13. Force majeure events (Section 13.2)
