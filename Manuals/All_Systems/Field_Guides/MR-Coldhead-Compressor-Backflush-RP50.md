# Back flushing coldhead compressors (RP-50 hand pump)

Source: Phil Belcher, 7 Jan 2009.

Back flushing coldhead compressors can be a messy job which is often avoided as it can involve running garden hoses an extended length from the water source, to the coldhead compressor and then to a drain. The resulting water spills or leaks and the garden hoses running down the hallways often means the job can only be done after hours when the patient exams have been completed. There is a much easier way to back flush the compressor. Long garden hoses are not needed and the cleanup of the resulting water spills or leaks is greatly reduced.

## Items required to back flush the coldhead compressor
1. Service tool-manual pump RP50, Part Number 81 15 136.
2. A short section of hose, that will attach to the coldhead compressor water connection. This could be a 3/4" ID hose with a hose clamp which will slide directly over the compressor water connection. Or a brass 1/2" ID hose barb X 1/2" FIP female adapter with a short section of 1/2" ID hose.

## Procedure for backflushing
1. Turn off the coldhead compressor and close the ball valves on the cooling water hoses at the compressor.
2. Place a towel under the 2 brass elbows where the water hoses attach to the compressor. This is to absorb any liquid that drains out of the compressor.
3. Use a thin jawed 19mm or a 3/4" open end wrench and remove both cooling hoses at the compressor.
4. Attach the RP-50 manual hand pump to the coldhead compressor cooling water outlet connection.
5. Connect a short drain hose to the coldhead compressor cooling water inlet connection. The other end of the drain hose can be placed in a suitable container or in the tank of the RP-50 hand pump.
6. Pump water through the coldhead compressor. If an excessive amount of debris is expelled with the drain hose draining into the RP-50 hand pump tank, change the water frequently. Otherwise pump until no debris are seen being discharged from the compressor.
7. Disconnect the RP-50 hand pump and drain hose.
8. Reconnect the normal cooling water hoses to the coldhead compressor. Make sure the water lines are connected correctly, ball valves fully open and no water leaks are present.
9. Restart the coldhead compressor.
